# E1 quick look: the 2025 Kamchatka earthquake in GRACE-FO

**Date:** 2026-10-24. **Status:** quick look. This is not the full E1 test; what is missing is
listed at the end.

![Kamchatka 2025 in GRACE-FO](../figures/e1_kamchatka.svg)

## Result

The Mw 8.8 earthquake of 29 July 2025 shows up clearly in COST-G monthly gravity, even at
degree 60 with a 300 km Gaussian.

- **Dipole.** Gravity fell by 3.7 µGal over the Sea of Okhotsk (52.5° N, 151.5° E), on the
  back-arc side. It rose by 3.4 µGal on the trench side (50.5° N, 160.5° E), along the arc. That
  is about 7 µGal peak to peak, the classic pattern of a megathrust, as seen after Sumatra, Maule
  and Tohoku.
- **Against the null**, using the largest |step| within 500 km of the epicentre:

  | | µGal |
  |---|---|
  | Kamchatka | 3.42 |
  | 100 random ocean points at 40–65° latitude, median | 1.13 |
  | 95th percentile | 2.26 |
  | maximum | 2.95 |

  Kamchatka is above all 100, giving a rank p of 0.010. That is the smallest p a 100-point null
  can give.
- **After the event.** In the 12 months since, the trench-side lobe has kept rising, by a fitted
  +3.1 µGal per year. The back-arc lobe has hardly moved, at +0.5 µGal per year.

  This could be postseismic: afterslip, or viscoelastic relaxation. The earlier great earthquakes
  showed a two-stage pattern (Heki group). But with only 13 post-event months, the step and the
  ramp trade off against each other, and the ramp has not been tested against a null.

## Method

The code is `gravviz e1` ([`apps/gravviz/src/e1.rs`](../apps/gravviz/src/e1.rs)), and this is
its only run. The statistic and the null were fixed in the code before the Kamchatka number was
computed, and nothing was tuned afterwards.

```bash
cargo run --release -p gravviz -- e1 data/costg data/baseline data/TN-14_C30_C20_GSFC_SLR.txt data/TN-13_GEOC_CSR_RL0603.txt figures
```

1. Take all 96 GRACE-FO months, June 2018 to July 2026, 13 of them after the event. Apply the
   C₂₀, C₃₀ and degree-1 replacements and subtract the 2004–2009 COST-G mean.
2. Convert to gravity anomaly in µGal on a 1° grid: degree ≤ 60, 300 km Gaussian, no destriping.
   Destriping attenuates north–south structure, which an arc-parallel dipole has.
3. In every cell, fit one least-squares model with these terms:
   - intercept and trend;
   - annual, semi-annual and 161-day terms;
   - a step at 2025-07-29 23:25 UTC. July 2025 is weighted by the 6.5% of its span after the
     event.
   - a post-event ramp.
4. The statistic is the largest |step| within 500 km of 52.5° N, 160.3° E (the USGS epicentre,
   rounded).
5. The null is the same statistic around 100 random ocean points. They are drawn uniformly on the
   sphere at 40–65° N or S, to match the latitude-dependent noise of GRACE, and more than 2,500 km
   from the epicentre. The seed is 20250729.

## Missing for the full E1 test

- **A dislocation forward model** from the USGS finite-fault model, passed through the same filter.
  It would give the expected amplitude, test the aseismic excess the EGU26-15981 abstract suggests,
  and replace the 500 km maximum with a matched filter (M1).
- **The Q0 noise weights.** The window falls at solar maximum.
- **A null for the ramp.** Then a choice between exponential and logarithmic recovery, once more
  post-event months exist.
- **A comparison with the individual centres' solutions** listed in the COST-G headers.
