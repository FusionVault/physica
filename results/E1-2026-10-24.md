# E1: the 2025 Kamchatka earthquake in GRACE-FO

**Date:** 2026-10-24. **Status:** full test against the USGS fault model. The quick look from
earlier the same day is summarised at the end.

![The 2025 Kamchatka earthquake in GRACE-FO against the USGS fault model](../figures/e1_kamchatka.svg)

## Results

The Mw 8.8 earthquake of 29 July 2025 left a clear step in COST-G monthly gravity. Its size and
shape match the gravity change the USGS fault model predicts.

| Test | Result | Against 100 random ocean points |
|---|---|---|
| **S1, detection:** largest step within 500 km | 3.47 µGal | 95th percentile 2.26, maximum 3.39. **Rank p = 0.01** |
| **S2, after-slip:** largest post-event ramp within 500 km | 3.91 µGal per year | 95th percentile 4.16. Rank p = 0.08, **not significant** |
| **S3, model match:** amplitude α of the USGS model in the observed step map | **α = 0.91 ± 0.16** | Rank p = 0.01 |
| **S3:** correlation of the two maps within 1,500 km | r = 0.59 | Rank p = 0.01 |

**What this means:**
- **No aseismic excess at this scale.** The observed signal has 0.91 ± 0.16 times the
  amplitude of the coseismic slip model, consistent with 1. An EGU 2026 abstract
  ([EGU26-15981](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-15981.html)) suggested slow
  slip beyond the earthquake itself; across 1,500 km, the slip in the USGS model explains the
  signal.
- **One lobe differs.** The back-arc low over the Sea of Okhotsk matches: −3.5 µGal observed,
  −4.2 modelled. The trench-side high is larger than modelled: +3.5 against +1.4 µGal. Three
  explanations fit:
  - after-slip near the trench in the first months, which the step absorbs;
  - the half-space model's simplifications (no layering, no sphericity);
  - the ocean correction.
- **After-slip is not detected yet.** The trench-side lobe kept rising in the quick look. With
  months weighted by their Q0 noise, the largest ramp sits below the random points' 95th
  percentile. It needs more post-event months.

## The forward model

The model uses the [USGS finite-fault model](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/us6000qw60/finite-fault)
(`coulomb.inp`: 465 subfaults, M0 2.25 × 10²² N·m) and is computed with
`gravimetry::coseismic`:

1. **Gravity change** of each subfault in an elastic half-space:
   [Okubo (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1029/92JB00178), ported from Beauducel's `okubo92.m`.
   The rock density is 2,700 kg/m³ and Poisson's ratio is 0.25.
2. **What a satellite sees:** the gravity of the moved mass, without the free-air effect a
   gravimeter on the moving ground also records.
3. **Ocean correction:** where the seafloor rises, the water above it moves away, so 1,025 kg/m³
   × uplift is removed.
4. **Equivalent surface masses** on a 0.1° grid from 44° to 61° N and 150° to 172° E. They are
   expanded to degree 60, degrees 0 and 1 are removed, and the 300 km Gaussian of the data is
   applied.

**Checks:**
- The port matches Okada's (1985) published check values.
- A buried shear fault's equivalent mass sums to zero.
- On this fault model, our uplift matches the USGS's own surface displacement
  (`surface_deformation.disp`, 1,600 points) with **slope 0.84 and r = 0.89**. The USGS uses a
  layered crust; ours is a uniform half-space.

**Limits:** a flat, uniform, non-gravitating Earth. For great earthquakes at GRACE resolution,
this is usually within 10–20% of a layered spherical Earth.

## Method

```bash
cargo run --release -p gravviz -- e1 data/costg data/baseline data/TN-14_C30_C20_GSFC_SLR.txt data/TN-13_GEOC_CSR_RL0603.txt data/usgs_us6000qw60 figures/q0_noise.csv figures
```

**Observations:**
- The 96 GRACE-FO months, 13 of them after the event.
- Gravity anomaly against the 2004–2009 mean: degree ≤ 60, 300 km Gaussian, no destriping.
- **Each 1° cell** gets one weighted least-squares fit:
  - an intercept and a trend;
  - annual, semi-annual and 161-day terms;
  - a step at 2025-07-29 23:25 UTC, with July weighted by the 6.5% of its span after the event;
  - a post-event ramp.
- **Weights:** Q0's noise weight for each month, (median noise / month's noise)², between 0.38
  and 1.84.

**Null:** 100 random ocean points drawn uniformly at 40–65° N or S, more than 2,500 km from the
epicentre (seed 20250729). For S3, the model template keeps its offsets in latitude and its
kilometre scale in longitude when it is shifted to each point.

The statistics and the null were fixed in the code before this version ran.

## The quick look (earlier on 2026-10-24)

With equal weights and no model, the largest step was 3.42 µGal, giving the same rank p of 0.01.
The trench-side lobe's ramp was +3.1 µGal per year, but it was untested.
