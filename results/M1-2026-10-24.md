# M1 and E2: a blind search of 24 years of gravity for sudden mass changes

**Date:** 2026-10-24. **Status:** complete.

![A blind search of 24 years of gravity for sudden mass changes](../figures/m1_detector.svg)

## Result

The scan looked at every month from December 2002 to February 2026 and every place on Earth. It
was not told about any earthquake.

- **It found 2 events, and both are real earthquakes:** Sumatra–Andaman 2004 (Mw 9.1, |Z'| 13.7)
  and Tohoku 2011 (Mw 9.1, |Z'| 7.8). Each was found in the right month and at the right place.
- **No false alarms** in 254 months. The threshold allows a 1% chance of one per month.
- **No flood, drought or ice event made a sudden step this large.** The record's big water
  changes build up over seasons and years, not from one month to the next.

**Against the catalogue** (USGS, Mw ≥ 8.0; the largest |Z'| within 600 km and two months of each
event; threshold 7.71):

| Earthquake | Mw | Largest \|Z'\| | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sumatra–Andaman 2004 | 9.1 | 13.7 | found |
| Nias 2005 | 8.6 | 8.6 | above the threshold, merged with Sumatra |
| Tohoku 2011 | 9.1 | 7.8 | found |
| Wharton Basin 2012 (two events) | 8.6, 8.2 | 6.4–6.7 | below: strike-slip, little vertical mass movement |
| Kamchatka 2025 | 8.8 | 4.7 | below |
| Maule 2010 | 8.8 | 3.9 | below |
| All other Mw 8.0–8.4, and the deep ones | | 1.8–3.7 | below |

**The E2 hypothesis is rejected.** It said the scan would find every shallow Mw ≥ 8.5 earthquake
blind; it found 3 of 6. At a false-alarm rate of 1% per month, blind detection takes about Mw 9,
or Mw 8.6 right next to Sumatra.

Kamchatka is not a contradiction. The E1 test found it (p = 0.01) because it looked at one known
place and time. A blind scan pays for searching about 16,000 places in 254 months.

## Method

The code is `gravviz m1` ([`apps/gravviz/src/m1.rs`](../apps/gravviz/src/m1.rs)).

```bash
cargo run --release -p gravviz -- m1 data/costg data/baseline data/TN-14_C30_C20_GSFC_SLR.txt data/TN-13_GEOC_CSR_RL0603.txt figures/kalman_degrees.csv data/usgs_m8_2002_2026.csv figures viewer/data
```

1. **Data.** Take every coefficient of degree 2–60, minus a global seasonal and trend fit. These
   are the residuals.
2. **Step estimates.** For each candidate month τ, take the months within a year of it and fit an
   intercept, a local trend, annual and semi-annual terms, and a step at the start of τ.
3. **Noise under "no step".** This comes from the M3 Kalman model of each degree: the natural
   signal, an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, pushed exactly through the step estimator,
   Σᵢⱼ kᵢkⱼ sd² e^(−|tᵢ−tⱼ|/τ). Add the files' errors scaled by Q2's κ.
4. **Generalised likelihood-ratio test.** The template is a uniform cap of mass, 400 or 800 km in
   radius, of either sign, loading an elastic Earth. Z = Σ g·s/v / √(Σ g²/v). Its numerator at
   every 2° centre is a single spherical-harmonic synthesis.
5. **Standardisation.** Each cell's Z is divided by its robust spread across all months. The
   median spread is 0.76, so the M3 noise model is slightly conservative.
6. **Null.** Shift the residual months circularly by at least two years (8 shifts × 40 months),
   which keeps the noise pattern and the temporal correlation. The shifted null still contains the
   real earthquakes at other times: its raw 99th percentile is 11.1, against a median of 4.7. So
   the threshold is the 99th percentile of a Gumbel law fitted to the null's quartiles: 7.71.
7. **Merging.** Detections within a year and 1,000 km of each other are one event, because a ±1
   year window cannot time a step better than that.

**How the method developed.** There was no pre-registration, and the earlier versions are kept
here:
- **First version:** only the files' errors as noise, with a time-shuffled null. It found
  nothing. Low degrees, where hydrology dominates, got far too much weight, and shuffling turned
  that hydrology into white noise.
- **Second version:** the M3 noise model and a circular-shift null with a raw 99th-percentile
  threshold. It found only Sumatra, and the real earthquakes inflated the null.
- **This version:** the Gumbel threshold and the merging. The catalogue was used to score each
  version, but never in the scan.

**Checks:** the cap template matches numerical integration of the cap to 1e-9.

## What would make it sharper

- **Earthquake templates.** A dipole template oriented along the trench would fit an earthquake
  better than a cap.
- **The covariance of the stripes.** Method M6 would sharpen the noise model.
- **Shorter windows** for events late in the record, such as Kamchatka, which has only 12 months
  after it.
