# W1 pre-registration: land water storage after the 2026 El Niño

**Registered:** 2026-10-24, as the git tag `prereg/W1-2026-10-24`.
**Targets:** the COST-G fields for December 2026, March 2027 and June 2027, and the 12 months from
July 2027 to June 2028. None of them existed when this was registered; the last field was July 2026.

## Question

Global land water storage drops during El Niño and recovers during La Niña. Is the response
asymmetric, so that land loses more water per degree of El Niño than it gains per degree of
La Niña? And does storage "ratchet" down, failing to recover after a strong El Niño?

The 2026 El Niño gives a live test. Niño 3.4 reached +2.09 °C in September 2026. That is
already above the 2023 event at the same point and close to 2015.

## Method

The code is `gravviz w1` at this tag ([`apps/gravviz/src/w1.rs`](../apps/gravviz/src/w1.rs) and
[`lab.rs`](../apps/gravviz/src/lab.rs)). To rerun it:

```bash
cargo run --release -p gravviz -- w1 data/costg data/baseline data/TN-14_C30_C20_GSFC_SLR.txt data/TN-13_GEOC_CSR_RL0603.txt data/indices/nina34.anom.data figures
```

1. **S, land storage in km³.** Each month goes through the GRACE Tellus chain:
   - swap in C₂₀ and C₃₀ from TN-14 and add degree 1 from TN-13;
   - subtract the 2004–2009 mean;
   - truncate to degree 60, remove the stripes and smooth with a 300 km Gaussian;
   - convert to water height on a 2° grid.

   Then sum over land, using Natural Earth 1:110m land with fractional cells. Antarctica (south of
   60° S) is excluded, and so is a Greenland box (north of 58° N, 80° W to 10° W). That leaves
   132.0 million km².
2. **S′, storage without seasons.** Fit annual, semi-annual and 161-day terms jointly with a linear
   trend, then subtract the seasonal terms. S′ keeps the trend.
3. **Rate model, as first announced.** Take the central-difference rate of S′ in km³ per month,
   using only months whose neighbours are within 45 days. Regress it on N⁺ = max(Niño 3.4, 0) and
   N⁻ = min(Niño 3.4, 0) at lags 0 to 9 months, plus an intercept. The asymmetry statistic is
   Σβ⁺ − Σβ⁻.
4. **Level model, primary:**

   S′ = a + b·(t − 2015) + γ⁺·N̄⁺ + γ⁻·N̄⁻ + e

   N̄ is the mean Niño 3.4 over lags 2 to 6 months, and e is AR(1). The asymmetry statistic is
   γ⁺ − γ⁻.
5. **Uncertainty.** A moving-block bootstrap with 12-month blocks: 2,000 refits on fitted values
   plus resampled residual blocks, with seed 20261024. p is the two-sided bootstrap probability
   of the opposite sign.
6. **Forecast.** Run 4,000 draws month by month from July 2026. Each draw takes:
   - one set of bootstrap coefficients;
   - a Niño 3.4 path after September 2026 from one analogue year (1997, 2015 or 2023, every year
     whose September anomaly was above +1.4 °C), offset to start at +2.09 °C;
   - noise: AR(1) innovations for the level model, residual blocks for the rate model.

### Why there are two models (deviation from the announced plan)

The plan announced before any numbers were computed was the rate model alone. Running it, in
sample only, showed two problems:

- **The lag coefficients are unstable.** They swing between +456 and −470 from one lag to the next,
  because Niño 3.4 changes little from month to month, so neighbouring lags are nearly collinear.
- **Differencing amplifies noise.** S′ changes by about 500 km³ rms from one month to the next.

I then computed one diagnostic: the correlation of detrended S′ with Niño 3.4 at lags 0 to 12
months. It peaks at lag 4, r = −0.50, and it is above |0.4| at lags 2 to 6. The level model was
built from that diagnostic.

So the level model's form was chosen after seeing in-sample data, and **its in-sample p-values are
optimistic.** The forecasts are the real test. Both models are registered and both will be scored.
Nothing about the target months was known, because they had not been observed.

## In-sample results (2002–2026)

| | Level model (primary) | Rate model (as announced) |
|---|---|---|
| El Niño response | γ⁺ = −622 km³ per °C, 90% CI [−861, −376], p < 0.001 | Σβ⁺ = +77 km³/month per °C, [−44, 206], p = 0.32 |
| La Niña response | γ⁻ = −189 km³ per °C, [−511, 122], p = 0.32 | Σβ⁻ = −4, [−141, 127], p = 0.95 |
| Asymmetry | γ⁺ − γ⁻ = −432, [−908, 51], **p = 0.14** | Σβ⁺ − Σβ⁻ = +81, [−159, 323], p = 0.58 |
| Fit on GRACE, correlation with GRACE-FO | r = 0.38 (96 months) | r = 0.32 (92 rate samples) |

**Reading.**
- Land loses about 620 km³ of water for each degree of El Niño, and the evidence for that is strong.
- The response to La Niña is a third of that, and its interval includes zero.
- The asymmetry points the way the ratchet idea predicts, but it is **not significant** (p = 0.14).
- The rate model finds nothing.

On sea level, 1,000 km³ is 2.77 mm, so 620 km³ per °C is about 1.7 mm of global mean sea level per
°C of Niño 3.4.

Other numbers from the run:
- trend in S: +25.6 km³/yr (glacial isostatic adjustment not removed);
- annual amplitude: 2,933 km³;
- level-model residual sd: 556 km³, AR(1) φ = 0.608.

## How it will be scored

1. **Observed S′.** Compute S for each target COST-G RL02.1 field with the code at this tag. Use
   the same 2004–2009 baseline and the same land mask, and the TN-14 and TN-13 files current at
   scoring time, because the frozen versions stop before the targets. Subtract the frozen seasonal
   terms at the field's mid-epoch.
2. **Missing months.** If a target month has no COST-G field, that target is not scored. No
   substitute month is used.
3. **For each target and model**, record:
   - whether the observed S′ falls inside the 90% range;
   - the absolute error of the median.
4. **Directional claims of the level model:**
   - S′ in December 2026 is below 755 km³, the July 2026 value. The model gives this more than 95%
     probability.
   - S′ in March 2027 is below 0.
5. **Ratchet test.** The ratchet is supported if the mean level-model residual over July 2027 to
   June 2028 is below **−367 km³**. The residual is observed S′ minus the frozen level model, driven
   by the observed Niño 3.4. The threshold is the 5th percentile of the 175 fully covered 12-month
   residual means in 2002–2026.

   A residual mean of −367 km³ or above means no ratchet, and that result will be reported too.

**Outcomes that count against the hypotheses:**
- the level model's December 2026 or March 2027 value falls outside its 90% range;
- S′ in December 2026 is at or above 755 km³;
- the ratchet residual is at or above −367 km³.

Every outcome, hits and misses alike, will be added to this file below a dated line, and the
registered text above will not be edited.
