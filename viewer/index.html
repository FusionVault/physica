The Kamchatka earthquake, seen in gravity

The magnitude 8.8 earthquake of 29 July 2025 moved enough rock to change gravity over 1,000 km. GRACE-FO saw it as a dipole: lighter over the Sea of Okhotsk, heavier along the trench. The USGS model of the fault's slip predicts the same pattern.

A blind search of the whole record (M1)

Every month, a likelihood-ratio test looks for a sudden gain or loss of mass, 400 or 800 km across, anywhere on Earth, using every coefficient weighted by its calibrated error. The earthquake catalogue is not used in the search; it is shown only to score it.

Every magnitude 8+ earthquake, scored

Gravity anomaly to degree 60 with a 300 km Gaussian and no destriping, because destriping removes north–south structure like this arc. Each cell's trend and seasons are fitted with a step at the event time and a ramp afterwards, weighting each month by its noise. The model is the USGS finite-fault solution in an elastic half-space (Okubo 1992), with the water pushed aside by the rising seafloor removed, filtered exactly like the data. Notes: E1 results.

What makes a month noisy?

Each monthly gravity field carries noise. Its size depends on the sun, on the satellites' hardware and on how the field was put together. This matters for every claim above, because solar maximum, a degraded accelerometer and a new pointing mode all overlap the 2023–2026 events.

Are the stated errors right? (Q2)

The Kalman smoother fitted to all 24 years: above degree 10 the fields scatter 2 to 2.5 times more than their files state. Scaled up, withheld months are predicted with calibrated errors (rms 1.07), which is what the uncertainty layer in the Water view uses. Notes.
