T1 feasibility: the Hunga Tonga Lamb wave in GRACE-FO laser ranging
Pulse: Gaussian across the front, 1 hPa = 10.2 kg/m², moving at 318.2 m/s.
Pair: 490 km altitude, 220 km apart, 7.08 km/s over the ground; noise SuperSTAR + GRACE-FO LRI (Zingerle et al. 2024).
The front reaches Tonga's antipode after 17.5 h and is back at Tonga after 34.9 h.

Peak range acceleration (nm/s²) and matched-filter SNR of one crossing, per hPa of pulse:
       pulse σ (min) →            1.25             2.5               5              10
       crossing at 90°      0.07 / 3.0      0.13 / 5.9     0.24 / 11.2     0.39 / 19.2
       crossing at 45°      0.03 / 1.6      0.07 / 3.2      0.12 / 6.1     0.20 / 10.3
       crossing at 20°      0.01 / 0.4      0.02 / 0.8      0.03 / 1.6      0.05 / 2.7
       crossing at 10°      0.00 / 0.1      0.00 / 0.2      0.01 / 0.4      0.01 / 0.7

90% of SNR² lies in (mHz, perpendicular crossing):
  σ =  1.25 min: 1.2–7.6
  σ =   2.5 min: 1.2–7.6

Reading it:
- Far from Tonga barometers recorded 1.3–2 hPa: multiply the SNRs by that.
- Over 4 km of ocean the sea rises under the pulse (it is faster than ocean long waves,
  Proudman 1929), adding its mass: ×1.63 (×1.94 over 5 km).
- SNR 5 per crossing needs the real noise to be no worse than SNR/5 times nominal.
  GRACE-D's accelerometer data are transplanted from GRACE-C, and in 1–8 mHz (degrees ~7–43)
  weather and ocean signals are removed only by AOD1B, whose residuals there are unknown. The
  LRI itself resolves ~0.1 nm/s² (Ghobadi-Far et al. 2020); the margin is what must survive.
- Many crossings: each orbit cuts the expanding front about twice, ~30 a day.
