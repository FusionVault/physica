# Atmospheric gravity waves, infrasound and Lamb waves seen by geodetic satellites (GOCE, GRACE/GRACE-FO, CHAMP, Swarm, GNSS TEC)

Scope note: "gravity waves" here means buoyancy waves in a stratified fluid (atmosphere or ocean), not gravitational waves. Research done 2026-10-24. Several publisher pages (AGU/Wiley, SWSC, Springer) returned 403 errors or login redirects, and the TU Delft thermosphere portal refused the connection. Where a detail rests only on an abstract or a search summary, it is marked as such.

## 1. GOCE's detection of Tohoku 2011 infrasound and acoustic-gravity waves (Garcia et al. 2013), and later GOCE work

### Takeaway
GOCE, flying at about 270 km, was the first satellite to record earthquake-generated infrasound directly. Its accelerometers (drag and cross-track) saw air-density changes of about 11% and vertical accelerations of about 1e-7 m/s^2 as it crossed the Tohoku wavefront, and these matched a forward model. The GOCE accelerometer record (Nov 2009 to Oct 2013, 10 s sampling) then became the main dataset for thermospheric gravity-wave climatologies (Garcia 2016, Forbes 2016, Vadas 2019).

### Cited Findings
- Citation: R. F. Garcia et al., Geophys. Res. Lett. 2013, DOI 10.1002/grl.50205. ESA's press title for the work is "GOCE: the first seismometer in orbit". Co-author Eelco Doornbos (TU Delft) is confirmed by SciDev coverage. — [Physics Today, "A seismometer in orbit"](https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/a-seismometer-in-orbit); [ESA, "GOCE the first seismometer in orbit"](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/GOCE/GOCE_the_first_seismometer_in_orbit); [SciDev.net](https://www.scidev.net/global/news/satellite-based-earthquake-detection-mooted-1/)
- What was measured: as GOCE crossed the infrasound wavefront on 11 March 2011, its accelerometers registered air-density variations of about 11% and vertical acceleration variations of about 1e-7 m/s^2. The team modelled the waves numerically, and the modelled amplitude, timing and waveform compared well with GOCE's record. — [Physics Today](https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/a-seismometer-in-orbit); [ESA](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/GOCE/GOCE_the_first_seismometer_in_orbit)
- Geometry and timing: GOCE flew at about 270 km. The first infrasound crossing came about 27 to 30 min after the earthquake, over the Pacific, and a later crossing came over Europe about an hour after it. CHAMP (about 320 km) and GRACE (about 480 km) did not register these signals, and the authors suggested about 300 km as a good altitude for future detection. They judged real-time land-earthquake detection by satellite unlikely because of the lag. — [Physics Today](https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/a-seismometer-in-orbit); [SciDev.net](https://www.scidev.net/global/news/satellite-based-earthquake-detection-mooted-1/)
- Follow-up: an EGU 2014 abstract by Garcia, Doornbos, Bruinsma and Hébert extends the Tohoku analysis to tsunami-generated gravity waves in GOCE data, with joint seismic, infrasonic and tsunami-GW modelling. — [EGU2014-11737](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2014/EGU2014-11737.pdf)
- Garcia et al. (2016), "Medium-scale gravity wave activity in the thermosphere inferred from GOCE data", J. Geophys. Res. Space Physics (journal per HAL record). Method: spectral analysis of GOCE density and crosswind over the whole mission, averaged over two-month periods near the solstices. They introduce a GW marker, "C3f", which shows solar damping of GW activity. Dominant horizontal wavelengths grow with solar flux and span 200 to 500 km. The authors warn that correlated error sources in density and crosswind must be considered. Results agree with earlier CHAMP analyses and with GCMs. — [HAL hal-01503757](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01503757)
- Forbes et al. (2016), "Gravity wave-induced variability of the middle thermosphere", J. Geophys. Res. Space Physics. Data: GOCE accelerometer densities and cross-track winds at 230 to 280 km, Nov 2009 to Oct 2013. They resolve horizontal structures of 128 to 640 km. Zonal-mean RMS variability shows seasonal and latitudinal dependence and dawn-dusk differences. Longitude structure tied to source regions is weak, except at high latitudes and near the Andes and Antarctic Peninsula in local winter. — [TU Delft research portal](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/gravity-wave-induced-variability-of-the-middle-thermosphere)
- Vadas et al. (2019), "Characteristics of the Quiet-Time Hot Spot Gravity Waves Observed by GOCE Over the Southern Andes on 5 July 2010", J. Geophys. Res. Space Physics. They find 17 GWs with horizontal wavelengths of 170 to 1,850 km and density perturbations of 0.03 to 7%. Because of cancellation, the time-averaged zonal-mean Eliassen-Palm flux divergence from the resolved GWs is negligible next to that of the tides. — [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10375080-characteristics-quiettime-hot-spot-gravity-waves-observed-goce-over-southern-andes-july); [TIB open-access copy](https://oa.tib.eu/renate/items/11ded753-6f34-479a-acfe-0baa907e7541/full)
- GOCE thermosphere product v2.0 provides winds (and densities) at 10 s steps, derived from accelerometer and ion-thruster data combined with GPS tracking and star cameras. GOCE flew at about 270 km early in the mission and about 250 km in 2012 to 2013, with about 20 km of altitude variation along each orbit. The product is distributed by ESA. — [Molina & Scherliess 2023, Front. Astron. Space Sci. 10:1214591, DOI 10.3389/fspas.2023.1214591](https://frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2023.1214591/full); [ESA GOCE thermosphere data catalogue](https://earth.esa.int/eogateway/catalog/goce-thermosphere-data)
- An EGU 2016 abstract (EGU2016-8163) also covers GOCE gravity-wave work. It turned up in the search but was not opened. — [EGU2016-8163](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2016/EGU2016-8163.pdf?pdf)

### Inferences
- Sampling sets the floor on resolvable wavelength. At an orbital speed of about 7.7 km/s, 10 s sampling is about 77 km along-track, so the Nyquist horizontal wavelength is about 155 km. This fits the smallest scales reported (128 km by Forbes, 170 km by Vadas). The 7.7 km/s is a standard LEO value, not taken from these sources.
- The Tohoku detection was a single event with a strong forward model. The method was "predict the waveform from a seismic or atmospheric coupling model, then compare with the 10 s accelerometer-derived density and vertical acceleration". It was not a blind detector, and any Rust module would follow the same pattern.
- GOCE's lower altitude (about 255 to 270 km) is why it saw infrasound and short GWs that GRACE and GRACE-FO at about 450 to 500 km mostly do not. Viscous damping grows quickly with altitude, which favours longer and faster waves higher up (consistent with the solar-damping result in Garcia 2016).

- Could not open the Garcia 2013 GRL full text (Wiley 403). The exact processing (filter band, reference model, use of cross-track wind) and the full author list are unverified. Search results confirm only Garcia and Doornbos, and the EGU 2014 abstract adds Bruinsma and Hébert.

- No GOCE infrasound or GW detection published in 2023 to 2026 was found beyond climatology reuse (Molina & Scherliess 2023 only mention GWs as a possible residual).

## 2. Thermospheric density and wind perturbations from CHAMP, GRACE, GRACE-FO, GOCE and Swarm accelerometers: open datasets, sampling and wave-extraction processing

### Takeaway
Accelerometer-derived neutral density and crosswind are openly available for CHAMP, GRACE, GRACE-FO, GOCE and Swarm, from TU Delft (thermosphere.tudelft.nl) and from ESA (GOCE thermosphere product; Swarm DNSxACC at 10 s and DNSxPOD at 30 s). The standard way to extract waves is to remove a background along-track and analyse the residual relative perturbation (dρ/ρ) spectrally or by wave fitting. The 2023 TU Delft reprocessing (Siemes et al. 2023) is the current best CHAMP, GRACE and GRACE-FO set. GRACE-FO density comes from GRACE-C only, because GRACE-D's accelerometer degraded.

### Cited Findings
- Siemes, C., et al. (2023), "New Thermosphere Neutral Mass Density and Crosswind Datasets from CHAMP, GRACE, and GRACE-FO", J. Space Weather Space Clim., DOI 10.1051/swsc/2023014. The work reprocesses each mission over its lifetime, recalibrates all accelerometer data, and adds a new ray-traced radiation-pressure model. The GRACE-FO densities and crosswinds are new. Density standard deviations relative to thermosphere models drop by up to 15% (GRACE, 2009), while mean densities change only marginally. — [SWSC article](https://www.swsc-journal.org/10.1051/swsc/2023014) (search summary; full text returned 403)
- GRACE-FO: GRACE-D's accelerometer degraded shortly after launch, so TU Delft density and crosswind use GRACE-C only (Hładczuk et al. 2023). — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_50f7522b-273c-400d-8ce2-d2b34f735176) (from search summary)
- The TU Delft thermosphere portal serves density and wind data for CHAMP, GRACE, GRACE-FO, GOCE and Swarm. Recent updates: GRACE-FO density and crosswind v02 extended through May to July 2025, and GOCE v02 reprocessing covering all science operations, Nov 2009 to Oct 2013. Distribution moved from FTP to a web server. — [thermosphere.tudelft.nl](https://thermosphere.tudelft.nl/) (from search snippet; the site refused the connection when fetched)
- ESA also announced an additional GRACE-FO density dataset. — [ESA Earth Online news](https://earth.esa.int/eogateway/news/additional-grace-fo-density-dataset-available)
- Swarm DNSxACC_2 (accelerometer-based density) has a nominal 10 s time step and is derived from calibrated non-gravitational accelerations. — [Swarm Product Data Handbook, DNSxACC_2](https://swarmhandbook.earth.esa.int/catalogue/sw_dnsxacc_2_)
- Swarm DNSxPOD_2 (GPS-based density) has a nominal 30 s time step and is derived from aerodynamic accelerations from precise orbit determination only, with a validation report DNSxVAL_2. — [Swarm Product Data Handbook, DNSxPOD_2](https://swarmhandbook.earth.esa.int/catalogue/sw_dnsxpod_2_); see also [ACCxPOD_2](https://swarmhandbook.earth.esa.int/catalogue/sw_accxpod_2_)
- Accelerometer-derived Swarm densities exist only for Swarm-C and the early mission phase of Swarm-A. GPS-derived densities have lower time resolution but cover all three satellites. — [EGU23-5432](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU23/EGU23-5432.html)
- ESA reprocessed Swarm non-gravitational acceleration and density data, and the reprocessed DNSxPOD has baseline "02". — [ESA news](https://earth.esa.int/eogateway/news/swarm-non-gravitational-acceleration-and-thermosphere-density-data-reprocessed)
- A 2024 study used 30 s Swarm-A/B/C GPS-derived densities to study the May 2024 superstorm. — [arXiv 2411.14071](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.14071)
- Climatology link: Park et al. (2014) showed with CHAMP that upper-thermosphere density-fluctuation climatology closely follows stratospheric GW climatology. This is cited secondhand in a 2023 Frontiers article. — [Front. Astron. Space Sci. 2023, 10.3389/fspas.2023.1214612](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2023.1214612/pdf)
- GOCE processing example (winds): bin by dawn/dusk and 1° latitude, subtract the 27-day median per latitude band, keep geomagnetically quiet windows, and remove tides as longitudinal wave-1 to wave-5 terms with an FFT. Space-weather indices (F10.7, Kp) come from NASA OMNIWeb. — [Molina & Scherliess 2023](https://frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2023.1214591/full)
- GW-specific processing (GOCE): spectral analysis of density and crosswind, the C3f marker, and two-month solstice averages. Correlated density and crosswind errors are a known pitfall. — [Garcia et al. 2016, HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01503757)

### Inferences
- A minimal open pipeline for TIDs and GWs from these products:
  1. Read the time, position, local solar time, density and crosswind series.
  2. Compute the relative perturbation dρ/ρ = (ρ − ρ̄)/ρ̄, where ρ̄ is a running mean or low-pass background over a window longer than the target wavelength. A window of about 1/8 to 1/4 orbit isolates TIDs; one of a few minutes isolates medium-scale GWs.
  3. Optionally normalise to a common altitude with an empirical model such as NRLMSIS. Li et al. 2023 normalised GRACE-FO to 510 km and Swarm-C to 440 km (Section 4).
  4. Analyse spectra or wavelets in along-track distance.
  5. Fit wave packets, or cross-correlate between satellites (GRACE-FO, Swarm A/C) to get phase speed.
  This is a synthesis. The cutoffs are typical choices, not values quoted by a source here.
- Swarm POD-only density at 30 s (about 230 km along-track) can only resolve horizontal scales above about 460 km, so it suits large-scale TIDs and not medium-scale GWs. DNSxACC at 10 s (Swarm-C) is the useful Swarm product for GWs.
- Feasibility check: the user already has COST-G monthly gravity fields, but monthly fields carry no thermospheric wave information. Wave work needs the Level-1B or 2 accelerometer and density time series, not Level-2 spherical-harmonic fields.

## 3. Tsunami- and earthquake-generated gravity waves in the ionosphere (GNSS TEC), GUARDIAN, and the physics formulas

### Takeaway
Tsunamis and large earthquakes drive acoustic waves (fast, minutes) and internal gravity waves (slow, tracking the tsunami). These grow in amplitude as density falls with height, and in the F region they appear as TEC perturbations of the electron density. This has been detected routinely since Sumatra 2004 and Tohoku 2011. NASA JPL's GUARDIAN (Martire et al. 2023, GPS Solutions) runs this in near real time. For the Mw 8.8 Kamchatka event of 29 July 2025 it flagged the tsunami's ionospheric signature about 30 to 40 min before landfall in Hawai'i. The governing physics is the isothermal acoustic-gravity-wave dispersion relation, with acoustic cut-off ω_a = γg/(2c), Brunt–Väisälä N = √(γ−1)·g/c, and the Lamb mode ω = c·k_h.

### Cited Findings
- Tohoku 2011, near field: a preliminary GPS-TEC analysis of Japan's GEONET network found a first intense signal about 10 min after rupture, with two modes, one propagating above 3 km/s and one near 1 km/s. — [Earth Planets Space, DOI 10.5047/EPS.2011.06.020](https://www.doi.org/10.5047/EPS.2011.06.020) (from search summary)
- Occhipinti, Rolland, Lognonné & Watada (2013, JGR Space Physics, DOI 10.1002/jgra.50322) extend the Sumatra 2004 analysis to Tohoku 2011. They show routine detection of early ionospheric anomalies after rupture and argue for ionospheric sounding in tsunami detection. — [PDF at IPGP](https://www.ipgp.fr/~ninto/Ninto-JGR2013.pdf)
- Rolland et al. (2010, GRL; file 2010GL044479, DOI 10.1029/2010GL044479) found ionospheric disturbances in the dense Hawaiian GPS network within hours of tsunami passage at sea level. Makela et al. (2011, GRL) imaged the Tohoku tsunami in airglow over Hawai'i. — [Rolland et al. 2010 PDF](https://www.ipgp.fr/~ninto/2010GL044479.pdf); [Occhipinti 2011 slides](https://www.tectonics.caltech.edu/meetings/brownbag/material/occhipinti_2011.pdf)
- Komjathy et al. (2012, Earth Planets Space): tsunami-driven TIDs match the period, velocity and direction of the ocean tsunami, which separates them from other TID sources. — [EPS 10.5047/eps.2012.08.003](https://earth-planets-space.springeropen.com/counter/pdf/10.5047/eps.2012.08.003.pdf)
- Tang et al. (EGU 2017) separate earthquake-driven GWs, which cluster around the epicentre, from tsunami-driven GWs, which appear over the ocean, in Tohoku TEC. — [EGU2017-2510](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2017/EGU2017-2510.pdf?pdf)
- Coïsson et al. tested whether FORMOSAT-3/COSMIC radio occultation could detect tsunami GWs in March 2011. — [Coïsson, BSS 2016 slides](https://isrconferences.bc.edu/bss2016/Sessions/Session3A-NaturalHazards/Coisson.pdf)
- GUARDIAN citation: Martire, L., Krishnamoorthy, S., Vergados, P., Romans, L. J., Szilágyi, B., Meng, X., Anderson, J. L., Komjáthy, A., Bar-Sever, Y. E. (2023), "The GUARDIAN system: a GNSS upper atmospheric real-time disaster information and alert network", GPS Solutions 27(1), 32, DOI 10.1007/s10291-022-01365-6. It uses more than 70 GNSS stations around the Pacific Ring of Fire and four constellations (GPS, Galileo, BeiDou, GLONASS). NRT TEC time series are validated against post-processing and shown on a public website, with a tsunami inverse-modelling proof of concept. In the 2023 paper, automatic detection was architecture only. — [UCAR record](https://impacts.ucar.edu/en/publications/the-guardian-system-a-gnss-upper-atmospheric-real-time-disaster-i/); [UCAR data record](https://data.ucar.edu/dataset/07042293-2be8-4ef5-a82f-5420a3c34284)
- Kamchatka, 29 July 2025 (Mw 8.8): GUARDIAN stations G027 and QSPP detected early tsunami signatures 8 min after the earthquake. Station KOKB detected the incoming wave at 18:50 HST, 32 min before the earliest tide-gauge detection. GUARDIAN triggers automatically for M ≥ 6.5 events. The first real test came one day after JPL deployed an AI model for tsunami signals and a prototype alert-messaging system. — [NASA SVS 5626](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5626/); [JPL, "See NASA's GUARDIAN Catch a Tsunami"](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/see-nasas-guardian-catch-a-tsunami/); [NASA news](https://www.nasa.gov/earth/natural-disasters/tsunamis/nasas-guardian-tsunami-detection-tech-catches-wave-in-real-time/)
- Peer-reviewed Kamchatka results (2026): "GNSS-based automated detection of the 2025 Kamchatka tsunami 30 minutes before landfall using GUARDIAN", Natural Hazards 122(9), DOI 10.1007/s11069-026-08151-4. It describes an ML extension called "Scout". A second paper, "Near-real-time detection of tsunami-induced traveling ionospheric disturbances using multi-constellation GNSS and a temporal convolutional network", Natural Hazards, DOI 10.1007/s11069-026-08178-7, uses GUARDIAN detrended TEC from Japanese stations. — [IDEAS/RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/nathaz/v122y2026i9d10.1007_s11069-026-08151-4.html); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11069-026-08178-7)
- Lead-time figures conflict by reference point: 30 min before Hawai'i landfall (Natural Hazards), 32 min before the first tide gauge (NASA), and 40 min (Phys.org). One NASA page gives the magnitude as 8.5, others as 8.8. — [Phys.org 2025-10](https://phys.org/news/2025-10-tsunamis-gps-satellites.html); [Phys.org 2026-03](https://phys.org/news/2026-03-nasa-experimental-technology-tsunami.html)
- An alternative early-warning approach using GW effects on the ionosphere is in Remote Sensing 13(11):2150 (2021), DOI 10.3390/rs13112150. — [DOI link](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13112150)
- Dispersion relation (local, no wind, no dissipation): k_z² = (ω² − ω_a²)/c² − k_h²(1 − N²/ω²), where c is the adiabatic sound speed, ω_a the acoustic cut-off and N the Brunt–Väisälä frequency. The relation is quadratic in ω², so it has two branches: acoustic (ω > ω_a) and internal gravity (ω < N), with an evanescent band between them. — [arXiv 1612.04729](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.04729) (solar form, same physics); [Ann. Geophys. 42, 491 (2024)](https://angeo.copernicus.org/articles/42/491/2024/)
- Isothermal cut-offs: ω_a = γg/(2c) and ω_BV = N = √(γ−1)·g/c. — [arXiv 1705.01388 (VLF detection of AGWs, uses T = 250 K)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.01388)
- Lamb waves: horizontally propagating acoustic waves, non-dispersive with ω = c·k_h. The Lamb curve meets the Brunt–Väisälä curve, which allows energy exchange between regimes. — [arXiv 1612.04729](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.04729)
- Infrasound with ω > 0.035 s⁻¹, propagating almost vertically, can reach the lower thermosphere. — [Ann. Geophys. 42, 491 (2024)](https://angeo.copernicus.org/articles/42/491/2024/)

### Inferences
- Expanded polynomial form, from the relation above multiplied by ω²c²: ω⁴ − ω²[c²(k_h² + k_z²) + ω_a²] + c²N²k_h² = 0. This is Hines' (1960) form. The Hines citation, Can. J. Phys. 38, 1441, DOI 10.1139/p60-150, is from background knowledge and was not verified this session.
- Worked numbers, computed here from the formulas with T = 250 K, γ = 1.4, R = 287 J/(kg·K) and g = 9.81 m/s²: c ≈ 317 m/s; ω_a ≈ 0.0217 rad/s (period ≈ 4.8 min); N ≈ 0.0196 rad/s (period ≈ 5.3 min). This is why co-seismic acoustic waves show periods of about 3 to 5 min and tsunami GWs periods of about 10 to 30 min.
- The amplitude of an upward wave grows roughly as exp(z/2H), because energy is conserved as density falls. This is why the ocean's centimetre-scale tsunami height becomes a measurable TEC or density perturbation at 250 to 350 km. It is textbook physics, not taken from a source fetched here.
- In the thermosphere, molecular viscosity and thermal conduction dominate. A Rust model should use a dissipative dispersion relation, for example the Vadas & Fritts (2005) anelastic form (from background knowledge, not verified here), rather than the ideal relation above 150 to 200 km.
- Ray tracing: integrate dx/dt = ∂ω/∂k and dk/dt = −∂ω/∂x with ω(k, x) from the dispersion relation, in a background atmosphere with winds (NRLMSIS plus HWM, or ERA5 at lower levels). This is standard Hamiltonian ray tracing and is easy in Rust. TEC response then needs ion-neutral coupling: the B-field geometry factor (k·B) and a thin-shell IPP at about 300 to 350 km.

### Gaps
- Full GUARDIAN processing (TEC detrending filter, IPP height, latency) is not in the abstract. The Martire 2023 full text was not retrieved.
- GUARDIAN source-code licence and openness are unknown. The data website is public, but no code repository was found.
- No dedicated 2022 to 2026 review with a single consistent AGW dispersion convention was retrieved. Notation differs between sources (ω² vs ω, ω_a vs ω_ac, scale-height forms).

## 4. Hunga Tonga–Hunga Ha'apai, 15 January 2022: global Lamb wave and gravity waves, and whether gravity or accelerometer missions saw them

### Takeaway
Yes, the accelerometers saw it. GRACE-FO (about 510 km) and Swarm-C (about 440 km) accelerometer densities show three successive large-scale thermospheric density waves with phase speeds of 200 to 450 m/s (Li et al. 2023, GRL). Modelling disputes the source. Li et al. (2025, AGU Advances) find secondary gravity waves (MESORAC-HIAMCM) explain GRACE-FO's timing and amplitude, with the Lamb wave much weaker at 510 km. WACCM-X (Liu et al. 2023) gives the Lamb wave about 25% of the disturbance at 510 km. No study was found showing the event in GRACE-FO gravity-field (KBR or LRI) products.

### Cited Findings
- Wright, C. J., et al. (2022), "Surface-to-space atmospheric waves from Hunga Tonga–Hunga Ha'apai eruption", Nature 609, 741–746, DOI 10.1038/s41586-022-05012-5. They report Lamb waves at 318.2 ± 6 m/s at the surface and 308 ± 5 to 319 ± 4 m/s in the stratosphere. Gravity waves ran at 238 ± 3 to 269 ± 3 m/s in the stratosphere. The plume peaked above 50 km, and the response was global from the surface to the ionosphere. — [PMC9492537](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9492537/); [Oxford press release](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2022-06-30-tonga-volcano-eruption-triggered-atmospheric-gravity-waves-reached-edge-space)
- Li et al. (2023), "Large-Scale Disturbances in the Upper Thermosphere Induced by the 2022 Tonga Volcanic Eruption", Geophys. Res. Lett., DOI 10.1029/2022GL102265. GRACE-FO and Swarm-C accelerometer densities show three successive thermospheric density waves at about 500 km with phase speeds of 200 to 450 m/s. Densities were normalised to 510 km (GRACE-FO) and 440 km (Swarm-C). — [AGU/Wiley](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2022GL102265) (from search summary; full text 403)
- Li et al. (2025), "Were Gravity Waves or Lamb Waves Responsible for the Large-Scale Thermospheric Response to the Tonga Eruption?", AGU Advances, DOI 10.1029/2024AV001470. Secondary GWs from the MESORAC-HIAMCM model match the globally propagating GRACE-FO density disturbances in timing and amplitude. Lamb waves have much weaker effects at about 510 km. — [AGU/Wiley](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2024AV001470); [ZME Science coverage](https://www.zmescience.com/science/physics/tonga-eruption-gravity-waves-space/)
- Liu et al. (2023), "Atmospheric and Ionospheric Responses to Hunga-Tonga Volcano Eruption Simulated by WACCM-X", Geophys. Res. Lett., DOI 10.1029/2023GL103682. The Lamb wave can reach the thermosphere as a sharp, narrow packet and may contribute about 25% of the total disturbance at 510 km. — [AGU/Wiley](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2023GL103682) (from search summary)
- Conflict: another study notes the Lamb wave's energy density decays with height, so its thermospheric wind signature is small. — cited in the same search set ([EPS 2023, "Upward propagation of gravity waves and ionospheric perturbations triggered by the 2022 Hunga-Tonga volcanic eruption", DOI 10.1186/s40623-023-01827-2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40623-023-01827-2))
- 2026: "Analysis of Tonga eruption-induced disturbances in the upper thermosphere based on atmospheric drag accelerations from LEO satellites", GPS Solutions, DOI 10.1007/s10291-026-02150-5. Drag accelerations come from the accelerometer for GRACE-FO, but from a model-based (POD) method for Swarm because raw Swarm accelerometer data are unavailable. Swarm-A and -B show similar density enhancements over the same region. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10291-026-02150-5) (from search summary; page redirected to login)
- Ionosphere: a giant ionospheric density hole near the volcano was seen by several satellites. — [EPS 2023, DOI 10.1186/s40623-023-01933-1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40623-023-01933-1)
- Mesosphere and lower thermosphere: meteor radars plus the HIAMCM model track global GW propagation, and an arXiv study "X-rays" MLT neutral-density disturbances. — [ResearchGate (meteor radar/HIAMCM)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380079623_Gravity_waves_generated_by_the_Hunga_Tonga-Hunga_Ha'apai_volcanic_eruption_and_their_global_propagation_in_the_mesospherelower_thermosphere_observed_by_meteor_radars_and_modeled_with_the_High-Altitude); [arXiv 2410.09410](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.09410)

### Inferences
- Tonga is the best open test case for a Rust "TAD/GW detector". It has an exact source time and location, known Lamb (about 318 m/s) and GW (about 240 to 270 m/s) speeds, several satellites (GRACE-FO-C ACC, Swarm-C ACC, Swarm-A/B POD), and published amplitudes and speeds to check against.
- For the gravity-field side: AOD1B RL07 is built from 3-hourly ERA5 (Section 5), so a Lamb-wave pressure pulse crossing the globe in about 1.5 days, with a sharp front lasting minutes to tens of minutes, would be under-sampled and would alias into the GRACE-FO de-aliasing residuals. This would show, if anywhere, in along-track LRI or KBR residuals on 15 to 16 January 2022, not in monthly fields. This is a hypothesis; no source tested it.

### Gaps
- No publication was found that detects the Tonga Lamb wave or pressure pulse in GRACE-FO inter-satellite ranging (KBR or LRI) or in gravity-field residuals. This is untested, not shown absent.
- Exact amplitudes (% dρ/ρ) of the three GRACE-FO waves were not retrieved, since the Li 2023 full text returned 403.
- No result was found on whether the Kamchatka 2025 tsunami or earthquake GWs appear in GRACE-FO or Swarm accelerometer densities.

## 5. Ocean surface gravity waves, tides and tsunamis as signal and noise in satellite gravimetry (AOD1B dealiasing)

### Takeaway
In GRACE and GRACE-FO, fast atmosphere and ocean mass motions (including non-tidal ocean response, atmospheric tides and pressure waves) are noise. They are removed with the AOD1B background model, now RL07 (Shihora, Balidakis, Dill, Dobslaw, GFZ; 3-hourly ERA5 and MPIOM). Imperfect de-aliasing is among the largest GRACE and GRACE-FO error sources. GRACE-FO's laser ranging interferometer is about 10 times more sensitive than KBR at short scales, which makes along-track detection of transient mass signals more plausible. No direct GRACE detection of a propagating tsunami was found.

### Cited Findings
- AOD1B RL07: product description by L. Shihora, K. Balidakis, R. Dill and H. Dobslaw (GFZ), rev. 7.0, Oct 2023, with a reference paper in JGR Solid Earth (2022). Inputs are 3-hourly ERA5 atmosphere and MPIOM ocean bottom pressure forced by ERA5. Changes from RL06: hourly ERA5 atmospheric forcing in the ocean model, improved Antarctic bathymetry including ice-shelf cavities, and explicit self-attraction and loading feedback. Checked against GRACE-FO KBR and LRI and Jason-3, it improves high-frequency mass changes globally. — [GRACE AOD1B Product Description Document RL07 (GFZ ISDC)](https://isdc-data.gfz.de/grace/DOCUMENTS/Level-1/GRACE_AOD1B_Product_Description_Document_for_RL07.pdf); [GFZ AOD1B data](https://isdc.gfz.de/esmdata/aod1b/); [EGU2020-2596](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2020/EGU2020-2596.html)
- Imperfect de-aliasing is considered one of the largest contributors to GRACE and GRACE-FO error. — [GSTM2020-29](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/GSTM2020/GSTM2020-29.html) (from search summary)
- Atmospheric contributions to ocean tides for satellite gravimetry are a related background-model topic. — [AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/67597d8ec7a957febdf8ce1e); [J. Geodesy 2024, DOI 10.1007/s00190-024-01832-7](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00190-024-01832-7)
- GRACE-FO LRI captures gravitational signals down to about 0.1 nm/s² at 490 km, about an order of magnitude better than KBR, for short-wavelength (100 to 200 km) static anomalies. — [Ghobadi-Far et al. 2020, GRL, DOI 10.1029/2020GL089445](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2020GL089445)
- LRI in-orbit performance: continuous phase tracking for more than 50 days, and much lower noise than the microwave (KBR) system above about 100 mHz. — [Abich et al. 2019, Phys. Rev. Lett. 123, 031101](https://link.aps.org/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevLett.123.031101)
- Raw LRI phase needs correction for time-tag errors, phase jumps and ranging-system errors before gravity use. — [Chinese J. Geophys. 2024, DOI 10.6038/cjg2024R0814](http://www.geophy.cn/en/article/doi/10.6038/cjg2024R0814)
- Repo context: this project already has "E1 quick look: the 2025 Kamchatka earthquake in GRACE-FO" (commit 186aac5) and E1 against the USGS fault model (commit d437ee4). These are co-seismic, monthly-field analyses. — local git log, /home/dan/code/FusionVault/quantum-gravimeter

### Inferences
- A tsunami's mass anomaly is centimetres to a metre of water over about 100 to 500 km wavelengths, moving at √(gH) ≈ 200 m/s in 4 km deep ocean. This is shallow-water physics, computed here. It lasts hours at any place, which is far below monthly resolution and comparable to the time GRACE-FO takes to fly over it (about 90 s across 700 km), so it would appear only as a transient along-track LRI residual. Co-seismic permanent mass change is the established GRACE earthquake signal, and it is a different thing.
- Ocean surface gravity waves (wind waves and swell, periods of seconds) have no net mass transport at gravimetry scales and are irrelevant. Ocean tides and internal tides are signal for tide models, and their errors are a dominant aliasing source.
- A useful, novel and small module would difference GRACE-FO LRI range-acceleration residuals (after AOD1B RL07) on 15 to 16 January 2022 against the timing of the Tonga Lamb-wave front. A null result would also be publishable as a bound.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that detects a propagating tsunami in GRACE or GRACE-FO ranging data. Ghobadi-Far / Han group work on along-orbit LRI is the nearest lead, but no tsunami paper was retrieved.
- The DOI of the AOD1B RL07 JGR Solid Earth 2022 paper was not retrieved. It is possibly 10.1029/2022JB024360, unverified.
- No information was found on whether AOD1B RL07 or ERA5 resolves the Tonga pressure pulse.

## 6. What is computable in Rust, and existing open tools

### Takeaway
Everything needed is computable in Rust with no exotic dependencies:
- AGW dispersion and cut-offs (closed form)
- Hamiltonian ray tracing (an ODE integrator)
- along-track detrending and filtering, spectra and wavelets (FFT)
- wave-packet detection (matched filter or STA/LTA on dρ/ρ or dTEC)
- moving-satellite geometry, including inter-satellite phase speed

The hard parts are data I/O (CDF for Swarm, ESA and TU Delft text formats, RINEX for TEC) and background-atmosphere models (NRLMSIS, HWM), which today are Fortran or Python. Existing open tools are mostly Python: VARION, TIDD and OASIS for GNSS TEC.

### Cited Findings
- VARION (Savastano et al. 2017, Sci. Rep., DOI 10.1038/srep46607): open-source, all-Python, real-time detection of tsunami-generated TIDs. It is derived from VADASE and uses single-receiver time-differenced geometry-free carrier-phase combinations. It detected TIDs after the 2012 Haida Gwaii tsunami with 56 Hawaiian GPS receivers. Code: github.com/giorgiosavastano/VARION. — [Sci. Rep.](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/srep46607); [NOAA repository PDF](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/24063/noaa_24063_DS1.pdf)
- TIDD (Constantinou et al.): deep-learning TID detection. It turns VARION slant-TEC into images for CNNs and reports a 91.7% F1 score on out-of-sample Illapel 2015 validation. Code: https://github.com/vc1492a/tidd. — [search summary, NOAA/aitopics records](https://aitopics.org/class/Country/Oceania/Tonga/Ha`apai)
- OASIS: a modular open-source Python toolbox to pre-process GNSS data and compute ROTI, ΔTEC and SIDX. No repository URL was found. — [Atmosphere 13(2):183](https://p.gyb.elementfx.com/2073-4433/13/2/183) (mirror; journal-of-record unverified)
- Recent ML TID detection on GUARDIAN data uses temporal convolutional networks (2026). — [Natural Hazards, DOI 10.1007/s11069-026-08178-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11069-026-08178-7)
- Data inputs and their sampling: Swarm DNSxACC 10 s, DNSxPOD 30 s ([Swarm handbook](https://swarmhandbook.earth.esa.int/catalogue/sw_dnsxacc_2_)); GOCE thermosphere 10 s ([ESA GOCE thermosphere data](https://earth.esa.int/eogateway/catalog/goce-thermosphere-data)); AOD1B RL07 3-hourly ([GFZ](https://isdc.gfz.de/esmdata/aod1b/)); F10.7 and Kp ([OMNIWeb](https://omniweb.gsfc.nasa.gov/form/dx1.html)).
- Existing repo crates: geopotential, gravimetry, gravity-field, satgrav. The workspace is Rust 2024 edition, MIT licensed, with optional serde as the only common dependency. — local files /home/dan/code/FusionVault/quantum-gravimeter/Cargo.toml and crates/

### Inferences
- A minimal Rust module, a new crate such as `agw` or a `satgrav::thermo` module:
  1. `dispersion`: functions for c(T, γ, M), ω_a, N, the Lamb speed, and k_z²(ω, k_h). Tests: the worked 250 K numbers in Section 3 (c ≈ 317 m/s, ω_a ≈ 0.0217, N ≈ 0.0196 rad/s), and Lamb speed ≈ Wright 2022's 318 m/s near the surface.
  2. `along_track`: read TU Delft or Swarm density, compute dρ/ρ with a running-mean background, and map time to along-track distance.
  3. `detect`: matched-filter or STA/LTA detection of packets, plus a "moveout" check that crossing times fit a speed v from a known source (Tonga: v in 200 to 450 m/s; Tohoku infrasound: arrival about 27 to 30 min at GOCE).
  4. `ray`: an RK4 Hamiltonian ray tracer in an isothermal or tabulated atmosphere.
  Dependencies: rustfft (or realfft) for spectra, otherwise std. NRLMSIS can be put off by using tabulated profiles or calling pymsis offline.
- Swarm L1b/L2 files are NASA CDF. A Rust CDF reader is the main I/O risk; check crates.io for maturity before committing (not checked here). Alternatively, fetch via ESA VirES as CSV or NetCDF.
- What it would take: about 1 to 2 weeks for dispersion, filtering and Tonga-replication with tests, plus open data downloads (TU Delft GRACE-FO v02 for January 2022, Swarm-C DNSxACC for January 2022). TEC work (RINEX parsing, cycle-slip handling, satellite and receiver DCBs, IPP mapping) is substantially more and duplicates VARION and GUARDIAN. This is an engineering estimate, not sourced.
- Other Python tools known from background, not verified this session: viresclient (ESA VirES access to Swarm), pymsis (NRLMSIS 2.x wrapper), georinex (RINEX reader), and the TU Delft and ESA text/CDF readers. Verify licences and maintenance before use.

### Gaps
- No Rust crates for NRLMSIS, HWM, CDF or RINEX were checked in this session. Their availability and quality are unknown.
- No open-source GUARDIAN code was found, and no open thermospheric-density GW toolkit (Python or otherwise) was found as a named package. Published GW extraction (Garcia 2016, Forbes 2016, Vadas 2019) appears to use in-house code.
- Licences of VARION and TIDD repositories were not checked.
