# Unifying algorithms across the physica crates

Scope: algorithms and mathematical structures that gravity, magnetism, plasma physics and optics
share, which the `physica` family (metrology, geopotential, gravimetry, gravitational, gravitics,
magnetics, plasmoid, physica) could write once. Every benchmark marked "computed" was run in
`unifying_algorithms_checks.py` (next to this file; `python3 -I`, standard library only; 74 pass/fail
checks, all passing, plus 37 printed values; about 8 s).

Source marks: a plain link was opened or seen in a search result in this session; a link marked
† is a DOI given from my own knowledge, not opened this session (the report writer should treat
the bibliographic details of † items as needing a check, though the formulas behind them were
verified numerically where stated).

Crate facts below come from reading every crate's README "What is inside" table,
`crates/gravitics/src/electrodes.rs`, `crates/physica/src/analogies.rs`, the `Cargo.toml` files
and greps of `crates/*/src`. Dependency graph (from the `Cargo.toml` files): `metrology` is a leaf
with no dependencies; `geopotential` → metrology; `gravimetry`, `magnetics` → geopotential +
metrology; `gravitational` → metrology; `gravitics` → geopotential + gravitational + metrology;
`plasmoid` → metrology; `physica` → all. So anything `plasmoid` or `gravitational` must share has
to sit in `metrology` or in a new leaf crate, while anything for gravity + magnetics only can sit
in `geopotential` (which already hosts the shared `Field` trait).

## Potential-field forward models shared by gravity and magnetics (prism, polyhedron, Poisson's relation)

### Takeaway
One closed-form "unit-density Newtonian kernel" per body shape (prism or polyhedron), returning
the potential, its gradient and its Hessian, serves gravity directly and magnetics through
Poisson's relation (B = (μ₀/4π) Hessian·M), so a single `geopotential` module would give
`gravimetry::local` its missing prism/polyhedron and `magnetics` its first crustal-anomaly model.
Both formulas were implemented and pass exact tests here (Laplace/Poisson trace, Bouguer slab,
polyhedron = prism to 10⁻¹⁵, cube demagnetising factor 1/3).

### Cited Findings
- **Already covered:** `gravimetry::local::sphere` and `gravimetry::local::cylinder` (gravity and Γ_uu of a buried sphere and horizontal cylinder); `geopotential` has `partials`/`second_partials` (the far-field multipole engine) and the `Field` trait already implemented for gravity (`Coefficients`), the main field (`magnetics::main_field::MainField`) and `gravitics::gem::GravitomagneticDipole`; `physica::analogies` asserts that gravity and the geomagnetic field share one spherical-harmonic engine. No prism, polyhedron, tesseroid or magnetised-body forward model exists in any crate (greps of `crates/*/src`). Note: `local` appears in gravimetry's "Applications" list but not in its "What is inside" table.
- **Right rectangular prism (foundational).** Nagy, Papp & Benedek (2000), J. Geodesy 74:552–560, give closed forms for the prism's potential and its derivatives to third order — [Springer, DOI 10.1007/s001900000116](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s001900000116); the pygeoid library implements them — [pygeoid docs](https://pygeoid.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/simple/prism.html); a 2010 comparison with a discrete element method found the formulas valid inside and outside the prism — [Han & Shen 2010, Geo-spatial Inf. Sci.](https://gsis.xml-journal.net/article/doi/10.1007/s11806-010-0129-9). Earlier forms: Plouff 1976 Geophysics 41:727 [†DOI 10.1190/1.1440645](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.1440645); Okabe 1979 Geophysics 44:730 (polyhedra, gravity and magnetics) [†DOI 10.1190/1.1440973](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.1440973).
  - Formula (offsets X = ξ − x_P, etc., R = √(X²+Y²+Z²), corner sum |||·||| = Σᵢⱼₖ (−1)^{i+j+k} with + at the upper corner): V = Gρ|||XY ln(Z+R) + YZ ln(X+R) + ZX ln(Y+R) − (X²/2)atan(YZ/XR) − (Y²/2)atan(ZX/YR) − (Z²/2)atan(XY/ZR)|||; g_x = ∂V/∂x_P = −Gρ|||Y ln(Z+R) + Z ln(Y+R) − X atan(YZ/XR)||| (cyclic for y, z); T_xx = −Gρ|||atan(YZ/XR)|||, T_xy = Gρ|||ln(Z+R)||| (cyclic). Implemented in `prism_terms` of the checks script — [Nagy et al. 2000](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s001900000116).
  - Computed benchmarks: (i) a slab 4000 km wide and 100 m thick at 2670 kg/m³ gives 0.11197 mGal/m, against the textbook Bouguer gradient 0.1119 mGal/m = 2πGρ (agreement with 2πGρh to 2×10⁻⁵, the residual being the finite width); (ii) tensor trace outside = 1.7×10⁻¹⁶ (Laplace), inside = −4πGρ to 10⁻¹⁶ (Poisson); (iii) potential at the centre of a unit cube, ∫1/r dV = 2.380077364 (Gρa² units), equal to brute-force 3-D quadrature to 3×10⁻⁹; (iv) far field of a 1 m cube against a point mass: 2.6×10⁻⁶ relative at 10 m (the cube's hexadecapole), 1.8×10⁻⁹ at 100 m but **3.4×10⁻⁷ at 1000 m** — the corner sum cancels catastrophically far from the body — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).
  - The far-field cancellation is the known weakness of the standard formula; Fukushima's 2018 recursive prism method (GJI 215:864–879, polynomial density) is reported as accurate near and on the surface — [Fukushima 2019 ASJ abstract](https://stg.asj.or.jp/nenkai/archive/2019a/pdf/R22a.pdf). I did not find the 2020 GJI "speed and accuracy improvements" paper named in the brief.
- **Polyhedron (foundational, with 2021–2024 implementations).** Werner & Scheeres (1997) give U = (Gρ/2)Σ_edges r_e·E_e·r_e L_e − (Gρ/2)Σ_faces r_f·F_f·r_f ω_f and g = −GρΣ E_e r_e L_e + GρΣ F_f r_f ω_f, with E_e = n̂_A n̂_e^A + n̂_B n̂_e^B, F_f = n̂_f n̂_f, L_e = ln[(r_i + r_j + e)/(r_i + r_j − e)], ω_f the face's signed solid angle, and ∇²U = −GρΣω_f (so Σω_f = 4π inside, 0 outside is a free inside/outside test) — [†Werner & Scheeres 1997, Celest. Mech. Dyn. Astron. 65:313, DOI 10.1007/BF00053511](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00053511). Tsoulis (2012, Geophysics) gives the full tensor by line integrals; the ESA/TU Munich "polyhedral-gravity-model" library (C++ with Python bindings, JOSS 9(98), June 2024, DOI 10.21105/joss.06384) implements Tsoulis's method and reports 253 µs per point for the Eros mesh on an M1 Pro — [Schuhmacher et al. 2024, JOSS](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.06384); Tsoulis & Gavriilidou (2021) review the line-integral formulation — [Geophys. Prospect. 10.1111/1365-2478.13134](https://www.earthdoc.org/content/journals/10.1111/1365-2478.13134).
  - Computed: the Werner–Scheeres sum on a 12-triangle cube equals the Nagy prism to 7×10⁻¹⁶ (potential) and 1×10⁻¹⁵ (g_z) at an outside point; Σω_f = 1×10⁻¹⁶ outside and 4π to 3×10⁻¹⁶ inside — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).
- **Poisson's relation (foundational).** For uniform density and magnetisation, the magnetic potential is the directional derivative of the gravity potential, so the magnetic field is the gravity-gradient tensor contracted with M: B = (μ₀/4π) ∇(M·∇)∫dV/R = (μ₀/(4πGρ)) T·M outside the body (inside it gives μ₀H; add μ₀M for B) — [†Blakely 1995, *Potential Theory in Gravity and Magnetic Applications*, DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511549816](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511549816). Computed: the prism Hessian gives a magnetised cube's far field equal to its dipole m = MV to 1.1×10⁻⁶ at 13 m, and at the cube's centre the Hessian is exactly −(4π/3)I, i.e. the demagnetising factor 1/3 to 10⁻¹⁶ — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).

### Inferences
- **Where it lives (DRY):** a `geopotential::bodies` module (name illustrative) holding `prism(p, box) -> (V, ∇V, Hessian)` and `polyhedron(p, verts, faces)` at unit Gρ, plus a one-line `magnetised(hessian, m) -> B`. `gravimetry::local` (gravity surveys, voids), `magnetics` (crustal/induced anomalies, magnetised rigs) and `gravitics`/`physica` rigs all depend on `geopotential` already, so no new dependency edge is needed. `gravimetry::local::sphere`/`cylinder` stay as the analytic special cases and become tests of the general code (a sphere and a prism must agree in the far field).
- **What it unifies:** the gravity forward model, the gravity-gradient forward model and the magnetic forward model become one Hessian; the inside/outside test (Σω_f) and Poisson's equation (trace −4πGρ) become shared tests. The prism Hessian for unit density is also the demagnetising tensor of a rectangular magnet, which `magnetics::magnets` (loops and solenoids only) lacks.
- The far-field cancellation (3.4×10⁻⁷ at 1000 side lengths) means the shared code should switch to the multipole (`geopotential` engine, or simply the point-mass limit) beyond ~100 body sizes; that switch is itself a natural test.
- Tesseroids (spherical prisms) are the next step for global topography/ice on a sphere; I did not research them in this session (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a published numeric test table from Nagy et al. (2000) or the 2002 erratum; the benchmarks above are exact identities and textbook limits, not the paper's own numbers.
- The brief's "Fukushima 2020" prism paper was not found by two searches; only Fukushima 2018a (GJI 215:864) is attested, through a conference abstract.
- Tesseroid formulas and their spherical-shell benchmark (Uieda et al. 2016, Geophysics) were not checked this session.

## Geometric integrators shared by orbits and charged particles (Boris, symplectic, guiding centre)

### Takeaway
A single `ode` module with RK4 (for non-Hamiltonian ODEs), Störmer–Verlet/leapfrog and Yoshida's
4th-order composition (for gravity), and the Boris rotation (for the Lorentz force) covers every
time integration in the family; the computed benchmarks reproduce the textbook behaviour exactly:
Boris keeps |v| to 10⁻¹⁵ over 10⁵ steps where RK4 loses N h⁶/72 = 1.39×10⁻³, and Verlet's Kepler
energy error stays bounded while RK4's grows linearly.

### Cited Findings
- **Already covered:** no shared integrator exists. `plasmoid::fusion::lane_emden` has an inline RK4; `physica/examples/gravity_patent.rs` advances the ball with a hand-written semi-implicit (symplectic) Euler step at `DT = 1e-3` s, with the Lorentz force inline; `gravitics::modified` tests cite "a direct orbit integration" for the Weber perihelion ratios; `magnetics::space_weather` has the dipole field and L-shells (`dipole_field`, `field_line_radius`) but no particle motion — (crate source, read this session).
- **Boris pusher (foundational).** "Why is Boris algorithm so good?" is Qin et al., Physics of Plasmas 20, 084503 (2013), DOI 10.1063/1.4818428 (not PRL 111 as the brief says; the PPPL preprint lists PRL as its submission target): the Boris map preserves phase-space volume although it is not symplectic, and the long-time energy bound of symplectic methods still applies — [PPPL-4872 abstract](https://bp-pub.pppl.gov/pub_report/2013/PPPL-4872-abs.html).
  - Algorithm: v⁻ = vⁿ + (q/m)E Δt/2; t = (q/m)B Δt/2; s = 2t/(1+t²); v′ = v⁻ + v⁻×t; v⁺ = v⁻ + v′×s; vⁿ⁺¹ = v⁺ + (q/m)E Δt/2; xⁿ⁺¹ = xⁿ + vⁿ⁺¹Δt — [Qin et al. 2013](https://bp-pub.pppl.gov/pub_report/2013/PPPL-4872-abs.html) (algorithm as implemented in `boris_step`, checks script).
  - Gyrophase correction: replacing |t| by tan(ΩΔt/2) makes the rotation exact and keeps volume preservation — [Zenitani & Umeda 2018, arXiv:1809.04378](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04378) (journal details not confirmed by search); multiple-substep and higher-order "hyper Boris" extensions — [Zenitani & Kato, arXiv:1905.12112](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12112), [Zenitani & Kato 2025, arXiv:2505.02270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02270), and a 2026 paper connecting them to 3-D rotation representations — [arXiv:2607.04465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04465) (title seen only). A 2021 Lie-operator analysis finds the symmetric second-order Boris form stays close to the exact orbit at steps longer than the cyclotron period — [arXiv:2109.01901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01901). Relativistic variants: Vay 2008 [†DOI 10.1063/1.2837054](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.2837054), Higuera & Cary 2017 [†DOI 10.1063/1.4979989](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4979989).
  - Computed benchmarks (Ω = 1, ΩΔt = 0.1): |v| after 10⁵ steps (1592 gyro-periods) = 1 + 7×10⁻¹⁶; RK4 at the same step loses 1.386×10⁻³ of the energy, matching N·h⁶/72 = 1.389×10⁻³ (RK4's amplification factor |R(ih)|² = 1 − h⁶/72 + …); the Boris rotation per step is exactly 2 atan(ΩΔt/2) (phase lag 5.23×10⁻³ rad per gyro-period ≈ 2π(ΩΔt)²/12); the Zenitani–Umeda form rotates by exactly ΩΔt; a particle started at the E×B velocity stays on it to 1.7×10⁻¹⁶ after 10⁴ steps — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).
  - Computed dipole benchmark: a 100 keV proton at L = 3 with equatorial pitch angle 30° in a B₀ = 3.12×10⁻⁵ T dipole (60 steps per minimum gyro-period, Boris) bounces in 17.490 s; the guiding-centre bounce period 4LR_E T(α)/v gives 17.462 s (0.16% apart, consistent with r_g/(L R_E) ≈ 10⁻³); |v| conserved to 10⁻¹⁴. T(α) = ∫₀^{λm} cosλ √(1+3sin²λ) dλ / √(1 − sin²α √(1+3sin²λ)/cos⁶λ), with exact limits T(0) = 1 + ln(2+√3)/(2√3) = 1.38017 and T(90°) = π/(3√2) = 0.74048, both reproduced by the quadrature — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py); the dipole bounce integral is from Schulz & Lanzerotti (1974) as documented in [rbamlib](https://rbamlib.readthedocs.io/dev/models/dip.html).
  - A 2026 comparison finds a Parker–Sochacki power-series method 4–13 orders of magnitude better in kinetic-energy conservation than RK methods in uniform, tanh and dipole fields, and that the symplectic Gauss–Legendre RK failed on all electron runs in the dipole problem (Boris not included) — [Jiles & Weigel 2026, arXiv:2604.20876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20876).
- **Guiding-centre integrators (foundational, 2008).** Qin & Guan, PRL 100, 035006 (2008): discretise the guiding-centre action rather than its equations; the result preserves a discrete symplectic structure and beats fixed- and variable-step RK4 over long times — [APS DOI](https://www.doi.org/10.1103/PHYSREVLETT.100.035006); the family's gauge properties and long-time energy conservation — [Squire, Qin & Tang 2014, arXiv:1401.6725](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1401.6725). This is the PRL the brief probably meant.
- **Symplectic orbit integrators (foundational).** Hairer, Lubich & Wanner, *Geometric Numerical Integration* (2006) [†DOI 10.1007/3-540-30666-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-30666-8); Yoshida's 4th-order composition with w₁ = 1/(2 − 2^{1/3}), w₀ = −2^{1/3}w₁ [†Phys. Lett. A 150:262, DOI 10.1016/0375-9601(90)90092-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/0375-9601(90)90092-3).
  - Computed Kepler benchmark (H = p²/2 − 1/|q|, e = 0.6, q₀ = (1−e, 0), p₀ = (0, √((1+e)/(1−e))), h = 0.01): Störmer–Verlet max|ΔH| = 3.71×10⁻⁴ over both 10 and 100 periods (bounded), |ΔL| = 2×10⁻¹⁴; Yoshida-4 max|ΔH| = 5.0×10⁻⁷ (bounded); RK4 |ΔH| = 1.1×10⁻⁷ after 10 periods and 1.1×10⁻⁶ after 100 (linear growth, ratio 10.006), |ΔL| = 1.9×10⁻⁷ — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).
  - Computed GR orbit benchmark: integrating u″ + u = GM/h² + 3GMu²/c² for Mercury (a = 5.7909×10¹⁰ m, e = 0.20563) gives 42.979″ per century, against the textbook 42.98″ — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py); value as in [†Will 2014, Living Rev. Relativ. 17:4](https://doi.org/10.12942/lrr-2014-4).

### Inferences
- **Where it lives:** a new leaf crate (single-word name per the user's naming rule, e.g. `numerics`; crates.io availability not checked) or a `math` module in `metrology`, because `plasmoid` (Lane–Emden, pinch particle orbits) and `gravitational` depend only on `metrology`. Contents: `rk4`, `verlet` (kick–drift–kick), `yoshida4` (three Verlet substeps), `boris(…, exact_phase: bool)`. Each is 5–15 lines; the checks script's versions are the test oracles.
- **What it replaces:** the inline RK4 in `plasmoid::fusion::lane_emden`; the semi-implicit Euler in `physica/examples/gravity_patent.rs` (Boris is the natural upgrade for its Lorentz term `lorentz[k]`); the orbit integration behind the `gravitics::modified` Weber test. It also enables a test that `magnetics::space_weather::dipole_field` + Boris reproduces the bounce period above.
- The same Verlet step integrates the Hamiltonian ray equations (next section), so one integrator serves orbits, particles and rays.
- Guiding-centre variational integrators matter only for very long runs in strong fields (tokamak, radiation belts); no current crate function needs them. Skip until one does.

### Gaps
- No published "energy error after N gyro-periods" table was retrieved; the numbers above are computed and match the analytic RK4 amplification factor.
- The brief's "Qin et al. 2013 PRL 111" does not match: the Boris paper is Phys. Plasmas 20, 084503 (2013); I found no PRL 111 paper on it.
- Hamlin et al.'s (1961) approximation T(α) ≈ 1.30 − 0.56 sin α was not found in a searchable source; the exact integral was used instead.

## Ray tracing that unifies optics in media, plasma and curved spacetime

### Takeaway
One Hamiltonian ray integrator, dx/ds = k, dk/ds = ½∇n² with H = ½(k² − n²) = 0, traces light
through the Sun's Gordon optical index (1.751201″ at the limb, computed) and through an
ionospheric plasma (exact parabola in a linear layer, computed), and the path integral of (n − 1)
gives both the Shapiro delay and the ionospheric 40.3 TEC/f² delay; the strong-field end is an
exact quadrature (Darwin) that converges to Bozza's coefficients (b̄ = −0.4002).

### Cited Findings
- **Already covered:** `gravitics::spacetime::light_bending` (second-order series 4m/b + c₂(m/b)², c₂ = 15π/4 in GR and 4π in Puthoff's polarizable vacuum; tested at 1.7512″); `gravitational::propagation::point_lens_geometric`, `lens_parameter`, `wave_optics_peak`; `gravitational::hf::plasma_frequency`, `critical_density` and `plasma_index` (whose doc comment already mentions the Gordon optical metric n = 1/√g₀₀). No ray integrator, no exact deflection, no Shapiro delay and no TEC/group delay exist in any crate (greps for `shapiro`, `tec`, `40.3`) — (crate source, read this session).
- **Gordon optical metric.** Gordon (1923) wrote light in a moving medium as a metric [†Ann. Phys. 377:421, DOI 10.1002/andp.19233772202](https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.19233772202); the converse used here, a static metric as a medium, gives Schwarzschild in isotropic coordinates the index n(r) = (1 + r_s/4r)³/(1 − r_s/4r) (derived; implemented as `gordon_isotropic`). Computed: tracing a ray at impact parameter R_sun through this index with RK4 and adding the analytic tails beyond 2×10⁴ R_sun gives 1.751201″, against the crate's 1.7512″ — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).
- **Plasma ray tracing.** In a cold unmagnetised plasma n² = 1 − ω_p²/ω²; for a linear layer ω_p²/ω² = z/H the ray is a parabola (the plasma is a uniform "gravity" for the photon) with apex H cos²θ₀ and ground range 2H sin 2θ₀ — computed to 10⁻¹³ with the same integrator — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py); standard treatment in [†Budden 1985, *The Propagation of Radio Waves*, DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511564321](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511564321). The HF ionospheric ray-tracing toolbox PHaRLAP is the 2014 reference implementation [†Cervera & Harris 2014, JGR 119:431, DOI 10.1002/2013JA019247](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013JA019247).
  - Ionospheric constant: e²/(8π²ε₀mₑ) = 40.3082 m³ s⁻² (computed), so the first-order range error is 40.3 TEC/f², 0.1624 m per TECU at GPS L1 (1575.42 MHz) — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py); standard GPS usage [†Klobuchar 1987, IEEE TAES 23:325, DOI 10.1109/TAES.1987.310829](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAES.1987.310829). Computed: integrating the exact index through a Chapman layer (NmF2 = 10¹² m⁻³, h_m = 300 km, H = 60 km; 24.80 TECU) gives a 4.0271 m phase advance and 4.0271 m group delay at L1, equal to 40.3 TEC/f² to 5×10⁻⁶ and 1.6×10⁻⁵ (second-order terms) — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).
  - Vertical-incidence virtual height of a parabolic layer, h′ = h_b + (y_m/2)(f/f_c) ln[(1 + f/f_c)/(1 − f/f_c)] (classical result, attributed to Appleton & Beynon 1940; not opened), matches numerical integration of the group index 1/√(1−X) to 4×10⁻¹¹ at f/f_c = 0.5 and 0.9 — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).
- **Delays: one (n − 1) integral.** With n − 1 = 2GM/(rc²) (weak-field Gordon index) the path integral gives the Shapiro delay, computed 247.2 µs round trip Earth–Mars grazing the Sun — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py); original prediction [†Shapiro 1964, PRL 13:789](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.13.789); Cassini's γ − 1 = (2.1 ± 2.3)×10⁻⁵ [†Bertotti, Iess & Tortora 2003, Nature 425:374](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01997). With n − 1 = −40.3 N/f² (phase) or +40.3 N/f² (group) the same integral is the ionospheric delay above.
- **Strong field.** Bozza (2002) gives, for Schwarzschild, α(b) ≈ −ā ln(b/b_c − 1) + b̄ with ā = 1, b̄ = −π + b_R + ln 6 = −0.4002, b_R = 2 ln[6(2−√3)], u_m = 3√3/2 (in r_s units) — [Bozza 2002, arXiv:gr-qc/0208075 (PRD 66, 103001)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0208075); exact integral [†Darwin 1959, Proc. R. Soc. A 249:180](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1959.0015).
  - Computed: b̄ = ln[216(7 − 4√3)] − π = −0.400230; the exact deflection α = 2∫₀^{u₀} du/√(1/b² − u² + 2u³) − π (G = c = M = 1, root divided out analytically) gives 4.22966, 6.51065 and 8.81049 rad at b/b_c − 1 = 10⁻², 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴, differing from Bozza by 2.5×10⁻², 3.1×10⁻³, 3.8×10⁻⁴ (linear convergence, as expected); at b = 2×10⁴ M it equals 4/b + 15π/(4b²) to 2.7×10⁻⁸, an independent check of the 15π/4 coefficient in `gravitics::spacetime::light_bending` — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).
  - Shadows (computed, 2√27 GM/(c²D)): M87* with M = 6.5×10⁹ M_sun at 16.8 Mpc gives 39.7 µas, against the EHT ring 42 ± 3 µas [†EHT 2019 Paper I, ApJL 875 L1](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ab0ec7); Sgr A* with 4.0×10⁶ M_sun at 8.0 kpc gives 51.3 µas, against the EHT 2022 ring 51.8 ± 2.3 µas and shadow 48.7 ± 7.0 µas [†EHT 2022 Paper I, ApJL 930 L12](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ac6674).
  - Kerr equatorial photon orbits r_ph = 2M{1 + cos[(2/3) arccos(∓a/M)]}: 3M at a = 0, M (prograde) and 4M (retrograde) at a = M, reproduced exactly — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py); formula from [†Bardeen, Press & Teukolsky 1972, ApJ 178:347](https://doi.org/10.1086/151796).

### Inferences
- **Where it lives:** the integrator itself is the `numerics` Verlet/RK4 step (shared with orbits). The index functions stay with their physics: `gravitics::spacetime` (Gordon index; plus an `exact_deflection(b)` quadrature, ~10 lines, that would replace nothing but test `light_bending`), `gravitational::hf::plasma_index` (already there). Two one-liners would complete the unification: `gravitational::hf::group_delay(tec, f) = 40.3·TEC/(c f²)` and `gravitics::spacetime::shapiro_delay(m, r1, r2, b)`. Their shared test: both equal ∫(n − 1)ds/c computed by one quadrature.
- The sibling notes `light_in_plasma_and_magnetic_fields.md` cover plasma optics physics in more depth; this section only claims the shared ray/delay machinery.
- Kerr null geodesics in general need a Hamiltonian integrator in Boyer–Lindquist coordinates (H = ½g^{μν}p_μp_ν = 0); the same RK4 step works, but no crate currently needs Kerr ray tracing. Skip until one does.

## Special functions and transforms (Faddeeva, elliptic integrals, Bessel, spherical-harmonic transforms, Slepian functions)

### Takeaway
The family already computes K, E (AGM, private in `gravitics::electrodes`), Jₙ (Simpson on Bessel's
integral, `plasmoid`), Legendre functions to degree 2700 (`geopotential`) and an FFT
(`gravimetry::noise`), each in one crate; making Carlson's R_F/R_D and Bulirsch's cel public
(passing Carlson's 1995 check values to 10⁻¹²) would give `magnetics` off-axis loop and solenoid
fields, a Faddeeva/Z function would give both plasma Landau damping (1.41566 − 0.15336i at
kλ_D = 0.5, computed) and the kinetic Jeans instability, and a Gauss–Legendre analysis plus
Slepian polar caps would add the missing grid-to-coefficient transform and basin averaging.

### Cited Findings
- **Already covered:** `gravitics::electrodes::elliptic` (private; K and E by the AGM, tested against A&S Table 17.1 at m = 0.5) used for charged rings; `plasmoid::relaxation::bessel_j` (Simpson's rule on Bessel's integral, 2000 panels) with constants `J01`, `J11`, `SPHERE_EIGENVALUE`; `geopotential::Legendre` (Holmes & Featherstone 2002, to degree 2700); `gravimetry::noise::fft`; `gravitational::integrate_log` (Simpson on a log grid); `gravimetry::gaussian_weights` (Jekeli recursion with a floor); `gravimetry::mass_coefficients` (point masses to coefficients). `magnetics::magnets::loop_bz` and `solenoid_bz` are on-axis only. No erf, Faddeeva or plasma dispersion function exists in any crate — (crate source, read this session).
- **Faddeeva / plasma dispersion function.** Z(ζ) = i√π w(ζ), w(z) = e^{−z²} erfc(−iz). Algorithm 916 (Zaghloul & Ali 2011, ACM TOMS 38:15) is accurate across the plane with a speed/accuracy parameter — [arXiv:1106.0151](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.0151); libcerf uses a continued fraction for large |z| (after Gautschi 1970 and Poppe & Wijers 1990) and Algorithm 916 elsewhere — [libcerf w_of_z(3)](https://man.archlinux.org/man/w_of_z.3.en); a 2024 Chebyshev-subinterval Voigt code targets ~10⁻⁶ at high speed and is used in the Meudon PDR code — [Zaghloul & Le Bourlot 2024, arXiv:2411.00917](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00917v1). Weideman's (1994) rational approximation [†SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 31:1497, DOI 10.1137/0731077](https://doi.org/10.1137/0731077) is the compact alternative.
  - Computed checks (series |z| < 3, Laplace continued fraction beyond): w(i) = e·erfc(1) = 0.4275835761558071; Re w(1) = e⁻¹; Im w(1)·√π/2 = Dawson F(1) = 0.5380795069127684; series and continued fraction agree to 3×10⁻¹³ at |z| ≈ 3 — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).
  - Computed Landau benchmark: the Maxwellian Langmuir dispersion 1 + [1 + ζZ(ζ)]/(kλ_D)² = 0, ζ = ω/(√2 k v_th), has its least-damped root at ω/ω_p = 1.41566 − 0.15336i for kλ_D = 0.5, the pair commonly quoted as 1.4156/0.1533 — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py). Conflict: one 2019 Vlasov–Ampère paper running this test quotes an expected decay rate γ = −0.155 without stating its normalisation — [arXiv:1910.02099](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.02099).
  - Computed kinetic Jeans benchmark (same Z, the attractive sign): 1 − (k_J²/k²)[1 + ζZ(ζ)] = 0 reduces on the imaginary axis to k²/k_J² = 1 − √π y e^{y²} erfc(y), with growth rate γ = √2 kσ y; at k = k_J/2, y = 0.971850, and the root satisfies the complex dispersion relation to 4×10⁻¹⁶ — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py); the gravitational analogue is standard in [†Binney & Tremaine 2008, *Galactic Dynamics*, §5.2](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691130279/galactic-dynamics).
- **Elliptic integrals.** Carlson (1995, Numer. Algorithms 10:13–26) gives the duplication algorithm λ = √x√y + √x√z + √y√z, (x, y, z) → (x+λ, y+λ, z+λ)/4, stopping when 4⁻ⁿQ < |Aₙ|, then R_F ≈ A^{−1/2}(1 − E₂/10 + E₃/14 + E₂²/24 − 3E₂E₃/44), with check values R_F(1,2,0) = 1.3110287771461, R_F(2,3,4) = 0.58408284167715, R_D(0,2,1) = 1.7972103521034, R_D(2,3,4) = 0.16510527294261, R_J(0,1,2,3) = 0.77688623778582, R_C(9/4,2) = ln 2 — [Carlson 1995, arXiv:math/9409227](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math/9409227). Computed: my R_F and R_D reproduce the four values to < 10⁻¹²; K(m) = R_F(0, 1−m, 1) and E(m) = R_F − (m/3)R_D reproduce the electrodes test values K(0.5) = 1.854074677301372, E(0.5) = 1.350643881047675 to 10⁻¹⁵ — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).
  - Bulirsch's general complete integral cel(k_c, p, a, b) [†Bulirsch 1969, Numer. Math. 13:305, DOI 10.1007/BF02165405](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02165405), in the form of Derby & Olbert's cylindrical-magnet paper [†Am. J. Phys. 78:229 (2010), DOI 10.1119/1.3256157](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.3256157): computed cel(k_c,1,1,1) = K and cel(k_c,1,1,k_c²) = E to 10⁻¹³; the Derby–Olbert off-axis solenoid field equals `magnetics::magnets::solenoid_bz` on the axis to 10⁻¹⁵ and a 4000-loop sum off axis to 8×10⁻⁹ (B_z) and 2.4×10⁻⁷ (B_ρ, limited by the loop sum) — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).
  - Current loop off axis: the textbook K, E formulas for B_z and B_ρ match a 20,000-element Biot–Savart sum to 8×10⁻¹⁵ and `loop_bz` on the axis exactly, but B_ρ at ρ = 10⁻⁷a is wrong by 6.1×10⁻³ (catastrophic cancellation) — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py). This is the instability Ortner, Leitner & Slanovc (2023) analyse; their cel-based expression is stable to 15–16 significant figures everywhere and faster — [Magnetism 3:11–31](https://silicon-austria-labs.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/numerically-stable-and-computationally-efficient-expression-for-t), and Magpylib uses it for its circular-current field — [Magpylib core docs](https://magpylib.readthedocs.io/en/5.2.1/_autogen/magpylib.core.html).
- **Bessel functions.** Computed: the crate's own method (Simpson, 2000 panels) gives J₀(1) = 0.7651976865579665, J₁(1) = 0.4400505857449335, J₀(2.404825557695773) = −4×10⁻¹⁷ and J₀(50) = 0.0558123276692518 (A&S value 0.05581232766925182) — accurate to ~10⁻¹⁶, because the periodic integrand makes the trapezoid/Simpson sum spectrally accurate — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py). Zeros and tables: [†DLMF §10.21](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.21); complex-argument algorithm [†Amos 1986, ACM TOMS 12:265, DOI 10.1145/7921.214331](https://doi.org/10.1145/7921.214331).
- **Gaussian smoothing weights are Bessel functions.** Computed: the Jekeli/Wahr weights in `gravimetry::gaussian_weights` satisfy W_n = i_n(b)/i₀(b), modified spherical Bessel functions (the recursion W_{n+1} = −(2n+1)/b W_n + W_{n−1} and W₁ = coth b − 1/b are exactly i_n's), confirmed against direct quadrature of the Legendre transform of e^{−b(1−cos γ)} to 10⁻¹³ at n = 30, 300 km. Forward recursion of this minimal solution is unstable: without the crate's 10⁻¹⁰ floor its error is 1.7×10⁻¹³ (300 km, n = 96) but 1.0×10⁻³ (500 km, n = 120) and 2×10¹⁴ (1000 km, n = 96); the crate's floor caps the error at 5–7×10⁻⁹. Backward recurrence (continued fraction i_n/i_{n−1} = 1/[(2n+1)/b + i_{n+1}/i_n]) gives the exact weights at every radius and degree — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py); the weights' definition is [Wahr et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1029/98JB02844).
- **Spherical-harmonic transforms.** SHTns (Schaeffer 2013, G³ 14:751, DOI 10.1002/ggge.20071) uses Gauss–Legendre quadrature with on-the-fly Legendre recurrence, O(N³) yet faster than lower-complexity codes up to N = 1023 — [arXiv:1202.6522](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1202.6522v3). Reinecke, Belkner & Carron (2023) extend fast SHTs to arbitrary grids via iso-latitude grids and non-uniform FFTs (ducc), with a 2026 partial-sky follow-up — [arXiv:2304.10431 via lenspyx docs](https://lenspyx.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_sources/experimental.rst.txt), [arXiv:2603.17166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17166). Driscoll–Healy sampling [†Adv. Appl. Math. 15:202 (1994), DOI 10.1006/aama.1994.1008](https://doi.org/10.1006/aama.1994.1008); SHTOOLS [†Wieczorek & Meschede 2018, G³, DOI 10.1029/2018GC007529](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018GC007529).
  - Computed: synthesising random 4π-normalised coefficients to degree 16 on (L+1) Gauss–Legendre latitudes × (2L+2) longitudes and analysing back recovers every coefficient to 1.6×10⁻¹⁴ — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).
- **Slepian localisation.** Simons, Dahlen & Wieczorek (2006, SIAM Rev. 48:504–536, DOI 10.1137/S0036144504445765): bandlimited functions optimally concentrated in a region, from an algebraic eigenproblem; the eigenvalue sum is the Shannon number; the polar cap commutes with a Sturm–Liouville operator — [arXiv:math/0408424](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0408424). Computed for a 40° cap at L = 18: N = (L+1)²(1 − cos Θ)/2 = 42.229 equals the eigenvalue sum to 10⁻¹²; λ₁…₃ = 1.000000, λ₄₂ = 0.579, and 42 eigenvalues exceed 0.5 — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py). GRACE application to Greenland [†Harig & Simons 2012, PNAS, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1206064109](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1206064109).

### Inferences
- **Where it lives:** `numerics::special` (leaf crate or `metrology::math`): `carlson_rf`, `carlson_rd`, `ellipk`, `ellipe`, `cel`, `bessel_j` (moved from plasmoid unchanged; its accuracy is fine), `faddeeva`, `plasma_z`, `spherical_i_ratios` (backward recurrence). `plasmoid` (Landau damping, Bessel model), `gravitics` (electrodes, a kinetic Jeans function in `modified` if wanted), `magnetics` (off-axis loops/solenoids via Ortner's cel form) and `gravimetry` (weights) all reach it through `metrology`'s level.
- **What it replaces:** the private `electrodes::elliptic` (AGM stays valid; Carlson adds incomplete integrals and R_J); the inline Simpson in `bessel_j`; the recursion-plus-floor in `gaussian_weights` (a ~10-line backward recurrence removes the floor heuristic; practical impact today is small, errors ≤ 7×10⁻⁹). One shared unification worth a doc test in `physica::analogies`: the charged ring (`electrodes`), a ring of mass (gravity), and a current loop (magnetics) all come from the same ring average of the Laplace kernel, ∮dφ′/|x − x′| = 4K(m)/√((a+ρ)²+z²).
- **Where SHT/Slepian live:** `geopotential` (owns `Legendre` and `Coefficients`): an `analysis(grid) -> Coefficients` on a Gauss–Legendre grid reusing `gravimetry::noise::fft` (the FFT would move down to `numerics` so geopotential can use it), and `slepian::polar_cap(L, Θ)`. That addresses gravimetry's stated "not included yet: basin averages and leakage corrections" and serves magnetic satellite data equally.
- The kinetic Jeans/Landau identity (same Z, opposite sign of the coupling) is a candidate `physica::analogies` doc test alongside "Pinch and filament".

### Gaps
- No primary source for the (1.4156, −0.1533) Landau root was opened (Fried & Conte 1961 tables, Cheng & Knorr 1976 were not retrieved); the computed root and the 1910.02099 value (−0.155) disagree at the 1% level, probably a normalisation difference.
- DLMF test values for w(z) at complex arguments were not fetched; checks used exact identities (w(iy) = e^{y²}erfc y, Re w(x) = e^{−x²}).
- Published Slepian eigenvalue tables for a specific (L, Θ) were not retrieved; only the exact trace identity is a published benchmark.

## Green's functions and multipole methods (Laplace kernel, FMM, Barnes–Hut)

### Takeaway
Newton, Coulomb and Biot–Savart are one Laplace Green's function: gravity and electrostatics sum
q/|x − x′|, and the magnetic vector potential sums J/|x − x′| componentwise; `geopotential`'s
spherical-harmonic `Coefficients` already are the exterior multipole expansion (P2M is
`gravimetry::mass_coefficients`), so an FMM would reuse them, but no current caller has enough
sources to need one.

### Cited Findings
- **Already covered:** `gravitics::electrodes` sums ring and segment kernels directly and solves the dense collocation system with its own Gaussian elimination (`gauss`); `gravimetry::sim` has its own `cholesky`, `cholesky_solve`, `cholesky_inverse_diagonal` (two dense solvers in two crates); `gravimetry::mass_coefficients` turns point masses into coefficients (the particle-to-multipole step); `geopotential::partials` evaluates the expansion (multipole-to-particle); `gravitational::laboratory` computes LIGO's rotor force "by direct summation" — (crate source and READMEs, read this session).
- **Fast multipole method (foundational).** Greengard & Rokhlin 1987 [†J. Comput. Phys. 73:325, DOI 10.1016/0021-9991(87)90140-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9991(87)90140-9); 3-D version [†Cheng, Greengard & Rokhlin 1999, JCP 155:468, DOI 10.1006/jcph.1999.6355](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcph.1999.6355). The Flatiron FMM3D library (successor of FMMLIB3D) evaluates Laplace and Helmholtz sums to a requested precision ε with effort O((M+N) log^{3/2}(1/ε)) — [FMM3D docs](https://fmm3d.readthedocs.io/).
  - Computed truncation bound: 60 positive sources inside a unit sphere evaluated at r = 3.24 obey |Φ − Φ_p| ≤ [Q/(r − a)](a/r)^{p+1} for every p = 0…12 — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py) (the Greengard–Rokhlin error bound, from †1987 above).
- **Barnes–Hut (foundational).** [†Barnes & Hut 1986, Nature 324:446, DOI 10.1038/324446a0](https://doi.org/10.1038/324446a0); accuracy study [†Hernquist 1987, ApJS 64:715, DOI 10.1086/191215](https://doi.org/10.1086/191215). Computed, monopole-only octree, N = 1000 uniform in a cube, against direct summation: θ = 0.3 → median force error 1.0×10⁻³ (99th percentile 4.1×10⁻³), 431 interactions per body; θ = 0.5 → 4.5×10⁻³ (2.5×10⁻²), 193; θ = 0.7 → 1.2×10⁻² (5.9×10⁻²), 101; θ = 1.0 → 3.2×10⁻² (2.0×10⁻¹), 48; direct = 999 — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).

### Inferences
- **Unification:** Biot–Savart is ∇× of A = (μ₀/4π)∫J/|x − x′|, three scalar Laplace sums; gravitomagnetism (`gravitics::gem`) is the same with G/c² for μ₀/4π and mass current for J (with the spin-2 factor the crate already tracks). A single `laplace_sum(sources, targets) -> (Φ, ∇Φ)` would serve electrodes, a loop discretised into segments, and mass models. The ring/segment closed forms in `electrodes` are already exact "panel" integrals of that kernel.
- **DRY now (cheap):** move `electrodes::gauss` and `gravimetry::sim::cholesky*` into `numerics::linalg` (two dense solvers → one module, each kept as is).
- **Defer:** FMM/Barnes–Hut. At N ≤ a few thousand (the patent rig has 24 rings plus lead segments) direct summation is exact and fast; Barnes–Hut at θ = 0.5 cuts work only ~5× at N = 1000 for 0.5% errors. Build it only when a caller has 10⁴+ sources.

### Gaps
- I found no published accuracy/cost benchmark specific to an FMM for the Biot–Savart kernel; the FMM3D docs give the complexity but no table was fetched.
- Hernquist's (1987) published error-versus-θ numbers were not retrieved, so the Barnes–Hut numbers above are this session's own runs, not a published comparison.

## Dimensionless numbers and scaling laws as one shared table

### Takeaway
The MHD numbers are already in `magnetics::mhd` but the Lundquist number lives separately in
`plasmoid::relaxation` with its own inline Alfvén speed and Spitzer coefficient, and the plasma
frequency is written twice (`gravitational::hf`, `magnetics::mhd::inertial_length`); exact
identities (Ha = S/√Pm, Rm = Re·Pm, Λ = S²·E_m, (k_J/k_D)² = Gm²/(e²/4πε₀) = 8.09×10⁻³⁷ for
protons) pass to 10⁻¹³ and are natural `physica::analogies` doc tests.

### Cited Findings
- **Already covered:** `magnetics::mhd::alfven_speed`, `beta`, `magnetic_pressure`, `magnetic_diffusivity`, `magnetic_reynolds`, `hartmann_number`, `magnetic_prandtl`, `elsasser`, `free_decay_time`, `inertial_length`, `gyroradius`; `plasmoid::relaxation::lundquist` (Spitzer η = 1.03×10⁻⁴ lnΛ T^{−3/2}/1.96 and v_A = B/√(μ₀ n A m_p) written inline); `plasmoid::pinch::Resistivity` (the same Spitzer coefficient as 9.71×10³ = 1/1.03×10⁻⁴ and the factor 1.96); `plasmoid::discharge::cunningham`, `sphere_drag` (Knudsen number), `mean_free_path`; `gravitational::hf::plasma_frequency` and `critical_density`, while `magnetics::mhd::inertial_length` recomputes ω_p inline; the virial theorem appears in three applications (`magnetics::magnets::virial_specific_energy`, `plasmoid::relaxation::virial_energy_cap`, `plasmoid::fusion::virial_temperature`); `physica::analogies` already carries Bennett ↔ Ostriker and the vacuum constants — (crate source, read this session). No Rossby, Ekman or Debye-length function exists (greps).
- Computed identities (liquid sodium: σ = 10⁷ S/m, ν = 7×10⁻⁷ m²/s, ρ = 930 kg/m³, B = 0.1 T, L = 1 m, U = 1 m/s, Ω = 10 s⁻¹): Ha = S/√Pm (12,393.9), Rm = Re·Pm (12.566), Elsasser Λ = σB²/(ρΩ) = S²·E_m with E_m = η/(ΩL²) (10.753), S = Rm at U = v_A (36.759), all to ≤ 3×10⁻¹⁶ relative — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).
- Computed: the Jeans and Debye wavenumbers have the same form, k_J² = 4πGρ/c_s² and k_D² = ne²/(ε₀k_BT); for hydrogen their squared ratio is Gm_p²/(e²/4πε₀) = 8.0936×10⁻³⁷, the gravity-to-Coulomb force ratio — [checks script](unifying_algorithms_checks.py).
- Gravity–magnetism critical mass-to-flux ratio: a magnetised self-gravitating sheet collapses when Σ/B > 1/(2π√G) in Gaussian units [†Nakano & Nakamura 1978, PASJ 30:671](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1978PASJ...30..671N) (my SI conversion: 1/√(πGμ₀); not checked against a source); the cloud value c_Φ ≈ 0.126 is from [†Mouschovias & Spitzer 1976, ApJ 210:326, DOI 10.1086/154835](https://doi.org/10.1086/154835).
- Dimensionless plasma similarity (ρ*, β, ν*) underlies tokamak scaling laws such as `plasmoid::tokamak::ipb98` [†Connor & Taylor 1977, Nucl. Fusion 17:1047, DOI 10.1088/0029-5515/17/5/015](https://doi.org/10.1088/0029-5515/17/5/015).

### Inferences
- **DRY, smallest change:** inside `plasmoid`, make `relaxation::lundquist` use one `spitzer(te, ln_lambda, z, Resistivity)` function shared with `pinch` (same crate, no dependency change). Across crates, do not add dependency edges for one-liners: instead add `physica::analogies` doc tests asserting `gravitational::hf::plasma_frequency` agrees with `magnetics::mhd::inertial_length` (c/ω_p) and that `plasmoid::relaxation::lundquist`'s v_A equals `magnetics::mhd::alfven_speed`. That catches drift without moving code.
- **A shared table** (if wanted) belongs in `magnetics::mhd` (it already holds most entries); the identities above become its tests. Rossby and Ekman numbers would be two one-liners there for `dynamo`.
- Candidate new analogies (each an exact identity): Jeans ↔ Debye (k_J²/k_D² = force ratio); kinetic Jeans ↔ Landau (same Z, opposite sign); magnetic critical mass-to-flux 1/(2π√G) beside Bennett ↔ Ostriker.
- **Overall order, by value over cost (across all sections):** (1) prism + polyhedron + Poisson in `geopotential`; (2) a `numerics` leaf (rk4/verlet/yoshida4/boris; carlson/cel/faddeeva; `gauss`, `cholesky` and `fft` moved down from `gravitics::electrodes`, `gravimetry::sim` and `gravimetry::noise`); (3) backward-recurrence `gaussian_weights`; (4) Gauss–Legendre analysis and Slepian polar caps in `geopotential`; (5) exact deflection, Shapiro and TEC delay one-liners; (6) `physica::analogies` doc tests for the duplicates and identities. Defer FMM/Barnes–Hut, guiding-centre integrators and Kerr ray tracing until a caller needs them.
