# Shared kernels, not a new crate, unify physica

The 2022–2026 literature supports about forty additions to the physica crates that reproduce published numbers. Five shared algorithms carry most of the value, because each serves two or more crates at once. The first is a closed-form prism and polyhedron kernel returning potential, gravity and gradient tensor: it gives `gravimetry` its missing bodies and `magnetics` its first crustal-anomaly model through Poisson's relation, exact to 3×10⁻²⁴ T. The second is a set of FFT-based planar transforms that work on gravity and magnetic grids alike. The third is Longman's Earth tide, reproduced to 10⁻⁹ mGal; no absolute gravimeter reaches µGal without a tide model. The fourth is the Kerr ringdown and remnant fits, which reproduce GW150914's 251 Hz ringdown. The fifth is a Faddeeva plasma dispersion function that gives the Landau root 1.415662 − 0.153359i and, with its sign flipped, the kinetic Jeans instability. The same checks found ten problems in the published crates. `gravimetry::gaussian_weights` recurses an unstable minimal solution, held to 7×10⁻⁹ only by a 10⁻¹⁰ floor. `gravimetry::atom` hard-codes double diffraction, so it halves a ground gradiometer's noise. `gravitics::electrodes` loses 5% of the radial field 10⁻⁷ ring radii off the axis. A doc comment in `gravitational::hf` gives an optical index that halves light bending. And the Spitzer coefficient, plasma frequency, Alfvén speed, dense solvers and FFT are each written twice or sit where the next caller cannot reach them. The dependency graph makes `geopotential` the only possible home below `physica` for numerics that gravity and magnetics share, so no ninth crate is needed (`numerics`, `numerica` and `quadrature` are taken on crates.io anyway). Several proposals in the notes already live in, or are planned for, the user's separate `geo5d` crate: normal gravity, W₀, clock geodesy, the Shapiro delay and PPN parameters. They become new test vectors there, not new code. Every number below was recomputed by the seven check scripts in `notes/`, all passing on 2026-10-25, or by one script added for this report. Values that rest on search snippets, memory or unopened sources are held back in a verify-before-coding table.

## Five shared kernels do more than forty separate formulas

No crate models a prism, a polyhedron, a tesseroid or a magnetised body; `gravimetry::local` stops at a sphere and a horizontal cylinder ([local.rs](../../../crates/gravimetry/src/local.rs)). Nagy, Papp & Benedek give the prism's potential and its derivatives as one signed sum over the corners ([Nagy et al. 2000](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s001900000116)). Werner and Scheeres' edge-and-face sums do the same for any triangulated polyhedron, and the face solid angles double as a free inside/outside test: they sum to 4π inside and 0 outside ([Werner & Scheeres 1997](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00053511)). ESA's open polyhedral-gravity library evaluates the Eros mesh in **253 µs per point** ([Schuhmacher et al. 2024](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.06384)). The checks script implements both. A 12-triangle cube **equals the prism to 7×10⁻¹⁶**. The tensor trace is zero outside and −4πGρ inside to 10⁻¹⁶. A wide thin slab gives the textbook Bouguer gradient of 0.1119 mGal/m ([unifying_algorithms_checks.py](notes/unifying_algorithms_checks.py)).

The payoff is Poisson's relation. For uniform density and magnetisation, the magnetic field is the gravity-gradient tensor contracted with M. So one Hessian at unit Gρ is at once the gravity model, the gradiometry model, the magnetic-anomaly model and the demagnetising tensor of a rectangular magnet. The cube-centre Hessian is exactly −(4π/3)I, a **demagnetising factor of 1/3 to 10⁻¹⁶** ([unifying_algorithms_checks.py](notes/unifying_algorithms_checks.py)). Applied to the crates' own sphere, the vertical gradient that `gravimetry::local::sphere` already returns predicts the pole magnetic anomaly **to 3.3×10⁻²⁴ T**. The crate's documented 5 m void at 20 m depth, with Γ_uu = −23.6 E, becomes a **13.09 nT magnetic low** if it also removes 1 A/m of magnetisation ([magnetics_sensing_checks.py](notes/magnetics_sensing_checks.py)). The one trap is numerical. The corner sum cancels far from the body, reaching 3.4×10⁻⁷ relative error at 1000 side lengths, so the kernel must hand over to the point mass or the existing multipole engine beyond about 100 body sizes ([unifying_algorithms_checks.py](notes/unifying_algorithms_checks.py)).

Gravity and magnetic surveys also share transforms of a gridded field:

- **Upward continuation:** multiply the 2-D spectrum by e^(−|k|h).
- **Reduction to the pole:** divide by Θ_mΘ_f.
- **Pseudo-gravity:** divide the reduced field by |k|.
- **Euler deconvolution:** solve the homogeneity equation for depth, with an integer structural index ([Reid & Thurston 2014, via SBGf](https://sbgf.org.br/mysbgf/eventos/expanded_abstracts/15th_CISBGf/session/POTENTIAL%20FIELDS%20METHODS/Correct%20structural%20index%20defined%20by%20base%20level%20estimates%20in%20Euler%20deconvolution%20.pdf)).

The first three come from Blakely's textbook, which was not opened. On a 128×128 grid the script:

- continues a sphere's anomaly 30 m up and matches the analytic one **to 6.9×10⁻⁴ rms**;
- reduces an I = 45° anomaly to the pole to 1.1% rms, where the opposite sign convention gives 168%, so the test pins the convention;
- matches pseudo-gravity to `gravimetry::local::sphere` to −0.13%;
- recovers a dipole's 20 m depth to 1.2×10⁻⁹ m ([magnetics_sensing_checks.py](notes/magnetics_sensing_checks.py)).

The reduction filter grows as 1/sin²I along the declination direction, reaching 33.2 at I = 10°. Low-latitude reduction is therefore an open problem, and the function should return a warning rather than a silent number. All four transforms need the FFT that today sits in `gravimetry::noise`, where `geopotential` and `magnetics` cannot reach it.

Plasma kinetics has one function at its centre. Z(ζ) = i√π w(ζ) gives Landau damping, ion-acoustic damping and the Voigt line shape. With the restoring term's sign flipped (ω_p² → −4πGρ), it gives the collisionless Jeans instability: a plasma–gravity twin that `physica::analogies` does not yet have, though its source must be read before it ships (see the verify table). Zaghloul & Ali publish reference values across the complex plane ([Zaghloul & Ali 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.0151)), and the plasma script reproduces them to 10⁻¹³. It also recovers the Maxwellian Langmuir root at kλ_D = 0.5, **1.415662 − 0.153359i ω_pe**, matching the Vlasov-code benchmark decay rate of 0.153359 ([arXiv 2301.05581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05581); [plasma_frontiers_checks.py](notes/plasma_frontiers_checks.py)). The trap is the real axis, where the true Re w(6.3 + 10⁻²⁰i) is 5.792×10⁻¹⁸. Poppe & Wijers' algorithm returns 1.479×10⁻²² there, and Weideman's rational series returns a negative value, a relative error of 2×10³. Either corrupts weak damping unless Re w = e^(−x²) is handled explicitly on the axis ([Zaghloul & Ali 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.0151); [plasma_frontiers_checks.py](notes/plasma_frontiers_checks.py)).

Three smaller identities join crates that look unrelated. The first is the tidal tensor. The lunar tide on a gravimeter (peak 109.99 µGal, gradient 1.73×10⁻⁴ E), a gradiometer's signal and a gravitational wave's ½ω²hL are one tidal tensor. `gravimetry::tides` and `gravitational::detectors::tidal_acceleration` therefore belong in one analogy ([gravity_frontiers_checks.py](notes/gravity_frontiers_checks.py)).

The second is that every propagation delay in the family is ∫(n − 1)ds/c:

- with n − 1 = 2GM/(rc²), it is the Shapiro delay;
- with ∓40.3N/f², it is the ionospheric phase advance and group delay. The constant is 40.3082 m³ s⁻² from CODATA, and a 24.8 TECU Chapman layer gives 4.0271 m at GPS L1, equal to 40.3 TEC/f² to 5×10⁻⁶;
- with the interstellar electron density, it is the dispersion delay k_DM·DM/f², with k_DM = 4.148808 ms GHz² cm³ pc⁻¹ ([unifying_algorithms_checks.py](notes/unifying_algorithms_checks.py); [light_in_plasma_checks.py](notes/light_in_plasma_checks.py)).

That last delay equals `gravitational::propagation::massive_graviton_delay` evaluated at the plasma's effective photon mass ħω_p, **to 10⁻¹²**. A function already in the crate computes plasma dispersion under a graviton's name ([light_in_plasma_checks.py](notes/light_in_plasma_checks.py)). The identity also shows why the 2025 FRB photon-mass bounds near 4.8×10⁻⁵¹ kg must model the dispersion measure first: interstellar plasma alone gives an effective mass of 1.15×10⁻⁴⁷ kg ([Zhang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14163)).

The third is projection noise. An atom gravimeter's floor, 1/(C√N)/(k_eff T²), and a Ramsey magnetometer's, 1/(C√N)/(γτ), differ only in the scale factor ([magnetics_sensing_checks.py](notes/magnetics_sensing_checks.py)).

The black-hole items share one piece of algebra. Bardeen, Press & Teukolsky's Kerr orbits give the ISCO that the remnant fits need and the photon orbits that the shadow functions need ([Bardeen, Press & Teukolsky 1972](https://doi.org/10.1086/151796)). That algebra belongs in `gravitational::inspiral`, beside the Schwarzschild-only `f_isco`. `gravitics` depends on `gravitational` and can call it there.

## Below physica, geopotential is the only home that gravity and magnetics share

The `Cargo.toml` files fix where code can live:

- `metrology` is a leaf with no dependencies.
- `geopotential`, `plasmoid` and `gravitational` depend only on `metrology`.
- `gravimetry` and `magnetics` depend on `geopotential` and `metrology`.
- `gravitics` depends on `geopotential`, `gravitational` and `metrology`.
- `physica` depends on all seven.

So anything that `plasmoid` or `gravitational` must call has to sit in `metrology` or a new leaf crate. Anything needed only by gravity, magnetics and the electrode code can sit in `geopotential`. `physica` can test across every crate, but it cannot host library code that a leaf crate calls without a cycle. The notes proposed a new `numerics` leaf crate for integrators, special functions, solvers and the FFT ([unifying notes](notes/unifying_algorithms_across_crates.md)). Counting the actual callers points elsewhere. Every shared routine with two or more callers today, or with a caller planned below, serves crates that already depend on `geopotential`:

- **FFT:** `gravimetry` now; next `geopotential`'s grid analysis, the planar transforms, and the band-pass filter in `magnetics::aeromag`.
- **Dense solvers:** the electrode collocation solve and the GRACE simulator's Cholesky now; next the aeromagnetic and Euler least squares.
- **Elliptic integrals:** the electrodes now; next off-axis loops in `magnetics`.

The routines that `plasmoid` or `gravitational` would need each have one caller: the Faddeeva function, the Bessel J function and the Lane–Emden Runge–Kutta step ([fusion.rs](../../../crates/plasmoid/src/fusion.rs); [relaxation.rs](../../../crates/plasmoid/src/relaxation.rs)).

| Option | What it would hold | Who can call it | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Modules in `geopotential` | FFT (re-exported from `gravimetry::noise`); Cholesky and pivoted Gauss; K, E, Carlson R_F/R_D/R_J and Bulirsch's cel; Gauss–Legendre nodes; prism, polyhedron and ring kernels; planar transforms | `gravimetry`, `magnetics`, `gravitics`, `physica` | No new dependency edge; the crate's scope widens from "Earth's gravity field" to "potential fields", which is what Poisson's relation says it already is | **Recommended** for everything gravity, magnetics and electrostatics share |
| A module in `metrology` | Anything `plasmoid` or `gravitational` must share | Every crate | Breaks the crate's contract ("pin the version to pin the constants"), because numerics fixes would bump the constants crate | Not recommended |
| New leaf crate (single-word name) | Integrators and special functions for every level | Every crate that adds the edge | A ninth crate against the user's "prefer merging" rule; `numerics`, `numerica` and `quadrature` are taken (`cargo search`, 2026-10-25) | Only when a routine gains callers on both sides of the `metrology` level |
| `physica` | Cross-crate identity tests, analogies and examples | Sees every crate | Cannot host code that a leaf crate calls | Use to pin twins together |
| Stay local | Faddeeva (`plasmoid`), Bessel J (`plasmoid`), Lane–Emden RK4 (`plasmoid`), modified-spherical-Bessel ratios (`gravimetry`), a Boris step (its first caller) | One crate | Duplication only once a second crate needs it | Default until then |

The plasma one-liners are the exception the graph forces. The plasma frequency, critical density and Alfvén speed are each one line. `gravitational`, `magnetics` and `plasmoid` all need them, and their only common dependency is `metrology`. Three copies of √(ne²/ε₀m), pinned together by one `physica` identity test and marked with a `shortcut:` comment, cost less than either a constants crate that computes or a crate created for three formulas.

The larger duplication risk is outside the workspace. The user's `geo5d` 0.5.1 already ships the following ([geo5d](https://crates.io/crates/geo5d); source read 2026-10-25):

- GRS80 and WGS84 Somigliana normal gravity, with U₀;
- both conventional W₀ values (62 636 856.0 and 62 636 853.4 m²s⁻²) and L_G;
- geopotential numbers with dynamic, normal and orthometric heights;
- clock rates in TT and TCG, and the fractional frequency shift of a potential difference;
- the GNSS eccentricity constant F = −4.442807633×10⁻¹⁰;
- proper time along a trajectory through any gravity model;
- the Shapiro delay, Lense–Thirring rates and geodetic precession;
- a Tokyo Skytree test.

The notes proposed putting nearly all of this into `geopotential` as `chronometric`, `height` and `normal` modules ([light and gravity notes](notes/light_and_gravity.md); [gravity notes](notes/gravity_and_gravimetry_frontiers.md)). That would duplicate `geo5d`. The earlier gravity-anomalies plan also assigned the PPN calculators (perihelion advance, limb deflection, round-trip Shapiro, Nordtvedt, PPN limits) to `geo5d`, and that follow-up is still open ([gravity-anomalies report](../gravity-anomalies/report.md)). The 2022–2026 clock and PPN results therefore become test vectors for `geo5d`'s existing and planned functions, not new physica code. `geo5d` still pins `geopotential` 0.1.1, so it needs a dependency bump before it shares the workspace's engine. One overlap is already live: `gravitics::spacetime::light_bending` and `geo5d`'s planned limb deflection are the same physics. When PPN γ arrives, it should arrive in one of them only.

The first four defects share a cause: correct formulas evaluated in a numerically unstable form. Each was found by testing a closed form in its limiting regime (high degree, near the axis, the weak field) rather than at a textbook point. D1 matters most for future users: wider smoothing radii and higher degrees are where GRACE-FO follow-on work is heading, and the floor silently replaces true weights below 10⁻¹⁰.

D2 matters most for the crates' credibility. The README advertises reproductions of published cold-atom sensitivities, but the struct cannot reproduce the one published outdoor gradiometer without a factor-two correction.

D5 to D9 are duplication rather than error. Their numerical gaps (1.3×10⁻⁴, 0.15%, 2.7×10⁻⁴) are conventions far inside every published tolerance, which is exactly why they drift unnoticed without a test that pins them.

Three things look like problems and are not:

- **`plasmoid::relaxation::bessel_j`** applies Simpson's rule to a periodic integrand, which is spectrally accurate. J₀(50) matches Abramowitz & Stegun's 0.05581232766925182 to the last digit, so moving it is a DRY choice only ([unifying_algorithms_checks.py](notes/unifying_algorithms_checks.py)).
- **The 0.8% gap between 1.96 and 1/α∥(1) = 1.976** is a documented NRL convention.
- **PlasmaPy's `Spitzer_resistivity`** is a cross-section estimate 1.43 times NRL's η⊥, not the Spitzer–Härm value. It must not be used to test the crate's resistivity ([plasma frontiers notes](notes/plasma_frontiers_and_kinetic_algorithms.md)).

## Forty-two items in six phases, each pinned to a number

The ranking puts first the items with the hardest-to-fake test: an exact identity, or a number from a primary source opened during the research. Among those, it favours items that join more crates or remove a duplicate, and then the smallest code. Phases follow the dependency order: a kernel lands before its callers. Phase 0 is the ten fixes above, built in the order D1, D2, D5, D4, D3, D8, D9, D6–D7, D10. Each row below names its test value. "Identity" means the test asserts an exact mathematical relation, recomputed by the cited script; "computed" means the script's value is set beside a published one. Items that earlier reports proposed but never built are marked as extensions where the new research supplies the missing test.

## What stays out, and what would bring it in

The user has put three slots on hold, so nothing here is recommended for them:

- **`magnetics::devices`:** nothing in this plan; the off-axis loops and NI coils go to `magnets`.
- **`magnetics::astro`:** the pulsar ⟨B∥⟩ table, the FRB rotation-measure registry, the gravity–magnetism mass-to-flux ratio, and magnetar fields.
- **`magnetics::frontier`.**

Military uses were left out by design: anomaly detection of vessels, military flight trials, and patents assigned to defence agencies. `aeromag` is scoped to survey-aircraft compensation.

Some items stay out until a published test exists:

- ion-acoustic Landau rates (the exact roots differ from the small-k estimate by up to 60%, but there is no external table);
- Mora's TNSA cut-off, which diverges without a stopping time;
- inverse-bremsstrahlung absorption, the inverse Faraday effect, MOKE and whistler dispersion;
- stellarator ε_eff and the 1/ν law;
- the gravitational spin Hall effect;
- WMMHR2025 and LCS-1 coefficients (test values sit in NCEI's zip; downloading is the user's call);
- observatory jerk amplitudes;
- tesseroids, which were not researched.

Record tables (W7-X's 1.8 GJ, WEST's 1337 s, the 42.02 T resistive and 35.6 T superconducting magnets) belong in documentation with their category, not in tests. The radiation-belt bounce period is nearly ready: T(α) has exact limits 1.38017 and 0.74048, and a Boris run gives 17.490 s against the guiding-centre 17.462 s. It should wait only until Schulz & Lanzerotti is read in place of the [rbamlib summary](https://rbamlib.readthedocs.io/dev/models/dip.html) ([unifying_algorithms_checks.py](notes/unifying_algorithms_checks.py)).

The shared integrators are deferred with explicit triggers. The Boris step keeps |v| to 10⁻¹⁵ over 10⁵ steps where RK4 loses 1.39×10⁻³, and Verlet and Yoshida-4 keep Kepler energy bounded ([unifying_algorithms_checks.py](notes/unifying_algorithms_checks.py); [Qin et al. 2013](https://bp-pub.pppl.gov/pub_report/2013/PPPL-4872-abs.html)). But today only one example would call them: Boris for the magnetic term of `physica`'s `gravity_patent`, which uses a semi-implicit Euler step at 1 ms. Build them when a library function in two crates integrates a trajectory. Build a fast multipole or Barnes–Hut code only when a caller has 10⁴ or more sources: at N = 1000, Barnes–Hut at θ = 0.5 cuts work about five times for a 4.5×10⁻³ median error ([unifying_algorithms_checks.py](notes/unifying_algorithms_checks.py)). Guiding-centre variational integrators and Kerr ray tracing wait for a caller in the same way.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "unifying the crates" means. The common ground is not a numerics library but potential theory: Newton, Coulomb and Biot–Savart are one Laplace kernel. A prism Hessian is simultaneously a gravity model, a magnetic model and a demagnetising tensor, and the ring kernel behind the patent rig's electrodes is the same one an off-axis current loop needs. Describing `geopotential` as the potential-field crate is what keeps the code DRY without a ninth crate. A shared integrator or special-function crate earns its cost only when a routine gains callers on both sides of the `metrology` level, and none has one today. The other half of DRY lies outside the workspace: the clock-geodesy and PPN results are best spent as test vectors for `geo5d`, which already owns that physics.

The defects point to a cheap discipline. Every accuracy problem found was a correct formula in an unstable form: a minimal solution recursed forward, nearly equal elliptic integrals subtracted near an axis, a corner sum far from its body, a rational series on the real axis, and an optical index that drops the spatial curvature. A tested-at-the-textbook-point closed form hides all five, while one assertion in each formula's limiting regime (high degree, near axis, far field, real axis, weak field) exposes it. Fast-moving results such as NIF shots, O4 events, lensing H₀ and magnet records change faster than formulas do. They belong in status-labelled registries like `gravitational::hf::REGISTRY`, with the formulas themselves pinned to stable primaries such as the 2022 NIF table, Berti–Cardoso–Will and Zaghloul–Ali.
