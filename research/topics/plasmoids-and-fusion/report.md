# Pinches confine; bremsstrahlung still beats boron

The mainstream physics of Z-pinches, plasma foci and self-organised plasmoids is sound and compact: Bennett's pinch balance, the Pease–Braginskii radiative limit, Lee's plasma-focus circuit, Taylor relaxation, Tuszewski's FRC beta, Sweet–Parker and plasmoid reconnection and the Bosch–Hale reactivities all reproduce their published test values to rounding in three standard-library scripts, so all of it can become verified Rust calculators, and most of the claims built on top fail against those same calculators. The decisive result is that **a thermal p–¹¹B plasma never outruns its own bremsstrahlung**: with the newest (Tentori–Belloni) reactivity the best fusion-to-bremsstrahlung ratio at T_e = T_i is about **0.49**, a positive balance needs electrons held at a third to a half of the ion temperature, holding them there costs **11–36 times the fusion power** in ion–electron coupling, and unreflected synchrotron emission would add **40–400 times** the bremsstrahlung. Direct conversion cannot rescue that, because measured converters reach 48–65% and only on the charged fraction; once X-rays are half the output a plant's conversion efficiency falls to about **0.6**, barely above a steam cycle. What experiments have delivered is modest and real: MagLIF's 2026 record of **2.33×10¹³ D–D neutrons**, FuZE's sheared-flow pinch at more than 10¹⁰ neutrons (company-reported), LPPFusion's 240 keV confined ions at a wall-plug efficiency of 3.3×10⁻⁶ with no boron yield yet, TAE's 30–40 ms beam-driven FRCs at **7.6×10⁻⁵ of the D–T ignition triple product**, and Helion's unquantified D–T neutrons, whose case hinges on an energy-recovery efficiency it has not published for a fusing plasma. Plasma cosmology fails as cosmology; the pinch–gravity analogy is an exact mapping onto Ostriker's isothermal filament with a binding 5.5×10²⁷ times stronger than gravity and no physical coupling; ball lightning cannot be a self-confined plasmoid at its reported energies; and MARAUDER, Lockheed's CFR and the Navy's Pais device left hardware or paperwork but no demonstrated headline number. Nearly sixty formulas with published test vectors are ready for a plasma and fusion crate, listed at the end with the items that need checking before they ship.

*How to read this report.* **[mainstream]** means standard physics or a peer-reviewed measurement nobody disputes; **[contested]** means published but disputed, model-dependent, company-reported or an unmeasured projection (for company items it means no independent peer-reviewed check, not that the claim has been shown false); **[refuted]** means contradicted by later analysis or data, or internally inconsistent by orders of magnitude. Numbers marked *(check)* were recomputed with the stdlib scripts beside the notes, all of whose asserts pass under `python3 -I`: [z_pinch_checks.py](notes/z_pinch_checks.py), [plasmoid_checks.py](notes/plasmoid_checks.py) and [aneutronic_checks.py](notes/aneutronic_checks.py). A few (the Gamow peaks, the solar triple product, the Pais power and the Lawson minimum) come from the companion Coulomb-barrier scripts. They are our inferences, not published claims.

## Bennett's 1934 balance still governs pinches, and radiation caps them near 1.4 MA

A Z-pinch is a plasma column squeezed by the magnetic field of its own current, and its whole equilibrium fits in one line. Bennett's relation, μ₀I²/(8π) = NkT(1+z), with N the ions per metre and T the density-weighted temperature, holds for any pressure and current profile because it is an integral of radial force balance; in practical units T ≈ 3.12 I²/((1+z)N) keV with I in MA and N in 10¹⁸ cm⁻¹ ([Klír 2005, §2.1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0703207)) **[mainstream]**. The check script recovers the coefficient as **3.121** and finds that a 1 MA deuterium pinch at T_e + T_i = 2 keV must hold **1.56×10²⁰ ions per metre** *(check)*. Radiation caps that picture. Above the Pease–Braginskii current, bremsstrahlung exceeds Ohmic heating and an optically thin pinch should collapse; Klír derives I_PB = (π/μ₀)√(48 lnΛ/(σ₀A))·(1+z)/z and about 1.4 MA for deuterium at lnΛ ≈ 10 ([Klír 2005, §2.1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0703207)) **[mainstream]**. Rebuilt from NRL resistivity and bremsstrahlung constants it comes out at **1.37 MA** with the perpendicular Spitzer conductivity, 0.98 MA with the parallel one and 1.94 MA at lnΛ = 20 *(check)*. The perpendicular value is the consistent choice because J ⊥ B in a Z-pinch, and the √lnΛ scaling turns the 1.4 MA into a band rather than a constant. Klír adds that anomalous resistivity, leakage currents, an axial field and opacity shift I_PB substantially, and that fibre pinches expanded rather than collapsed even above polyethylene's 600 kA limit ([Klír 2005](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0703207)).

Stability is the older problem. In ideal MHD the sausage (m = 0) mode grows at roughly v_A/R, with a growth rate that rises without bound in wavenumber until finite-Larmor-radius effects cap it ([Klír 2005, §2.1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0703207)) **[mainstream]**. The notes derive the sharp-boundary (Kruskal–Schwarzschild) dispersion relation for an incompressible column with a surface current, (Γa/v_A)² = [ka·I_m′(ka)/I_m(ka)]·[1 + m²K_m(ka)/(ka·K_m′(ka))]. It gives Γa/v_A = 0.348, 0.668, 1.181 and 2.114 for m = 0 at ka = 0.5, 1, 2 and 5, and 0.481–1.942 for the kink over the same range, while m = 2 stays stable until ka approaches m²; the m = 0 value at ka = 1 has the closed form √(I₁(1)/I₀(1)) = 0.668 *(check)*. That relation is the notes' own derivation, not a fetched source, so it must be checked against Freidberg's *Ideal MHD* before it ships.

### Sheared flow buys thousands of growth times, not yet gain

Shumlak and Hartman predicted in 1995 that axial flow sheared faster than dV_z/dr > 0.1 k V_A stabilises the pure Z-pinch, and ZaP then held a 100 cm by 1 cm column quiescent for more than 700 static growth times, with shear above threshold during the quiet period and near zero afterwards, though the authors note this does not prove causation ([Shumlak et al., PRL 87, 2001](https://plasma2.physics.wisc.edu/pub_files/journal/Shumlak170.pdf)); a 2005 poster reported about 2000 growth times ([APS DPP 2005](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/dpp/2005/bp1/20/)) **[mainstream for the measurements; scaling to gain unproven]**. FuZE turned that stability into neutrons: **(1.25 ± 0.45)×10⁵ per pulse** in 2019 at about 200 kA from a 20% deuterium fill, at 1–2 keV, 10¹⁷ cm⁻³ and a 0.3 cm radius, with emission lasting 5 µs inside a 16 µs quiescent period ([Zhang et al., PRL 122, 135001](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019PhRvL.122m5001Z)). Those numbers sit almost on Bennett's line. A Bennett profile with n₀ = 10²³ m⁻³ and a = 3 mm carries 2.83×10¹⁸ ions per metre, which at 200 kA needs **T_e = T_i = 2.21 keV** against the measured 1–2 keV; the edge field is 13.3 T, v_A ≈ 920 km/s and a/v_A = 3.3 ns, so the quiescent period is about **4900 static growth times**, the same order as ZaP's 700–2000, and the ka = 1 shear threshold is about 92 km/s across the radius *(check)*. In 2024 Thomson scattering found T_e = 1–3 keV on axis, peaking with the neutron pulse ([Zap Energy](https://www.zapenergy.com/updates/2024/04/elevated-electron-temp)), and Ryan et al. bounded any deuteron beam below 7.4 keV in shots giving 4×10⁷ neutrons at 370 kA, so the yield is mostly isotropic and thermonuclear ([arXiv:2408.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05171)) **[mainstream]**.

Zap's later numbers are company-reported **[contested: not yet peer-reviewed]**: more than 5×10⁹ neutrons per shot on FuZE-Q in 2024 ([Zap 2024 review](https://legacy.zapenergy.com/blog/a-look-back-at-2024)), then more than 10¹⁰ neutrons, densities above 7×10²⁴ m⁻³, 1.6 GPa and shear above 200 km/s in 2025, with the hydrogen- and helium-fuelled Century device running 1,080 shots in three hours at 39 kW average input ([Zap 2025 highlights](https://legacy.zapenergy.com/blog/2025-highlights)). FuZE-3's quoted 830 MPa electron pressure is exactly n_e = 5.18×10²⁴ m⁻³ at 1 keV, so the 1.6 GPa total assumes T_i ≈ T_e *(check)* ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/11/251120002836.htm)). Yield climbs steeply with current, from 1.25×10⁵ at 200 kA to about 10⁷ at 400 kA ([APS DPP 2020](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/dpp/2020/cp20/14/)) and 4×10⁷ at 370 kA, but the published data do not pin the exponent, and none of them yet bears on gain.

### MagLIF doubled its own D–D record in 2026

Sandia's Z drives up to 27 MA in about 100 ns, and MagLIF uses it to crush a pre-magnetised, laser-preheated fuel cylinder ([Wikipedia: MagLIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetized_liner_inertial_fusion)) **[mainstream]**. The 2014 debut gave up to 2×10¹² D–D neutrons at 19 MA and 10 T, although Physics World reported about 10¹², so the sources disagree on the exact figure ([Physics World](https://physicsworld.com/a/new-approach-to-fusion-delivers-copious-neutrons/)). Raising the field to 15.9 T, the preheat to 1.2 kJ and the current to 20 MA gave **1.1×10¹³ neutrons** in 2020 at T_i = 3.1 keV, about 2 kJ DT-equivalent ([phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2020-10-neutron-sandia-labs-machine-fusion.html)). The **2026 record is 2.33×10¹³ D–D neutrons**, with 2.11×10¹³ on a sister shot, at T_i = 3.0–3.3 keV, 1.3–1.6 Gbar stagnation pressure and a Lawson parameter χ of 0.1–0.2, from a dielectric-coated beryllium liner at 18.5 MA, 15 T and 2.2 kJ preheat ([Weis et al., Phys. Plasmas 33, 062711](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/pop/article/33/6/062711/3395915/Improving-the-stability-and-performance-of-MagLIF)). Carrying over the 2020 ratio of about 65 DT-equivalent reactions per D–D neutron gives about **4.2 kJ DT-equivalent** for the 2026 shot, a rough number that ignores the temperature change *(check)*, while the 30–50 kJ DT yield on Z remains a projection ([Sandia via newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/record-neutron-numbers-at-sandia-labs-z-machine-fusion-experiments)).

## The plasma focus saturates near 4 MA, and LPPFusion's boron has not yet fused

### Lee's circuit model explains the plateau without exotic physics

A dense plasma focus is a coaxial gun whose current sheath runs down the electrodes and then collapses into a pinch at the end, and its yield scaling is circuit physics. As the sheath advances it adds inductance at dL/dt = 2×10⁻⁷ ln(b/a)·dz/dt; at b/a = 2 and the near-universal 10 cm/µs run-down speed in deuterium that is 1.4×10⁻² H/s, and half of it, the **dynamic resistance DR₀ ≈ 7 mΩ**, acts as a load in series with the bank ([Lee, APL 95, 151503](https://www.plasmafocus.net/IPFS/2009/09PP8APL.pdf)) **[mainstream]**. As capacitance grows the bank impedance √(L₀/C₀) falls towards zero and the peak current saturates at V₀/DR₀, **4.29 MA at 30 kV**, so neutron yield, which scales as E₀² at small bank energies, starts to deteriorate at several hundred kilojoules and approaches E₀^0.8 towards 25 MJ ([Lee 2009](https://www.plasmafocus.net/IPFS/2009/09PP8APL.pdf)). The fitted laws are Y_n = 3.2×10¹¹ I_pinch^4.5 over 0.2–2.4 MA and Y_n = 1.8×10¹⁰ I_peak^3.8 over 0.3–5.7 MA ([Saw & Lee](https://journals.ku.edu.np/kuset/article/view/413)); sub-500 J machines follow E₀^2.1 and I_peak^3.9 ([Singh, Lee & Saw](https://nepjol.info/index.php/KUSET/article/view/63652)), and experimental compilations give about I^3.5 ([Hassan et al., IAEA](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/TE-1713-CD/html/s10_4.htm)). Lee's Table I reproduces to within 1.2% for every row from 0.45 kJ to 4.5 MJ, but **its 45 MJ row is internally inconsistent**, giving 3,946 kA from its own impedances against the 4,209 kA printed, and should not be a test vector *(check)*. A snowplow balance with Lee's mass and current fractions (f_m = 0.1, f_c = 0.7) gives a 9.9 cm/µs run-down for the geometry of LPPFusion's FF-1, matching the 10 cm/µs rule *(check)*. A 2026 preprint argues that suppressing filamentation could beat saturation, but it is unreviewed ([arXiv:2606.27108](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27108)) **[contested]**.

### LPPFusion's measurements are peer-reviewed; its plasmoid model is not established

LPPFusion's FF-1, renamed FF-2B when its electrodes became beryllium in 2019, is a 113 µF, 45 kV bank delivering slightly over 1 MA in 1.8 µs to a 2.8 cm anode inside a 5 cm cathode ([Lerner et al., J. Fusion Energy 42:7, 2023](https://www.lppfusion.com/storage/s10894-023-00345-z.pdf)) **[mainstream]**. Its published results stand as measurements while their interpretation is disputed **[mainstream measurement; interpretation contested]**. In 2012 it reported ions above 150 keV confined for 7–30 ns and up to 1.5×10¹¹ neutrons ([abstract reproduction](https://www.nextbigfuture.com/2012/03/new-cathode-design-for-dense-plasma.html)); the 2017 paper reported a best-shot mean ion energy of **240 ± 20 keV**, a record **2.5×10¹¹ neutrons** from 60 kJ, and a 1.2×10⁻³ cm³ plasmoid at 3×10¹⁹ cm⁻³ or more lasting about 40 ns, with nτT = 3.4×10²⁰ keV s m⁻³ ([Lerner 2023](https://www.lppfusion.com/storage/s10894-023-00345-z.pdf)). The arithmetic holds: 3.5×10¹⁹ cm⁻³ × 40 ns × 240 keV is 3.36×10¹⁴ keV s cm⁻³, and the 2013 ion beam of 120 kA × 3 MV × 5 ns carried 1.8 kJ against the 2 kJ quoted *(check)*. Lee's fit predicts 2.6×10¹⁰ neutrons at 1.1 MA, so **FF-1 ran about ten times above the plasma-focus trend at its current** *(check)*, which supports LPPFusion's claim to outperform other foci. But the record is 0.2 J of fusion from 60 kJ, η = 3.3×10⁻⁶, below the 1.25×10⁻⁵ Speed-2 reached in 2001, and LPPFusion has not beaten its own 2016 yield ([Lerner 2023](https://www.lppfusion.com/storage/s10894-023-00345-z.pdf)). Even the 0.2 J is ambiguous, since 2.5×10¹¹ neutrons correspond to 0.13 J counting only the neutron branch of D–D or 0.29 J counting both *(check)*. The purity result, 6 ± 1 µg of beryllium per shot and z_eff = 1.004 against JET's best of 1.2, is published in the same journal but rests on the company's own analysis **[contested: company claim]**.

The model that is supposed to carry the device to boron is a different matter **[contested]**. Lerner pictures the sheath breaking into filaments that merge and kink into a plasmoid a few tens of microns across, whose decaying field drives an electron beam one way and an ion beam the other. The paper's scaling gives a core field B_c = 4zμ(M/m)B, which for p–¹¹B (z = 3, μ = 6) and a 100 kG cathode field is **13.2 GG**, against the paper's own 10 GG *(check)*. The proposed quantum magnetic field (QMF) effect holds that ion-to-electron energy transfer is suppressed once the ion energy falls below M/m times the electron Landau energy of 11.6 eV per GG; for 300 keV ions the thresholds are **14.1 GG for protons, 3.55 GG for alphas and 1.29 GG for ¹¹B** *(check)*, and the company's 0-D simulations claim ignition with 80% burn-up from a 25-fold reduction in that transfer ([Lerner 2023](https://www.lppfusion.com/storage/s10894-023-00345-z.pdf)). These fields are about 10⁶ T, still roughly 4,400 times below the Schwinger field of 4.414×10⁹ T at which ħω_ce reaches m_ec², an identity that also gives the calculator a free unit check (11.58 eV/GG × 44,140 GG = 511 keV). A 10⁶ T field stores 4.0×10¹⁷ J m⁻³, so a core of 14 µm radius, the minimum radius in the ignition runs, would hold about 4.6 kJ, essentially all of the 4–5 kJ FF-1 delivers to its pinch (computed for this report from the notes' figures): energetically possible, but only if nearly all the pinch energy ends up in the core field. Lerner's own beam-heating condition, I > 5.0/(μ^{1/6}z^{5/6}) MA, needs 4.45 MA for deuterium and 1.48 MA for p–¹¹B *(check)*, both above FF-1's 1.1 MA. The notes found no independent replication of the ion-energy or QMF claims and no peer-reviewed critique.

The I⁴ scaling and the plateau are **[mainstream]**; the claim that impurities and filament disruption cause the plateau, and that it can therefore be beaten, is **[contested]**, because Lee attributes the plateau to the circuit. The boron status is plain fact. LPPFusion's first pinch in nearly pure decaborane formed a plasmoid but no measurable fusion; the boron separated from the hydrogen, plated the anode with Be–B and cracked it in August 2025, and the proposed fix is a slower run-down ([LPPFusion year-end summary, 1 Jan 2026](https://www.lppfusion.com/year-end-lppfusion-summary/)). **No plasma focus has yet measured a p–¹¹B yield.** The best other p–¹¹B shot Lerner cites, HB11's at LFEX, gave 1.4×10¹¹ alphas (66 mJ) for 6.5 MJ of input, an efficiency of 10⁻⁸ ([Lerner 2023](https://www.lppfusion.com/storage/s10894-023-00345-z.pdf)).

### Bostick's 1956 plasmoids are the common ancestor

Bostick's 1956 Physical Review paper (104, 292) projected plasmoids from a plasma gun across a magnetic field at up to 10⁷ cm/s, and up to 2×10⁷ cm/s in an Armour Research Foundation report, which is 100–200 km/s; a perpendicular field halved the speed and stretched the plasmoid along B ([UNT digital library](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc714766/); [DTIC](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD1038383.pdf)) **[mainstream, historical]**. Three later lines descend from it. Lerner credits the Bostick–Nardi model as the origin of the focus-plasmoid picture ([Lerner 2023](https://www.lppfusion.com/storage/s10894-023-00345-z.pdf)), Peratt cited Bostick's discharges as galaxy-like precursors ([Wikipedia: Plasma cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_cosmology)), and MARAUDER's compact toroids belong to the same family of magnetised rings fired from coaxial guns.

## Peratt's galaxies and the pinch–gravity analogy share mathematics, not physics

### Plasma cosmology fails on the microwave background, not on field strength

Peratt's 1986 papers in IEEE Transactions on Plasma Science ran fully three-dimensional electromagnetic particle-in-cell simulations of interacting galaxy-scale current filaments, reported synchrotron emission resembling double radio galaxies and quasars, and over 10⁹–5×10⁹ years of evolution produced elliptical, barred and spiral forms that Peratt compared against H I maps and rotation curves ([OpenAlex record](https://openalex.org/W2000605847)) **[contested]**. The headline scenario has two filaments carrying 10¹⁸ A, 300,000 light years apart, joining in a Z-pinch to form a galaxy with flat rotation curves and no dark matter ([Wikipedia: Plasma cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_cosmology)). Field strength does not sink it: at that separation each filament feels B = 7.0×10⁻¹¹ T (0.7 µG) from its partner and a force of 7.05×10⁷ N/m *(check)*, the same order as observed galactic fields. Plasma cosmology fails as a cosmology for other reasons. Galactic-scale Birkeland currents have not been observed, the charge-neutrality length is tiny compared with cosmological scales, no plasma model reproduces the microwave-background anisotropy spectrum that WMAP and Planck fit, and Alfvén–Klein nucleosynthesis would overproduce X- and gamma rays; Peebles judged the background's isotropy irreconcilable with the model, though his criticism targets Alfvén–Klein cosmology rather than the galaxy runs ([Wikipedia: Plasma cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_cosmology)) **[refuted as a cosmology]**. The label does not extend to each simulation. These are real numerical runs in a peer-reviewed engineering journal, the mainstream response has been neglect rather than a formal rebuttal, and the notes found no rebuttal addressing the 1986 runs specifically. Peratt's petroglyph papers compare rock art with 84 laboratory instability shapes and propose an intense prehistoric Z-pinch aurora ([APS 2005](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/apr/2005/l1/30); [APS DPP 2006](https://archive.aps.org/dpp/2006/gp1/132)) **[contested]**, with no independent replication, and the term Peratt instability has no source in the literature reached.

### A Bennett pinch is an Ostriker filament with gravity 5.5×10²⁷ times too weak

The one rigorous link between pinches and gravity is a mathematical identity. Stodółkiewicz (1963) and Ostriker (1964) independently found the isothermal self-gravitating cylinder, whose critical line mass M_line = 2c_s²/G does not depend on central density and is **16.6 M⊙/pc at 10 K** for molecular gas with μ = 2.3; above it, filaments collapse radially and fragment into star-forming cores ([arXiv:2307.11162](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.11162); [André et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.7762)) **[mainstream]**. A Bennett pinch is the same equilibrium **[mainstream physics; the mapping is the notes' own derivation]**. If all ions drift at one uniform speed u, the magnetic pinch force JB has exactly the 1/r form of cylindrical gravity, and matching the two gives G m_i² ↔ μ₀e²u²/(4π) and c_s² ↔ k(T_e + T_i)/m_i. The critical line mass then maps onto N = 8πk(T_e+T_i)/(μ₀e²u²), which with I = euN is exactly Bennett's relation, and both equilibria share the density profile n ∝ (1 + r²/r₀²)⁻² (the Bennett form appears in [arXiv:2411.06674](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.06674)). The virial forms match as well: 2K + W = 0 gives μ = 2c_s²/G for the cylinder, and twice the integrated pressure equals μ₀I²/(4π) for the pinch, so Gμ² plays the role of μ₀I²/(4π). The check script confirms the 16.7 M⊙/pc line mass and builds an example pinch (u = 300 km/s, 2 keV, N = 2.774×10¹⁸ m⁻¹, I = 133 kA) that satisfies Bennett to 10⁻¹² *(check)*. No published paper stating the correspondence was found.

The magnitudes show why the analogy is only formal. In a 1 MA, 2 keV deuterium pinch the magnetic binding μ₀I²/(4π) exceeds the plasma's gravitational binding G(Nm_D)² by **5.5×10²⁷**; confining the same plasma by its own gravity would need a line mass of 2.87×10²¹ kg/m rather than its actual 5.22×10⁻⁷ kg/m; and two protons attract magnetically as strongly as gravitationally only when their relative drift is **2.70×10⁻¹⁰ m/s** *(check)*. Any claim that pinches couple to gravity is **[refuted]**, though none was found in the sources. Gravity's real role in fusion is the opposite trade. The Sun holds 1.35 keV plasma at around 10³¹ m⁻³ for millions of years, so its n_e kT τ_KH is about 4.4×10⁴⁶ keV s m⁻³, roughly 1.5×10²⁵ times the D–T ignition triple product, which is why it can burn through a weak-interaction first step no laboratory could use (computed from [NASA's Sun fact sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/sunfact.html)) **[mainstream]**. A pinch buys binding 10²⁷ times stronger than gravity's and pays for it with confinement measured in nanoseconds.

## Defence plasmoid programmes left hardware, not their headline numbers

The Air Force compact-toroid programme was real and well documented. Phillips Laboratory ran compact-toroid experiments on the **9.4 MJ Shiva Star** fast capacitor bank at Kirtland AFB from late 1990, with rings of 0.1–1.0 mg ([Peterkin, Degnan et al., DTIC ADA275568](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA275568.pdf)); Degnan et al. (Phys. Fluids B 5, 2938, 1993) formed toroids of 0.5–2 mg in 7–15 µs and accelerated them intact ([record summary](https://ilse.kobv.de/KobvIndexRecord/nat_lic_papers_NLZ219268614)); and an Air Force study set a goal above 1,000 km/s for a nonnuclear test system ([DTIC ADA639263](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA639263.pdf)) **[mainstream]**. The MARAUDER figures that circulate, 1–2 mg toroids accelerated at up to 10¹¹ g, 3,000 km/s projected for 1995 and 10,000 km/s for 2000, and a shot compared to about 5 lb of TNT (9.5 MJ), are projections, and no measured final velocity or later fate of the programme is published after 1995 ([Wikipedia: MARAUDER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MARAUDER)) **[mainstream for the hardware; projections not demonstrated]**. The kinematics show why. A 1 mg ring at 1,000 km/s carries 0.5 MJ and at 10¹¹ g gets there in 0.51 m and 1.02 µs, consistent with a metre-scale gun on a microsecond bank; but 2 mg at 3,000 km/s is **9.0 MJ**, 96% of Shiva Star's 9.4 MJ, so the projection implies near-perfect coupling from capacitor to ring, and 1 mg at 10,000 km/s would be 50 MJ *(check)*. Bostick's 100–200 km/s plasmoids and MARAUDER's rings are the same kind of object, but only the latter had a declared weapons-effects purpose. LLNL's RACE ring accelerator dates to 1991, but no numbers were found for it, and nothing public on DTRA pinch or plasmoid work was retrieved, so no claim is made.

Lockheed's Compact Fusion Reactor is the case of a programme with announcements and no data **[contested; never demonstrated]**. In October 2014 Skunk Works promised a 100 MW D–T unit 2.1 × 3.0 m in size within five years; by 2015 the published concepts weighed 200–2,000 t; the T4 experiment reached only 20 eV and 10¹⁶ m⁻³ at under 1% ionisation; T5 was due online at the end of 2019; and Aviation Week reported in 2023 that the effort had been halted before 2021 ([Wikipedia: Lockheed Martin CFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_Compact_Fusion_Reactor); [The War Zone](https://www.twz.com/29074/skunk-works-exotic-fusion-reactor-program-moves-forward-with-larger-more-powerful-design)). Even the design tables are only roughly self-consistent: T4B's listed density, temperatures and 0.1 T field give β = 0.65 rather than the stated 1, and 781 J of thermal energy rather than 1,170 J, while TX gives β = 0.84 and 43.5 MJ rather than 51 MJ *(check)*. Steven Cowley's objection that doubling the size improves confinement about eightfold went to the heart of a compact design.

The Navy's entry is a paper device **[contested; no data]**. The plasma compression fusion device application, filed on 22 March 2018 and published on 26 September 2019, claims gigawatt-to-terawatt output from a device 0.3–2 m across, and experts said it would need considerable proof ([TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2019-10-patent-plasma-compression-fusion-device.html)). It was never granted: the USPTO mailed two non-final actions in 2020, and the application was abandoned on 14 July 2021 for failure to respond ([Google Patents US20190295733A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20190295733A1/en)). The device was never tested, but its own parameters fail the arithmetic. At the stated 10²⁰ m⁻³ and 20 keV the Bosch–Hale D–D reactivity gives about **200 W** in a 0.3 m sphere, five to nine orders of magnitude below the claim; one coulomb on a 0.15 m sphere means a 4×10¹¹ V/m surface field; and spinning that charge at 10⁴ rad/s makes 4.4 mT, not 10⁶ T *(check)*.

## Self-organised plasmoids obey Taylor's eigenvalues, Tuszewski's beta and a virial cap

### Relaxation reduces to Bessel-function arithmetic

Magnetic helicity K = ∫A·B dV is exactly conserved in ideal MHD and nearly conserved in a turbulent, weakly resistive plasma, while magnetic energy decays much faster. Minimising energy at fixed K gives the linear force-free field ∇×B = λB with W = λK/(2μ₀) ([Woltjer 1958](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.44.6.489)), and Taylor's 1974 hypothesis that real plasmas relax to that state ([Taylor 1974](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.33.1139); [Taylor 1986](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.58.741)) is **[mainstream, with known limits]**. Every quantity a calculator needs then reduces to Bessel functions *(check)*. In the cylindrical Bessel-function model B_z = B₀J₀(μr) and B_θ = B₀J₁(μr), so the pinch parameter is Θ = μa/2 and the reversal parameter F = μa·J₀(μa)/(2J₁(μa)); the field reverses at the first zero of J₀, **μa = 2.4048, Θ = 1.2024**, and the minimum-energy state turns helical at **μa ≈ 3.11**, where Θ = 1.555 and F = −1.545 ([arXiv:1509.07305](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.07305)). A spheromak in a cylindrical flux conserver of radius a and length h has (λa)² = 3.8317² + (πa/h)², giving λa = 4.9550 at h/a = 1 and 4.2686 at h/a = 1.67, the tilt-stability limit quoted for an isolated spheromak ([APS DPP 2008](https://archive.aps.org/dpp/2008/tp6/91)); a spherical spheromak has λa = 4.4934, the root of tan x = x. The best spheromaks, CTX in 1990 and SSPX in 2007, reached triple products of 1.6×10¹⁵ and 1.1×10¹⁷ keV m⁻³ s ([Wurzel & Hsu, Table 7](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.10954)) **[mainstream]**, and gun formation amplifies flux through a kink whose onset matches the Kruskal–Shafranov limit ([arXiv:physics/0304104](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0304104)). Real reversed-field pinches sit away from the pure Bessel-function curve, meaning they are only partly relaxed; the notes did not retrieve a sourced F–Θ dataset.

### FRC beta follows from geometry; stability follows from s

Field-reversed configurations combine radial pressure balance with axial force balance into the profile-independent ⟨β⟩ = 1 − x_s²/2, with x_s the ratio of separatrix radius to wall radius, which Tuszewski calls perhaps the most useful relation in FRC physics; x_s = 0.5 gives **⟨β⟩ = 0.875**, and the typical range 0.4–0.6 gives 0.82–0.92 ([Tuszewski 1988](https://zenodo.org/records/1235740)) **[mainstream]**. Stability is organised by s, roughly the number of ion gyroradii between null and separatrix, bounded by S*x_s²/(4√2) < s < S*x_s/4 with S* = R/ρ_ie, which gives 0.442–1.250 at x_s = 0.5 and S* = 10 *(check)*. As of 1988 experiments had s ≲ 2, kinetic theory predicted the tilt mode becoming observable for s > 3, D–T reactors would need s ~ 20–40, lifetimes exceeded Alfvén times by at most a factor of 100, and flux confinement ran 3–5 times worse than classical Spitzer diffusion; adiabatic flux compression heats as T ∝ B_e^{4/5} with n ∝ B_e^{6/5} but nets only a few per cent heating efficiency on its own ([Tuszewski 1988](https://zenodo.org/records/1235740)). Merging works better. Tri Alpha's C-2 collided two θ-pinch FRCs, amplified the flux more than tenfold and reached T_i + T_e > 0.5 keV with flux confinement approaching classical values ([Binderbauer et al., PRL 105, 045003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20867853)), and MSNW, Helion's forerunner, fired FRCs at about 250 km/s ([APS DPP 2007](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/dpp/2007/to4/14/)), which is 652 eV of directed energy per deuteron, the right size for 0.5 keV after thermalisation *(check)*. TAE's Norm skipped both steps. In 2025 it formed an FRC with neutral beams alone in about 10 ms, with a 0.4 m separatrix, 6 mWb of trapped flux, 300–350 kA and 9 kJ ([Roche et al., Nat. Commun. 16, 3487](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11993635)) **[mainstream: peer-reviewed, company-authored]**. Those figures are consistent with Tuszewski's relation: estimating B_e ≈ μ₀I/(2ℓ) = 0.11 T puts x_s between 0.31 and 0.66 and ⟨β⟩ between 0.78 and 0.95, bracketing the paper's typical value of about 90%, although the check does not measure B_e *(check)*.

### Reconnection settles near 1% of the Alfvén speed once plasmoids form

Reconnection is how plasmoids form, merge and release energy, and its rate is set by the Lundquist number S = μ₀Lv_A/η ([Ji & Daughton 2011](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1109.0756)) **[mainstream]**. Sweet–Parker reconnection runs at S^{−1/2} of the Alfvén speed ([Bhattacharjee et al. 2009](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0906.5599)): 1.8% in MRX (S ≈ 3×10³) but 2×10⁻⁷ in the solar corona (S ≈ 10¹³), which would take about 200 days to release a flare's flux instead of minutes *(check)*. Above a critical S_c of about 10⁴ (Ji and Daughton) to 3×10⁴ (Bhattacharjee et al.), the sheet breaks into a plasmoid chain whose linear growth scales as S^{1/4}/τ_A and whose plasmoid count scales as S^{3/8} ([Loureiro, Schekochihin & Cowley 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0703631)), and the global rate becomes independent of S near S_c^{−1/2} ≈ 0.01, with plasmoid fluxes following an inverse-square distribution ([Uzdensky, Loureiro & Schekochihin 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.3330)). Recomputing S from Spitzer resistivity reproduces Ji and Daughton's table within a factor of 2.5 in every row checked, 3.13×10³ for MRX and 2.3×10¹³ for the corona *(check)*; the spread comes from lnΛ and the choice of parallel or perpendicular resistivity, so a calculator must expose both. The C-2 and Helion merging events are deliberately driven reconnection.

Helion's pulsed scheme merges two FRCs, compresses them magnetically in under a millisecond and recovers the energy inductively as the plasma pushes back on the coils, which Helion likens to regenerative braking ([Helion blog](https://www.helionenergy.com/blog/how-to-make-fusion-electricity-without-ignition)). Polaris began operating at the end of 2024, switched to D–T in January 2026 and reported 150 M°C, or 12.9 keV, without saying whether that is the ion or electron temperature ([Helion newsroom](https://www.helionenergy.com/newsroom/helion-achieves-new-fusion-energy-milestones)). The physics question that decides Helion is recovery efficiency, and it has not been published for a fusing plasma. A pulsed model that also credits direct conversion of the charged products gives a breakeven Q_sci = (1 − η_rec)/(η_dc f_ch), from 0.058 at 95% recovery to 0.47 at 60% *(check)*. The D–³He fuel releases 18.353 MeV as a 3.69 MeV alpha and a 14.66 MeV proton *(check)*, and Helion's figure of about 5% of the energy in fast neutrons ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helion_Energy)) lies between the 0.3–1.3% the notes compute from D–D side reactions alone and the 2–7% if bred tritium burns. ### Ball lightning cannot be a self-confined plasmoid

Ball lightning is usually 10–20 cm across and lasts from a second to over a minute ([Wikipedia: Ball lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_lightning)), and energies inferred from eyewitness reports are of order 10–100 kJ, a few higher **[contested estimates]** ([arXiv:1102.2829](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1102.2829)). The virial theorem rules out a self-confined magnetised plasma holding that: confinement by self-field alone violates the conservation laws, and including air pressure bounds the stored energy far below some reported values ([Finkelstein & Rubinstein, Phys. Rev. 135, A390](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRev.135.A390)) **[mainstream; refutes the energetic self-confined plasmoid]**. The cap is 3p₀V = **0.304 MJ m⁻³**, or 1.27 kJ for a 20 cm ball, atmospheric pressure can hold at most 0.505 T, and a 20 cm ball at 1–10 MJ m⁻³ would exceed the cap 3–33 times *(check)*. A minority holds that the bound does not apply to an open, field-fed ball ([arXiv:1007.4377](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1007.4377v52)) **[contested minority]**. Chemistry fits easily: silicon oxidation stores 7.55×10¹⁰ J per m³ of solid, so 3.9 MJ m⁻³ needs only about 120 g of silicon per cubic metre *(check)*. The laboratory analogues agree. Paiva et al. made 1–4 cm luminous balls from silicon arcs that lasted up to 8 s, confirming the Abrahamson–Dinniss oxidation picture ([PRL 98, 048501](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007PhRvL..98d8501P); [Physics Today](https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/ball-lightning-in-the-lab)), and the 2012 Qinghai spectrum showed silicon, iron and calcium lines from soil ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_lightning)) **[mainstream]**. SAFIRE/Aureon's claims of fourteen times more energy out than in, and of thorium transmuted into stable elements, come from a forum post and a newsletter with no peer-reviewed data ([Electrogenesis Substack](https://electrogenesis.substack.com/p/aureon-energy-the-safire-project)) **[contested; no data]**; the test they need is closed-boundary calorimetry with stated errors.

## Thermal p–¹¹B never outruns its own bremsstrahlung

Aneutronic fuels are low-neutron, not neutron-free, and the legal definition is at most 1% of the energy in neutrons (New Jersey A2731). In p–¹¹B the main source, ¹¹B(α,n)¹⁴N, occurs in at least 0.1% of reactions but its neutrons carry under 0.2% of the energy, ¹¹B(p,γ) branches at about 10⁻⁴ with 4–16 MeV gammas ([Wikipedia: Aneutronic fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aneutronic_fusion)), and ¹¹B(p,n)¹¹C, with a 2.765 MeV threshold, branches at about 10⁻⁵ ([Wang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00241v1)) **[mainstream]**. D–³He is dirtier than its reputation. Across 50–100 keV and deuterium fractions of 25–50%, D–D side reactions put 0.34–1.31% of the power into 2.45 MeV neutrons, rising to 2.0–7.3% if every bred triton burns (computed from Bosch–Hale fits) *(check)*, so Wikipedia's unsourced figure of several percent **[contested]** holds only when the tritium burns, and pumping it out or running ³He-rich keeps D–³He under the 1% definition. The Gamow energies show why boron is the aneutronic fuel worth trying: 22,601 keV for p–¹¹B from atomic masses (22,589 keV with Nevins–Swain's nuclear masses), 23,625 keV for ³He–³He and 7,607 keV for p–⁶Li, so ³He–³He faces the same barrier as p–¹¹B without boron's 0.6 MeV resonance *(check)*. At 300 keV the p–¹¹B Gamow peak sits near 800 keV with a width of about 1.1 MeV *(check)*, which puts the resonance region in control of the reactivity. Wikipedia's ideal ignition temperatures (D–T 13.6, D–³He 58, p–⁶Li 66 and p–¹¹B 123 keV) carry no per-value source **[mainstream, unsourced per value]**, and the p–¹¹B entry cannot be reproduced with the relativistic bremsstrahlung below.

### Bremsstrahlung, synchrotron and ion–electron coupling form the wall

Bremsstrahlung from a p–¹¹B plasma is relativistic at the temperatures that matter. Putvinski et al.'s fit, better than 1% against QED, is P_Brem/W₀ = 8.511 t^{1/2}[Z_eff(1 + 1.78t^{1.34}) + 2.12t(1 + 1.1t + t² − 1.25t^{2.5})] with t = T_e/m_ec² and W₀ = αr_e²m_ec³n_e² = 1.422×10⁻³⁶n_e² W m³, where the bracket structure is reconstructed from garbled OCR ([Putvinski et al. 2019](https://tae.com/wp-content/uploads/fliphtml5/139/files/basic-html/page3.html)) **[mainstream]**. The checks confirm that 8.511 = (16/3)√(2π/3)(2√3/π), that the low-temperature limit of 5.355×10⁻³⁷Z_eff n_e²√T_keV W m⁻³ matches the NRL Formulary's 5.344×10⁻³⁷ to 0.2%, and that the relativistic enhancement at Z_eff = 3 is ×1.16, ×1.63 and ×2.56 at 50, 150 and 300 keV *(check)*. Rider's form as quoted by Wang et al. agrees with Putvinski's to 1.4% at Z_eff = 3 but carries a prefactor 3.4% below NRL's that the notes could not trace ([Wang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00241v1)). With that loss and a boron fraction n_B/(n_p + n_B) of 0.15, the fusion-to-bremsstrahlung ratio is, as Nevins–Swain / Tentori–Belloni *(check)*:

Their conclusion that bremsstrahlung does not preclude p–¹¹B ignition, given near-perfect confinement ([Putvinski et al. 2019](https://tae.com/wp-content/uploads/fliphtml5/139/files/basic-html/page4.html)), is **[contested: the TAE position]** but consistent with this table. Wang et al. find a self-consistent T_e window of 0.17–0.38 MeV in which fusion leads ([Wang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00241v1)), and Ahmad et al. find net energy for T_i = 190–330 keV at T_e = 0.5T_i with Tentori–Belloni but none with Nevins–Swain, with some internally inconsistent numbers ([Ahmad et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nuclear-engineering/articles/10.3389/fnuen.2026.1714531/full)) **[mainstream]**. One conflict stays open. Ochs, Kolmes, Glasser and Fisch report that fusion exceeds bremsstrahlung at T_e = T_i above 150 keV for f_b = 0.1 ([arXiv:2511.10885](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10885v1)), about a factor of 2 above this computation, perhaps from a different f_b definition, their kinetic enhancement or the Svensson bremsstrahlung fit; a calculator should carry it as a known failing comparison until it is resolved.

Cold electrons have a price, and it is Rider's result **[mainstream]**. Holding T_e well below T_i means ions continuously heat electrons at P_ie = (3/2)ν_ei(T_i − T_e), and that power must be recirculated. Liu et al. re-ran Rider's case (T_i = 300 keV, T_e = 23.3 keV) and found P_ie/P_fus = 36 with P_brem/P_fus = 0.5 using Rider's cross sections, and 19.5 with 0.27 using the 2016 ones; for ENN's scenario at T_i = 150 keV and T_i/T_e = 4, P_ie/P_fus = 11, and in every case the recirculating power far exceeds the fusion power ([Liu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13260v1); [Rider 1995](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/11412)). The notes could not reproduce Liu's bremsstrahlung ratios (0.30 and 0.23 against 0.5 and 0.27) because the composition is not stated. Synchrotron emission makes it worse for any magnetic scheme. The optically thin Larmor loss, 6.21×10⁻¹⁷B²n_eT_e W m⁻³, is **41 times bremsstrahlung at β = 1** for T_i = 300, T_e = 150 keV, 82 times at β = 0.5 and 410 times at β = 0.1, and β = 1 at n_e = 10²⁰ m⁻³ already needs 3.7 T *(check)*. Magnetic p–¹¹B therefore needs more than a hundredfold synchrotron suppression by wall reflection and opacity, or very high β, which is the FRC's selling point. For inertial p–¹¹B, Ochs et al. put breakeven at n_eR ≳ 3×10²⁵ cm⁻², about 100 g/cm², roughly 100 times today's areal density at 1,000 times the pressure, with an optimum Q_sci ≈ 2 near 160 keV ([Ochs et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10885v1)) **[mainstream]**.

### Laser-driven boron sits 10⁶ below plant gain, and the avalanche is gone

The laser yields are real and small **[mainstream]**. LFEX's 1.4 kJ, 2.6 ps pulse on a pitcher–catcher target made protons above 10¹³ per steradian at 7% conversion and **5×10⁹ alphas per steradian**, described as preliminary ([Margarone et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphy.2020.00343/full)), and the series runs from Belyaev's corrected 10⁵ α/sr in 2005 through Labaune's 10⁷ in 2013 to Giuffrida's 10¹⁰ α/sr per shot at PALS in 2020 and 1.7×10⁹ α/sr from 550 J on decaborane in 2024 ([arXiv:2407.06729](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2407.06729)). Converted to fusion energy per laser energy, assuming isotropic emission, which flatters forward-peaked beam fusion, those give 2.1×10⁻⁵ (Margarone), 1.8×10⁻⁵ (PALS 2024) and about 7.7×10⁻⁵ (Giuffrida): **10⁶–10⁷ short of the gain of about 100 a plant needs** *(check)*. A 2023 preprint reports 10¹⁰ α/sr from boron-doped foam with a yield per proton up to four orders of magnitude above beam–target expectation ([arXiv:2308.10878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10878)) **[contested: preprint]**.

The avalanche that was meant to bridge the gap, alphas knocking protons into further reactions (Hora et al. 2017), fails on two counts **[contested → refuted for present experiments]**. Belloni et al. found an avalanche time of almost 1 µs on the 675 keV resonance in PALS-like plasmas, ten times longer than claimed ([search summary](https://www.frontiersin.org/researchtopic/11751)), and Belloni's kinetic model gives a multiplication factor k∞ of order 10⁻² even at the most favourable densities, where about 1 is needed ([arXiv:2012.14533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14533)). A study supported by HB11 itself, with Margarone, Batani and Belloni among its authors, attributes the yields to beam fusion with higher cross sections rather than an avalanche and concludes that "Beam fusion reactions do not scale to net gain" ([Mehlhorn et al.](https://hb11.energy/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Study-of-Proton-Boron-Fusion-Burn-Driven-by-Short-Pulse-Lasers-by-T-Mehlhorn.pdf)). Its thick-target yield of 6.56×10⁻⁵ reactions per 1 MeV proton in boron nitride caps beam fusion at E_fus/E_p = 5.7×10⁻⁴, so even a perfect proton driver cannot break even, and gain has to come from thermonuclear burn of compressed fuel at Ochs's roughly 100 g/cm² *(check)*. The same study's plant, 280 MJ pulses at 5 Hz from a 2 MJ laser at 7% efficiency with gain 140 and 560 MWe gross, gives Q_eng = 3.92 against the published 4, needs gain 35.7 merely to reach Q_eng = 1, and burns 3.68 mg of ¹¹B per pulse against the published 3.525 mg *(check)*.

### Direct conversion escapes Carnot only for the charged fraction

Measured electrostatic direct conversion reached **65% peak and 50% minimum on beams and 48% on the TMX plasma** ([Wikipedia: Direct energy conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_energy_conversion)), with Barr and Moir running single- and two-stage venetian-blind converters at up to 100 kV and 6 kW for about 80 hours ([Fusion Sci. Technol.](https://wx1.ans.org/pubs/journals/fst/a_20820)) **[mainstream]**, and Oliphant, Ribe and Coultas's magnetic cycle converts 62% of alpha energy to work **[mainstream]**. Everything higher is projection **[contested]**: venetian blinds at 60–70%, periodic electrostatic focusing at up to 90% for 600 keV ions, a traveling-wave direct converter (TWDEC) at 75% for the 14.7 MeV D–³He protons of the ARTEMIS design and up to 90% in Rostoker, Binderbauer and Monkhorst's proposal, and TAE's inverse cyclotron converter at 90% ([Wikipedia: Direct energy conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_energy_conversion)). Momota, Miley and Nadler estimate about 60% overall on an inertial-electrostatic core ([NIFS](https://www.nifs.ac.jp/report/nifs641.html)), and the Kobe TWDEC experiments report gains from dual-frequency modulation without figures ([Shibata et al.](https://www.jspf.or.jp/PFR/PFR_articles/pfr2019/pfr2019_14-2405027.html)). No measured TWDEC or X-ray converter efficiency was found, and LPPFusion's 81% photoelectric X-ray converter is a patent claim against Quimby's 1977 estimate that hundreds or thousands of layers are needed **[contested]**.

A simple model explains where the measured numbers sit. An ion flux with a Maxwellian-flux spread, f(E) ∝ (E−s)e^{−(E−s)} with s the sheath drop, landing on N optimally biased collectors returns at best 42% with one stage, 59% with two, 74% with four and 82% with eight, and a sheath drop of s = 3T raises those to 64–93% *(check)*. The one-stage case has the analytic optimum V* = ((1+√5)/2)·T and η = 0.420, and the measured 48–65% are what this ideal predicts once secondary electrons and grid losses are added. Direct conversion is not Carnot-limited because a directed ion flux carries little entropy; its limits are the energy spread against the number of stages, space charge, secondary electrons, grid interception and the inverter, and a monoenergetic beam with one matched collector could in principle approach 100%. Bremsstrahlung is the exception, since broadband X-rays must pass through heat, which is Carnot-limited, or through layered photoelectric stacks.

That exception decides the plant arithmetic. Magnetic fusion's engineering gain is Q_eng = η_cη_d(Q_sci + 1), so breakeven needs Q_sci = 1/(η_cη_d) − 1: 2.57 with a 0.4 steam cycle and 0.7-efficient heating, 4.0 at 0.4/0.5, 1.38 at 0.6/0.7 and 0.39 at 0.8/0.9 *(check)*, while Entler's form with η_heat = 0.7, f_recirc = 0.2 and η_elec = 0.4 gives the textbook Q ≈ 22 ([Wikipedia: Fusion energy gain factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_energy_gain_factor)) **[mainstream]**. Inertial fusion, which does not recover its driver, has Q_eng = η_cη_dG. NIF's 3.15 MJ from 2.05 MJ in December 2022 drew over 400 MJ from the wall, so the fusion yield was 0.79% of the wall-plug energy and Q_eng ≈ 0.003 at a 0.4 conversion; the April 2025 record, 8.6 MJ from 2.08 MJ (Q_sci = 4.13), gives about 0.009 if the wall-plug draw was similar *(check)* ([LLNL](https://lmf.llnl.gov/science/achieving-fusion-ignition)). For p–¹¹B, if a fraction f_x of the output is X-rays converted thermally at 0.4 and the charged particles are converted directly at 0.8, the plant conversion efficiency is η_c = 0.8 − 0.4f_x, **0.60 at f_x = 0.5 and 0.48 at f_x = 0.8** *(check)*. Because the radiation wall above guarantees P_br ≳ P_fus, f_x ≥ 0.5 is the realistic case, and most of direct conversion's advantage over steam disappears.

## Conclusion

The most useful reframing is that aneutronic fusion is less a temperature problem than an accounting problem with three ratios: P_fus/P_br at T_e = T_i (at most about 0.49), P_ie/P_fus once the electrons are held cold (11–36), and the round-trip recovery efficiency, which sets the breakeven Q_sci quadratically (0.108 at 95%, 0.56 at 80%). Every concept here is a bet on one of them. TAE bets on confinement good enough to tolerate the first two, Helion on the third with a fuel that still makes some neutrons, and LPPFusion on a QMF effect that would change the second, and none of the three ratios has been measured on a fusing plasma. That makes the next decisive numbers specific and checkable: Helion's measured recovery on Polaris pulses with plasma, TAE's energy-confinement scaling with T_e, LPPFusion's first boron yield, and a resolution of the factor-of-two disagreement with Ochs et al. Until then the defensible engineering case for direct conversion is modest. It converts the charged fraction at 50–65% today, and with half the output in X-rays a p–¹¹B plant converts at about 0.6, a real gain over steam that is far too small to offset the reactivity deficit.

The other strands converge on a method. The mainstream relations are compact enough that most contested claims can be tested with arithmetic their own numbers supply: MARAUDER's projection needs 96% of its bank, Lockheed's tables give β = 0.65 where they state 1, Trenta's maxima need 10.4 T where 8 T was reported, the Navy device makes about 200 W, and ball lightning overshoots its virial cap up to 33-fold. Building each claim function so its test returns the ratio to the mainstream bound turns this report into regression tests. The pinch–gravity identity gives the repository one honest bridge between plasma physics and gravity: a solver for Ostriker filaments is a solver for Bennett pinches, which makes a strong unit test and is the only sense in which pinches have anything to do with gravity.

## What goes into the crates

Neither the `fusion` module planned in the Coulomb-barrier work nor anything here has been built yet, so put both into one new crate, working name `plasmatics` to mirror `gravitics`, and leave `gravitics` for gravity and the vacuum. Reactivities, pinches, reconnection and converters are mainstream plasma physics rather than gravity control; this set adds nearly sixty functions, which would triple `gravitics`' 536 lines; the plasma functions need the reactivities for P_fus/P_br, neutron fractions and DT-equivalence; and keeping reactivities in `gravitics` would make a mainstream crate depend on a claims crate. The new crate takes `C`, `G`, `HBAR` and `MU_0` from `gwsens`, as `gravitics` does, and adds e, m_e, m_p, ε₀, the atomic mass unit, 931.49410242 MeV/u and the NIST atomic masses listed in the Q-value table. The `strong_field` and `spacetime` additions stay in `gravitics`, together with the Pais field checks that reuse `hfgw::surface_field`. If a single crate is preferred, the same modules drop into `gravitics` unchanged. The numerical helpers, J₀ and J₁, I_m and K_m, bisection, Simpson integration and a coordinate search, are a few dozen lines each in the scripts and need no dependency. Follow the `gravitics` pattern for claims: each claim function's doc comment opens with its label and the bound it is tested against, and its test asserts both the published value and the ratio to the mainstream function.
