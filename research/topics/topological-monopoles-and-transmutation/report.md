# Monopole mathematics holds; the talk's laboratory signatures are unverified

Bob Greenyer's TeslaTech 2026 talk, "The Power and Signature of the Topological Monopole", rests on real physics wherever it can be written as an equation. Dirac's condition fixes the magnetic charge at g_D = h/(µ₀e) = **3.291×10⁻⁹ A·m**, whose flux h/e is exactly two superconducting flux quanta. Its field is 3.29×10¹⁴ T at 1 fm. The field energy outside a grand-unified core is M_X/(8α) = 17 M_X, which gives **1.7×10¹⁷ GeV** for M_X = 10¹⁶ GeV, inside the Particle Data Group's 10¹⁷–10¹⁸ GeV. Callan–Rubakov catalysis of proton decay is mainstream theory, with σβ ~ 10⁻²⁷ cm², but it has never been observed. IceCube limits the monopole flux to 2.0×10⁻¹⁹ cm⁻² s⁻¹ sr⁻¹, and matter holds fewer than about 10⁻²⁹ monopoles per nucleon. The analogue monopoles the talk cites are real and were imaged: Dirac strings in spinor condensates (2014), spin-ice charges measured at 5 µ_B/Å, and skyrmion hedgehogs. They carry no magnetic charge in B. Toroidal dipoles and anapoles are a third multipole family. The ¹³³Cs anapole was measured at κ = 0.127 ± 0.019, and the Aharonov–Bohm phase of a flux quantum is exactly π.

Contested items are few: Huang et al.'s ²²Ne from cavitation (published with an editor's note), the 1994 carbon-arc iron (natural isotope ratios, leaning to contamination), Lochak's leptonic monopole, monopole–nucleus binding theory (which its own reviewer calls "primitive"), Puthoff's potentials-only patent and Santilli's magnecules. Several claims fail when computed:

- **The 10¹⁵ T threshold.** It does sit at the QCD field scale (eB = 3.0 m_π²), but lattice QCD keeps cold matter confined at 1.52×10¹⁷ T, 150 times higher.
- **Magnetic charge from currents.** ∇·B = 0 holds for any current, however fractal.
- **16 × 938 MeV per oxygen.** This exceeds the ¹⁶O atom's whole mass-energy, 14,899.2 MeV, by 0.76%. The talk's "166× D–D" matches no convention, which give 157×, 257× or 1028×.
- **The flying craft.** A perfect photon rocket burning all 950 kg of oxygen on a 5 t craft reaches 0.208 c, not eight times light speed. The user's audit simulator exceeds the photon thrust limit 952-fold in vacuum.
- **The other headline figures.** Relic neutrinos are under 3.1% of the baryons, not four to five times them. Srinivasan's 4 t of iron a day would need 0.87–4.1 TW from an 11 MW furnace. Wendt and Irion's tungsten helium was refuted in 1924. A tornado gives water 1.2% of one hydrogen bond's energy.

Everything else that the talk offers as a laboratory signature of the monopole is **unverified**: Urutskoev's foils and tracks, the ⁵⁷Fe shifts, Klimov's decay suppression, Adamenko's lead, Papp's engine, Hutchison's samples, Solin, Matsumoto and Kladov. In each case no independent measurement exists, and each section below says which measurement would settle it. The ⁵⁷Fe shift, for one, is the size of a 6–7 K temperature drift. The user's audit has five errors in its monopole mathematics: µ₀, a sign, Λ, its regime thresholds and the "Kramer" citation. Four of its five nuclear Q-values are wrong, and its three field models are not in the papers it cites. About 120 calculators survive with published or derived test values. They need two new library modules in `plasmoid` (`nuclear`, `cavitation`) and three in `magnetics` (`monopole`, which the user chose, `toroidal` and `mossbauer`). Every number below comes from the seven check scripts in `notes/`, all rerun under `python3 -I` on 2026-10-26 and passing.

### Merges, so each formula is written once

1. **Q-values and specific energy, in `plasmoid::nuclear`.** #20 put `reaction_q` and `specific_energy` in `plasmoid::fusion`, and #24 put `catalysis_yield` there too. All three are AME2020 table lookups, so `nuclear` owns them, and `fusion` keeps only reaction rates. The H₂/O₂ comparison in #20 uses the same NIST ΔfH(H₂O) as #26's heating values in `conversion`; write that constant once.
2. **The Dirac charge, field and energy gain, in `magnetics::monopole`.** #24's `dirac_field` and #25's `monopole_field` are one function, and so are the two `monopole_energy_gain` entries. Their argument orders differ ((n, b, l) against (b, l, n)), so pick one. `dirac_charge` comes from the magnetics report's frontier specification. `dirac_field`, `dirac_radius_for_field`, `monopole_field_energy` and `pole_moment_energy` call it rather than restating h/(µ₀e). `DIRAC_FLUX` belongs in `metrology` with the other flux constants, and `monopole` uses it from there.
3. **The decay law, in `metrology::decay`.** `plasmoid::nuclear`'s `activity` calls it. This adds the first functions to a crate that has so far held only constants. The cross-domain report warned that numerics in `metrology` would make a numerics fix bump the constants crate. So keep it to the one-line law. #25's `metrology::composition` helpers (`burnout_isotope_fraction`, `steel_fingerprint`, `transmutation_energy_bound`) have no caller outside `plasmoid` and examples. They fit better beside `plasmoid::nuclear`'s abundance table. `transmutation_energy_bound(n, ΔE) = nΔE` is the inverse of `max_atoms(E, Q) = E/|Q|`, so one of the two is enough.
4. **The Gamow-peak code, shared in `plasmoid::fusion`.** #22's `log10_thermal_tunnelling`, and #26's `maxwell_reactivity_from_s` and `fusion_per_collapse`, all use `gamow_energy` and `gamow_peak`; none restates them.
5. **`spin_ice_charge` and `equipartition_field`: extend, don't duplicate.** Both exist only as specification rows in the magnetics report (`frontier` and `astro`), not as code. #24 and the monopole note call them "existing", which is true of the specification only. `spin_ice_charge` is built once, in `magnetics::monopole`, from that row, with Bramwell's measured value added as a second test. The energy-density column of `zeeman_field` is `equipartition_field`'s √(2µ₀u), the inverse of the existing `magnets::stored_energy`. Implement it once, beside `stored_energy` in `magnets`, and have the `astro` specification point there when the hold lifts.
6. **`charge_cluster` is `charged_sphere` extended.** #21 proposes a new `charge_cluster` beside `charged_sphere`, and #22 says reuse `charged_sphere`. The existing function returns the stored energy kQ²/2R of charge on a conducting surface. The ball-lightning note's 2.77 J uses the uniform cloud's 3kQ²/5R. So add the uniform case to `charged_sphere`, and do not add a second function. The transmutation note describes the existing function as returning the (3/5) form, which it does not.
7. **`actuator_disk_power` is the frontier specification's `reactionless_power`.** Both are F^(3/2)/√(2ρA). Build it once, in `gravitics::propulsion`, and have the specification point there.

## Conclusion

The talk names real physics and then asks it to do what it cannot. The Dirac charge, the GUT mass, catalysis, toroidal radiation and the Aharonov–Bohm phase all compute exactly, and they belong in the crates as tested functions. The mechanism that would tie them to the laboratory needs three things standard physics excludes: magnetic charge from currents, a field threshold for catalysis, and bond failure in strong fields. Strong fields bind atoms tighter, lattice QCD keeps matter confined 150 times above 10¹⁵ T, and catalysis needs a GUT core, not a field strength. What remains are anomaly reports, each with one decisive measurement the notes name: an isotope ratio, a calorimetric balance, a blinded film or Mössbauer run, an etched track stack, an activity step. None has been made independently. Building the calculators makes each test a single function call, with the claim's figure computed beside the standard one.
