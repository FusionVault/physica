# One harmonic engine joins gravity and magnetism

Magnetics adds about 120 calculators to the crate family, and each one already has a published test value: the four standard-library check scripts behind this report run **135 checks of published values and exact identities, and all of them pass** when rerun (38 geomagnetic, 49 applied-magnetics, 35 MHD and 13 gravity–magnetism checks). The centrepiece needs no new numerical code. Give `geopotential`'s existing gravity engine GM = a², R = a = 6,371,200 m and C̄ₙₘ = gₙᵐ/√(2n+1), and it reproduces **every WMM2025 report test value within the published rounding (worst 0.050 nT, 0.005°)**. It also matches NOAA's 100-row high-precision file to 0.0008 nT. That one mapping is the case for the planned `geopotential::Field` trait. Gravity (∇V), the main magnetic field (−∇V) and the gravitomagnetic field of a spinning Earth (outside the source, a dipole with a scalar potential) become three implementors of one interface; a draft of the trait is already uncommitted in `crates/geopotential/src/field.rs`. Around that core, MHD, magnet engineering and space weather are mostly one-line closed forms tied to published numbers: PlasmaPy's Alfvén speed of 43,173.870 m/s, ITER's q95 = 3.0, KIT's duct pressure gradient of 0.0678, Berry and Geim's 16.5 T frog, and the 815 kN/m line load on the CFS/MIT magnet. The fringe-but-calculable claims each miss a bound by four to nine orders of magnitude: Blackett's rotating magnets, Cabrera's monopole, the "motionless" generator, and Roshchin and Godin's rotating converter. The cross-domain analogies belong in the umbrella crate `physica`, the only crate that can call all seven. There, tests can assert identities such as the factor of 2 that spin-2 gravity puts into every gravitomagnetic analogue, and the constant √(4πε₀G), shared by Blackett's hypothesis, the extremal black hole and the weak-gravity conjecture. Each crate gets examples that reproduce a number someone acts on: WMM2025 for navigation and directional drilling, NERC's 8 V/km storm benchmark, Hulse–Taylor's orbital decay, JET's 69 MJ, and SPARC's design point. The remaining risks are small and specific: a units clash in the draft trait, unverified Solov'ev coefficients, and about thirty published-value conflicts and gaps, all flagged in the last section.

## The gravity engine already computes Earth's magnetic field

The World Magnetic Model and the IGRF use the same potential that geodesy uses: V = a Σₙ (a/r)ⁿ⁺¹ Σₘ (gₙᵐ cos mλ + hₙᵐ sin mλ) P̆ₙᵐ(sin φ′). It has a reference radius a = 6,371,200 m, Schmidt semi-normalised Legendre functions, geocentric coordinates and linear secular variation ([Chulliat et al. 2025, WMM2025 Technical Report](https://doi.org/10.25923/prbc-s316); [Beggan et al. 2026, IGRF-14](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40623-025-02360-0)). The `geopotential` crate stores 4π fully normalised coefficients. The two normalisations differ by exactly √(2n+1), and **neither includes the Condon–Shortley phase**, so no (−1)ᵐ correction is needed. A library that does include that phase, as many Abramowitz-and-Stegun-style maths libraries do, would flip the sign of every odd-m term. The check script ran a Schmidt recursion and the crate-style 4π recursion side by side. They agree to **6×10⁻¹¹ nT**, and the engine path reproduces all 12 points × 15 elements of the report's Table 6, rates included. The mapping is the whole adapter:

| Gravity engine (`geopotential`) | Magnetic main field |
|---|---|
| `gm`, `radius` | a² (units become nT·m², which the engine ignores), a = 6,371,200 m |
| C̄₀₀; C̄ₙₘ, S̄ₙₘ | 0; gₙᵐ/√(2n+1), hₙᵐ/√(2n+1) |
| `partials(...).acceleration_neu(r, φ′)` = (N, E, U) | X′ = −N, Y′ = −E, Z′ (down) = +U, because B = −∇V |
| the same with ġ, ḣ as coefficients | rates Ẋ′, Ẏ′, Ż′ (synthesis is linear) |
| `second_partials` | magnetic gradient tensor −∂²V/∂xᵢ∂xⱼ |

After that come the geodetic-to-geocentric conversion (WGS 84), a rotation through ψ = φ′ − φ, and closed forms for H, F, D, I and their rates. The draft `geopotential::geocentric` helper and the in-progress `magnetics::main_field::Model::elements` implement exactly this. The time model also exists already. ICGEM's `TimeVariable` with `TermKind::Trend` terms and `valid` ranges expresses WMM's g(t) = g(t₀) + (t − t₀)ġ. It also expresses IGRF's piecewise-linear 5-year segments, whose ġ = [g(T + 5) − g(T)]/5 before 2025 and whose predictive secular variation runs from 2025 to 2030 ([Beggan et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40623-025-02360-0)). The in-progress `Model::at` loop duplicates that machinery. The duplication is harmless for WMM, but IGRF's 26 epochs should go through `TimeVariable` rather than a second time model.

The published targets are unusually clean. Table 6 is computed in double precision, with heights above the ellipsoid; the report warns that single precision can differ by 0.1 nT ([WMM2025 Technical Report](https://doi.org/10.25923/prbc-s316)). The worst residuals per element are **0.048 nT (X), 0.044 (Y), 0.049 (Z), 0.050 (H) and 0.045 (F), with 0.005° in D, I and grid variation**, all inside the one-decimal rounding. The 100-row file, `WMM2025_TestValues.txt`, which NCEI distributes ([NCEI WMM](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/world-magnetic-model)), matches to **0.00072 nT**. That residual is a 10⁻⁸ relative offset already present at 2025.0. It is not a time-handling error, and its source in NOAA's generator is unknown, so a 10⁻³ nT tolerance is the honest choice. IGRF-14 publishes no point-value table. Its checkable statements are structural, and all reproduce. The surface minimum of the South Atlantic Anomaly is **22,071.4 nT at 26.2231°S, 60.0285°W**, against the published 22,071 nT at 26.22°S, 60.03°W. The computed dip poles lie within 0.41 km of Table 3. The geomagnetic pole sits at 80.85°N, 72.76°W ([Beggan et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40623-025-02360-0)). The WMM eccentric dipole comes out at 604.6 km, 22.756°N, 135.336°E, against the published 605 km, 22.76°N, 135.34°E. From the IGRF-14 coefficients the dipole moment is **8.321×10²² A m² in 1900, 7.708 in 2020, 7.690 in 2025 and a forecast 7.669 in 2030**. The decline runs at 4.8% per century now and has averaged 6.1% per century since 1900. Those rates are computed here; the paper only plots them.

Five published-value problems surfaced, and each becomes a test decision. The README inside the WMM2025 zip still quotes WMM2020-era sample values, so tests must use Table 6 and the TestValues file. IGRF-14's Table 3 lists the geomagnetic pole at 80.85°, which is the geodetic latitude of the 80.79° geocentric point, although its text describes a pole on a sphere. The paper says the two dip poles share a longitude "around 136°W" in early 2026. Computing the poles puts the crossing near **134.9°E around 2026.2**, so W is almost certainly a typo for E. The paper's dipole share of the Lowes power, 92.31%, is not reproduced by the standard ratio R₁/ΣRₙ, which gives 92.86%, and no tried weighting closes the gap. WMM's north dip pole is at 139.30°E in the text and 139.29°E in Table 4; the computed 139.295°E explains both roundings. On licensing, NCEI places the WMM source code in the public domain ([NCEI](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/world-magnetic-model)), and IGRF-14 states that its coefficients and code are freely available ([Beggan et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40623-025-02360-0)). Shipping WMM2025.COF inside an MIT crate is therefore fine with attribution. The gap such a crate fills is real. The existing `wmm` 0.2.3 stops at WMM2020 and computes only declination ([docs.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/wmm/latest)), and `igrf` 0.2.0 calls itself not well tested ([docs.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/igrf/latest)).

The same engine serves the planets once the reference radius changes. Juno's JRM33 dipole of 4.177 G R_J³ at 71,492 km gives 1.526×10²⁷ A m², **19,849 times Earth's**, matching the published "~20,000 times" ([Connerney et al. 2022](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/dds0z-2vx98)). Cassini's g₁⁰ = 21,141 nT at 60,268 km gives 602 Earth moments ([Cao et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06952)). Swarm ties the two halves of this repository together. It is the backbone satellite of WMM2025, IGRF-14 and CHAOS-8 ([CHAOS-8](https://zenodo.org/records/15806240)), and its GPS-derived monthly gravity fields bridged the July 2017–May 2018 gap between GRACE and GRACE-FO below degree 12 ([Teixeira da Encarnação et al. 2020](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/12/1385/2020/)).

## Space weather and MHD reduce to one-line formulas with published anchors

Space weather supplies the magnetic crate's most decision-relevant numbers, and each needs only the IGRF dipole. Chapman–Ferraro pressure balance, with the factor-2 field compression at a planar magnetopause, gives r = (2B₀²/(μ₀ρv²))^(1/6) R_E ([Magnetopause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetopause)). For 5 protons cm⁻³ at 400 km/s that is **10.08 R_E**, falling to 8.00 R_E if the compression is dropped. The 4^(1/6) = 1.260 ratio between the two is the factor that most textbook slips get wrong. The Shue et al. (1998) empirical model gives 10.90 R_E for the same wind and 5.69 R_E at B_z = −20 nT and 20 nPa ([Shue et al. 1998](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1998JGR...10317691S)). The Dessler–Parker–Sckopke theorem, −μΔB = 2U ([Principles of Heliophysics](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.14022)), converts Dst to ring-current energy at **3.845×10¹³ J per nT**. The May 2024 Gannon storm's −412 nT ([arXiv:2407.07665](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.07665)) then holds 1.58×10¹⁶ J. March 1989's −589 nT holds 2.26×10¹⁶ J ([March 1989 storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_1989_geomagnetic_storm)). The Carrington estimates of −850 to −1760 nT ([SWSC 2026](https://www.swsc-journal.org/articles/swsc/full_html/2026/01/swsc250084/swsc250084.html)) correspond to 3.3–6.8×10¹⁶ J.

One formula in the original brief was wrong, and the correction belongs in the crate's documentation. For a uniform half-space the geoelectric field is |E| = √(ω/(μ₀σ))·|B|, with E leading B by 45°; the briefed form √(ωμ₀/σ)·B is correct only if "B" means H = B/μ₀. The script verifies the corrected form through the magnetotelluric identity ρ_a = 0.2T|E/B|². It then gives **12.9 V/km for a 1000 nT, 300 s variation over 10⁻⁴ S/m ground**, the same order as NERC's TPL-007 benchmark. That benchmark sets 8 V/km at 60° geomagnetic latitude for a 1-in-100-year event, scaled by latitude and ground factors, with a supplemental 12 V/km ([NERC FAQ](https://www.nerc.com/pa/Stand/Project201303GeomagneticDisturbanceMitigation/common_questions_June12.pdf)). What the half-space cannot reproduce is a transformer current. New Zealand's Halfway Bush substation measured a 113 A peak in May 2024 against about 200 A modelled without mitigation ([Mac Manus et al. 2025](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/539520)), and that comparison needs a layered-earth and network model. The Carpenter–Anderson plasmapause, L = 5.6 − 0.46 Kp_max, is valid only for roughly 2.25 < L < 8 ([rbamlib](https://rbamlib.readthedocs.io/dev/models/lpp.html)). At the Kp 9 of May 2024 it returns L < 2, so the function should refuse rather than extrapolate.

The MHD parameters are one-liners with exact regression targets. PlasmaPy publishes its density convention, ρ = n_i m_i + Z n_i m_e. That fixes its docstring values at **43,173.870 m/s for protons and 21,664.18 m/s for He²⁺** at 0.014 T and 5×10¹⁹ m⁻³ ([PlasmaPy](https://plasmapy-rocco8773.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/plasmapy.formulary.speeds.Alfven_speed.html)). Both reproduce to better than 10⁻⁶, and omitting the electron term leaves a 10⁻⁵ mismatch for helium. Chen et al. (2014) showed that the solar-wind spectral break sits at the larger of the ion inertial length and the ion gyroradius. Their Table 1 means give a predicted break of **0.635 Hz against 0.58 ± 0.19 Hz** at low β, and 0.223 Hz against 0.223 ± 0.068 Hz at high β ([Chen et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4459186/)). That result gives a `hall_regime` function an observed test. The perpendicular Rankine–Hugoniot jump is a quadratic, (2−γ)X² + [γ(1+β) + (γ−1)M_A²]X − (γ+1)M_A² = 0, equivalent to the published form ([Trinchieri et al. 2003](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0302590)). It satisfies momentum and energy conservation to 10⁻¹², reaches the strong-shock limit of 4, and gives X = 3.6155 at M_A = 8, β = 1. MMS bow-shock compressions follow the same trend toward 4 ([Lalti et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.04680)), but no event-level test case was retrieved.

Tokamak design formulas reproduce published operating points. The ITER Physics Basis edge-q formula gives **ITER q95 = 2.996 against 3.0** ([Sips 2005](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0410263)). For SPARC it gives a Uckan q* of 3.059 against the published 3.05, and an IPB value of 3.621 against "≈ 3.6" ([Creely et al. 2020](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-plasma-physics/article/overview-of-the-sparc-tokamak/DD3C44ECD26F5EACC554811764EF9FF0)). Greenwald's limit at f_G = 0.85 gives ITER's ⟨n_e⟩ = 1.015×10²⁰ m⁻³ against the published 1.01×10²⁰. IPB98(y,2) has no forward test, because neither source tabulates net heating power. Inverted, it needs **83.8 MW for ITER's 3.7 s and 29.0 MW for SPARC's 0.77 s**, both inside the physically allowed band. Stability theory gives three analytic results that reproduce published numbers. Kadomtsev's energy-principle criteria on the Bennett profile put the marginal kink surface at **r* = √3 r_p with β* = 1/3**, exactly as Crews and Turner publish ([arXiv:2608.00291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.00291)). The same criteria show the Bennett pinch is sausage-unstable everywhere unless γ ≥ 2. The magnetic Rayleigh–Taylor cut-off reproduces Khomenko et al.'s 38 km and 155 km prominence wavelengths ([Khomenko et al. 2014](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1310.7016)), provided the unstated solar gravity of 274 m/s² is supplied. The FKR tearing coefficient [Γ(1/4)/(2πΓ(3/4))]^(4/5) = 0.5474 matches Fitzpatrick's ≈0.55 ([Fitzpatrick](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/plasma/lectures/node127.html)). That formula hands off to the plasmoid crate's existing reconnection rates once Δ′ exceeds S^(1/3). For liquid-metal blankets, the inertialess gradient σūB²·c/(1+c) reproduces **KIT's 0.0678 at c = 0.0727** ([KIT MHD group](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000097959/38980113)). It does not reproduce KIT's flow-channel-insert value, 0.00508 against a computed 0.00474.

Dynamo theory contributes thresholds rather than predictions, and the laboratory record shows why. The Riga dynamo's optimised profiles gave thresholds of Rm 14.3 (convective) and 17.6 (absolute). Karlsruhe measured an α-number of 8.4–9.3 against 8.12 from mean-field theory. Most tellingly, VKS2 self-excited at **Rm 31–35, where numerical predictions gave 48–133**, because soft-iron impellers changed the boundary ([Stefani, Gailitis & Gerbeth 2008](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.0299)). The plane-wave αΩ dynamo's onset at D = αS/(η_T²k³) = 2, with cycle frequency η_T k² ([Brandenburg & Subramanian 2005](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0405052)), is recovered to 10⁻³ by bisection on the full dispersion relation. That is an exact test. Christensen's energy-flux law, B² = 1.2μ₀f_ohm ρ^(1/3)(Fq)^(2/3) ([Christensen 2009 lecture](https://cpaess.ucar.edu/sites/default/files/heliophysics/resources/presentations/2009_Christensen_planmagdynamo.pdf)), could not be tested on any planet, because its Earth and Jupiter inputs were not retrievable. It should ship as an order-of-magnitude predictor with that caveat in its documentation.

## Engineering magnets stop at 1 GPa of stress and 4.3 S/m of seawater

Two numbers organise applied magnetics: the magnetic pressure B²/2μ₀ against the strength of the structure, and the conductivity σ against what the working fluid supplies. The cleanest test case is the CFS/MIT Toroidal Field Model Coil. Its Lorentz line load I·B = 40.5 kA × 20.1 T = **814 kN/m, against the published 815 kN/m**, with almost 1 GPa carried by the case ([Hartwig et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12301)). The records as of October 2026 fall into categories that headlines often blur. Hefei's 45.22 T hybrid (2022) is a working steady user magnet ([CAS](https://english.cas.cn/head/202208/t20220815_311432.shtml)). The 45.5 T result of 2019 ([Hahn et al. 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v570y2019i7762d10.1038_s41586-019-1293-1.html)) and MagLab's 48.7 T of 2025 ([MagLab](https://nationalmaglab.org/news-events/news/a-prototype-miniature-superconducting-magnet/)) are high-temperature-superconductor test coils inside 31 T resistive magnets. The magnetic pressure at 48.7 T, 944 MPa, sits just below the more than 1.03 GPa that coil survived. That is why progress above about 45 T now depends on tape strength rather than critical current. The virial theorem caps stored energy per kilogram of structure at σ/ρ. At 100 MPa in steel that is 12.5 kJ/kg, against Tixador's "about 10 kJ/kg" ([Tixador](https://snf.ieeecsc.org/files/ieeecsc/slides/CR5_Final3_012008.pdf)). This explains why superconducting magnetic energy storage survives only as fast grid stabilisers, like the Bonneville Power Administration's 30 MJ, 10 MW unit, which holds three seconds at full power.

Levitation follows from one condition. Berry and Geim showed that Earnshaw's theorem does not cover induced moments, so water floats where B·dB/dz = μ₀ρg/|χ| = **1400.9 T²/m**. Their Bitter-magnet profile predicts a centre field of 16.5 T, and the script reproduces it to 0.3% ([Berry & Geim 1997](https://obelix.physik.uni-bielefeld.de/~schnack/Herbstakademie/CD-2008/frog-ejp.pdf)). Pyrolytic graphite needs only about 60 T²/m, which NdFeB arrays supply at room temperature. The force acts on magnetisation, not on mass. It is the only working "antigravity", and it is not gravity at all. Inductrack's lift-to-drag ratio, kvL/R, hides a consistency test. Post's two published examples, 300 at 500 km/h and 30 at 50 km/h, both imply the same transition speed of **1.67 km/h** ([US 6,629,503](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6629503)). The patent's own "a few m/s" disagrees with that, so the crate should take the transition speed as an input.

Conductivity is where MHD machines lose. A segmented Faraday generator delivers σu²B²k(1−k) per unit volume, which peaks at load factor ½. The large plants did produce tens of megawatts, but Avco's Mk V converted only 32 MW of 550 MW thermal (5.8%) and the U-25 20.4 of 250 MW (8.2%). They stalled on electrode corrosion, electrothermal instability and seed recovery ([MHD generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MHD_generator)). The often-quoted 30.2% belongs to a shock-tube disk generator, not to the Fuji-1 blow-down rig, which peaked at 18% ([OSTI 381436](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/381436); [OSTI 381435](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/381435)). Seawater at 4.2914 S/m ([TEOS-10](https://mail.teos-10.org/pubs/gsw/html/gsw_C_from_SP.html)) is about 10⁷ times worse than copper. An MHD thruster's electrical efficiency is σuB²/(σuB² + f), which with illustrative inputs is 1.7% at 4 T and 30% only at 20 T. That is consistent with Yamato-1's logged 6.2 knots and roughly 15% system efficiency ([Yamato-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato-1)). Electric propulsion obeys η = T g₀ I_sp/(2P), and the published numbers close on it: SPT-100B 48% ([SPT-100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPT-100)), and VX-200 at 69.9% against a claimed 72 ± 6% ([Longmier et al. 2014](https://www.adastrarocket.com/technical-papers-archives/Ben-JPP-2014.pdf)). VASIMR's 39-day Mars transit is a claim about a reactor at roughly 1 kg/kW that does not exist. At 20 kg/kW, 200 MW of reactor weighs 4000 t ([Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/zubrin-claims-vasimr-is-a-hoax)). Medicine and sensing give exact constants. The CODATA 2022 proton gyromagnetic ratio, 42.577 478 461 MHz/T, gives **127.732 MHz at 3 T** ([NIST](https://www.physics.nist.gov/cuu/pdf/wall_2022.pdf)). IEC 60601-2-33 sets 2 W/kg whole-body SAR in normal mode ([CMRR](https://www.cmrr.umn.edu/media/forms/CMRR_IEC-SAR_Guidelines.pdf)). Romalis's SERF gradiometer, 160 aT/√Hz in 0.45 cm³, reproduces its published 44ħ energy resolution to 1.2% ([Dang et al. 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.2206)).

The over-claims in applied magnetics are mostly scale-up extrapolations, not physics violations. Examples are open-cycle MHD's "up to 60%", M2P2's 15 km plasma bubble, and the railgun's range. The Navy fired 33 MJ in 2010, but barrels lasted 12–24 rounds and the programme ended in 2021 after about US$500 M ([NavWeaps](https://navweaps.com/Weapons/WNUS_Rail_Gun.php); [Task & Purpose](https://taskandpurpose.com/news/navy-electromagnetic-railgun-dead/)). M2P2's thrust was undone by kinetic analysis, because the bubble would be smaller than the ion Larmor radius ([NTRS 20030067378](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20030067378)). The Andrews–Zubrin magsail is sound physics with a corrected 3.1× drag error ([Magnetic sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_sail)). This suggests a crate-level pattern: store each claim beside the measured value and the inputs that reproduce it, and let the test assert the ratio.

## Gravity and magnetism share equations but never couplings

Gravitoelectromagnetism is linearised general relativity written to look like Maxwell's equations, and its factors of 2 and 4 are pure convention, with one exception. Mashhoon's convention puts the spin-2 factor into the charges: a test mass has q_B/q_E = 2, against unity for electromagnetism. The force is F = −mE − 2m(v/c)×B ([Mashhoon](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0311030)). Other authors put the 2 in the field or in the Ampère law; the "Maxwell-like SI" form carries a factor of 4 in the force ([Gravitoelectromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitoelectromagnetism)). The script computes the force per mass on a body at 7.9 km/s in all three conventions. All give **2.65×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², agreeing to 10⁻¹²**, which is the right regression target. The gyroscope precession Ω_P = B/c is equally convention-free, and it is what experiments measure. The script sums the LAGEOS and LARES 2 node rates to 61.35 mas/yr against the published 61.3 mas/yr ([Physics World](https://physicsworld.com/a/frame-dragging-measurement-around-the-earth-sets-new-precision-record/)). The Gravity Probe B average comes out at 39.17 mas/yr against GR's 39.2 ([Everitt et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3456)). Two traps need documentation. Ruggiero and Tartaglia's dipole equation (24) is −2 times the curl of their own potential (10), so it is consistent with their force law but not with their field definition ([Ruggiero & Tartaglia](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0207065)). The uniform-sphere Earth formula overstates the field by 0.4/0.3307 = 1.21.

Blackett's 1947 hypothesis is GEM's best-known refuted extension, and its numbers make the refutation concrete. In SI form, μ = (β/2)√(4πε₀G)·J. Blackett found β ≈ 0.3 for Earth and about 1.2 for 78 Vir, but **β across the measured bodies spans a factor of 4.7×10⁶**. Mars's missing dipole forces β < 2.4×10⁻⁵, about 10⁴ times below Earth's value ([Campanelli](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02734)). Blackett's own 1952 magnetometer experiment came out negative ([Royal Society](https://makingscience.royalsociety.org/items/rr_77_79/referees-report-by-edward-crisp-bullard-on-a-paper-a-negative-experiment-relating-to-magnetism-and-the-earths-rotation-by-patrick-maynard-stuart-blackett)). The IGRF series adds an argument the notes did not make. Earth's spin angular momentum is fixed, yet its dipole moment fell from 8.321 to 7.690×10²² A m² between 1900 and 2025. A body-constant β therefore drifted by 7.6% in 125 years on Earth alone, from 0.313 with Biemond's tabulated moment to **0.305 with the IGRF-14 2025 moment** (checked here). What survives is Barrow and Gibbons's conjecture that β is bounded by a number of order one, which charged rotating black holes obey ([Barrow & Gibbons](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.06343)).

The genuine frontier links are quantitative and testable. The Dirac charge is h/(μ₀e) = 3.291×10⁻⁹ A m in SI, and its flux, h/e, is two superconducting flux quanta. Cabrera's single 1982 event in 151 days through a 20 cm² loop ([Cabrera 1982](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.48.1378)) implies a flux of 6.10×10⁻¹⁰ cm⁻² s⁻¹ sr⁻¹. That is **6.1×10⁵ times the Parker bound** and 3×10⁹ times IceCube's limit for relativistic monopoles ([PDG 2025](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2025/listings/rpp2025-list-magnetic-monopole.pdf)). The event stays unexplained, but no flux at that level exists. Axion haloscopes and graviton conversion share one equation. ADMX's 2018 reference parameters give 1.94×10⁻²² W against the published 1.9×10⁻²² W ([ADMX 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05750)). In CAST's 9 T, 9.26 m magnet, a graviton converts exactly like an axion with coupling √(16πG) = 5.81×10⁻¹⁹ GeV⁻¹. That conversion probability, 5.74×10⁻³⁴, is **10¹⁶ below the axion probability at CAST's 2024 limit** ([CAST 2024](https://inspirehep.net/literature/2801541)), and it reproduces the existing `gravitational::hf::gertsenshtein_probability` to 10⁻³. Blandford–Znajek power in SI, kΦ²Ω_H²/(μ₀c), meets the EHT's 10⁴² erg/s jet floor for M87* only if the horizon field is at least about 23 G. That favours the magnetically arrested flows the EHT found among its 1–30 G models ([EHT Paper VIII](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01173); [EHT Paper V](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.11242)).

The fringe magnetic devices fail conservation, not subtle physics. The patented "motionless electromagnetic generator" claims a coefficient of performance of 3.44. Its 39 cm³ NdFeB magnet stores about 22 J, which the claimed 82 W excess would drain in 0.27 s, and the patent itself admits that the magnet demagnetises ([US 6,362,718](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6362718B1/en)). Roshchin and Godin's converter claims 7 kW and a 1.2 kN weight change ([Roshchin & Godin 2000](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2000TePhL..26.1105R/abstract)). Its magnets hold about 8.6 kJ and its rotor 47.7 kJ, a few seconds of output. A reactionless 1.2 kN would need a 30 kW air jet or 3.6×10¹¹ W of photons. The claimed 0.05 T "magnetic walls" 15 m away are 4×10⁴ times the magnets' own dipole field at that distance. Steorn's own 22-member jury ruled in 2009 that no energy production had been shown ([Orbo](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbo)). These belong in a `frontier` module as conservation checks that a test can assert, not as models.

## A field trait, an umbrella crate and runnable examples bind the seven crates

The dependency graph decides where things live. `metrology` sits at the bottom. `geopotential`, `gravitational` and `plasmoid` depend only on it, `gravimetry` builds on `geopotential`, and `gravitics` on `gravitational`. The new `magnetics` crate depends on `metrology`, `geopotential` (the engine and the trait) and `plasmoid`. The MHD notes recommend reusing plasmoid's Bennett profile, Lundquist number and Sweet–Parker rates rather than duplicating them. `gravitics` gains a dependency on `geopotential`, so that its gravitomagnetic dipole can implement `Field`. That is light, since `geopotential` itself depends only on `metrology`. The analogies module needs functions from five crates at once, and putting it anywhere below the top would create a cycle. It therefore belongs in `physica`, which then does more than re-export. One placement rule keeps tokamak material from splitting arbitrarily. First-principles MHD (waves, shocks, Grad–Shafranov, stability, Hartmann flow, dynamos) goes in `magnetics`. Empirical device scalings (q95, Greenwald, Troyon, IPB98) go in `plasmoid` beside Lawson, so that a SPARC example runs in one crate. The user's `geo5d` 0.5.1 already ships `lense_thirring_node_rate`, `gyroscope_frame_dragging` and Earth's angular momentum. It depends on `geopotential` 0.1.1 from crates.io, so it cannot implement the 0.2 trait until it is bumped. `gravitics::gem` should therefore supply the field and the conventions, and leave orbit-averaged rates to `geo5d`.

The trait needs one decision before it ships. The draft documentation says that `vector` returns tesla for a magnetic field, while the in-progress `MainField` returns nanotesla, the unit every published test value uses. Either the documentation should say "in the field's own units", or `MainField` should scale by 10⁻⁹ and `Elements` keep nT. The gravitomagnetic field fits the trait without strain. Outside its source, Mashhoon's B = (G/c)[3(J·r̂)r̂ − J]/r³ equals −∇ψ with ψ = (G/c)J·r/r³. Earth's equatorial value is **5.03×10⁻⁶ m/s², twice that at the pole**, the same pole-to-equator ratio as the magnetic dipole. A Swarm-altitude example can then evaluate gravity, the main field and the gravitomagnetic field through one `&dyn Field`. That is the family's unification in a single loop.

The practical uses give each crate a worked example worth running. WMM is the navigation and heading standard for the US DoD, UK MoD, NATO and the IHO ([NGA](https://www.nga.mil/news/NGA_NOAA_BGS_Publish_World_Magnetic_Model_2025.html)). Directional drillers need declination to 0.1°, dip to 0.05° and total field to 50 nT ([BGS](https://geomag.bgs.ac.uk/data_service/directionaldrilling/ifr.html)). A 0.1° azimuth error over a 5 km step-out moves the bottom hole 8.7 m sideways, and that number makes the case for the crustal corrections WMM omits. GRACE mass change feeds the US Drought Monitor and California's groundwater reports ([NASA JPL](https://grace.jpl.nasa.gov/applications/overview/)). JPL's Greenland mascon trend of −267.49 ± 21.00 Gt/yr ([NASA SVS](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/webapps/sealevelgraphs/Updated/data/greenland_mass_200204_202404.txt)) is a single-product example. Hulse–Taylor's GR orbital decay, −2.40263(5)×10⁻¹² ([Weisberg & Huang 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02744)), follows from published elements to 0.02%. Wurzel and Hsu's triple products multiply back exactly ([arXiv:2505.03834](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03834v6)), and JET's 69.26 MJ corresponds to 0.205 mg of fuel burned, against the published 0.21 mg ([DIFFER](https://www.differ.nl/news/JET-energy-record)). The argument for Rust over Python is flight heritage: the Rust tools Nyx, ANISE and Hifitime were the flight-dynamics backbone of Firefly's Blue Ghost Moon landing ([Nyx Space](https://nyxspace.com/blog/category/blue-ghost/)). Published-value tests like these are the evidence that such use demands.

## Conclusion

The unexpected finding is that geomagnetism is a relabelled gravity problem. Twelve rows of a NOAA table become regression tests for code written for GRACE. A geomagnetic library is then a parser and a frame rotation around an engine already built to degree 2700, not another Legendre recursion. Once that is clear, the `Field` trait is less a new abstraction than an admission of what the code already does. It also changes what "cross-domain" should mean in tests. The robust analogies are not loose resemblances. They are exact identities a test can assert: the spin-2 factor of 2 inside a spinning shell; one √(4πε₀G) behind Blackett, Reissner–Nordström, the weak-gravity conjecture and plasmoid's "equal attraction" drift; one conversion function for axions and gravitons; one skin-depth formula from a copper bar to Earth's core.

The second takeaway is that magnetic claims fail in two distinct ways, and the crates should label which. Engineering over-claims (VASIMR to Mars, MHD ships, open-cycle generators, 50 T coils) fail on materials and conductivity: stress near 1 GPa, or σB² that seawater and seeded gas cannot supply. A future material could revisit them. Fringe claims (Blackett's universal β, Cabrera's flux, magnet motors) fail on conservation or on a second measurement, and no material fixes them. The secular decline of Earth's own dipole, a number this crate computes on its first day, is enough to retire a universal Blackett β, without any 1952 magnetometer.

### What stays out

Leave out the following:

- WMMHR2025 (degree 133), CHAOS-8, LCS-1, Kalmag and a full JRM33 Jupiter field. The engine handles them, but no coefficients or point test values were obtained; add them when there is a published point to test.
- The oblique Rankine–Hugoniot cubic, switch-on and intermediate shocks, the Suydam and Mercier criteria, resistive wall modes, non-constant-ψ tearing, Hall-MHD dispersion and two-fluid tearing. None was sourced.
- The Shercliff and Hunt rectangular-duct series, Walker's thin-wall formula, eddy-current brakes, coilguns, the Halbach-array formula and the continuous-electrode and Hall generator factors. None has a retrieved benchmark number.
- Christensen's planet-by-planet predictions, solar dynamo numbers and the Biermann battery beyond its formula. No inputs or numeric example were found.
- Network GIC models (Halfway Bush's 113 A, Swedish transformer currents), layered or 3-D magnetotellurics, Dst* pressure correction and radiation-belt adiabatic invariants.
- Absolute REBCO Jc, the Gd magnetocaloric B^0.7 rule, Boldyrev's −3/2 spectrum and the tearing-mediated range.
- As models: the MEG, Roshchin–Godin, Searl, Hutchison, Steorn and Townsend Brown magnetic claims, Dyatlov's "vacuum domains", M2P2 as a thrust model, and VASIMR's 39-day Mars architecture. Keep them only as conservation or claim-against-measurement checks in `frontier` and `devices`.
- Weyl's gauge factor and Weinberg–Witten. Neither yields a number; the Aharonov–Bohm phase is the one measurable remnant.
