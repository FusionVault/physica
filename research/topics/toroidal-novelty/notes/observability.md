# Can a toroidal dipole be told apart from an electric dipole? Observability theorems, anapole tolerances, and exact properties of supertoroidal pulses

Scope: the dispute over whether the dynamic toroidal dipole T can be measured independently of the electric dipole p, what can be proved about it, the sharp numbers behind the Mie anapole, and new exact properties of the supertoroidal pulse family. Each candidate result is checked against the literature found, proved where it is ours, checked numerically, and assessed for `magnetics::toroidal`.

Conventions: SI, time dependence e^(−iωt), k = ω/c, ρ = ∇·J/(iω), p = ∫ρ r dV = (i/ω)∫J dV, T = (1/10)∫[(r·J)r − 2r²J] dV (the form used by `magnetics::toroidal::toroidal_dipole`). The exact electric dipole is that of Alaee, Rockstuhl & Fernandez-Corbaton (2018), p_exact = (i/ω){∫J j₀(kr) + (k²/2)∫[3(r·J)r − r²J] j₂(kr)/(kr)²}. Its k-expansion is p + (ik/c)T + O(k⁴), the `effective_dipole` of the crate.

Checks: `notes/observability_checks.py`, run as `python3 -P observability_checks.py` (needs numpy). It makes 91 assertions, takes about 20 s wall time, and exits 1 on any failure; all pass. Two values were cross-checked against the Rust crate itself: `mie_a1_zero_diameter` and `supertoroidal_generating_fn`, both called from a throw-away binary in the session scratchpad (no crate code was edited). Their printed values are pinned in the script.

Downloads are in `notes/dl_obs/` and were treated as untrusted data.

## 1. State of the debate

### Takeaway
Nobody disputes the formulas. The dispute is over what "independent" means:
- Fernandez-Corbaton, Nanz & Rockstuhl prove that at one frequency no measurement outside the source can separate the electric and toroidal parts. They also argue, by reductio, that spectroscopy cannot separate them either.
- The Southampton group (Papasimakis et al., Savinov) and Radescu & Vaman hold that the different frequency, refractive-index or acceleration dependence separates them. Canós Valero et al. claim a "pure" toroidal source, verified with interior and far fields.
- The rest are neutral. Nemkov et al. say the radiation alone cannot reveal T "but requires an investigation of the source". Gurvitz et al. claim only formal (SO(3)) independence. Yang & Bozhevolnyi call it an open debate that does not affect anapoles.

Every proposed separation either holds the current pattern fixed while k changes, or looks inside the source. Section 2 shows those are exactly the cases where separation is possible. Status of the single-frequency no-go: **mainstream** (a proof, no published rebuttal found). Status of "spectroscopy separates them": **contested**.

### Inferences
- The two camps answer different questions:
  - Fernandez-Corbaton et al.'s single-frequency theorem is a statement about exterior data.
  - The separations that Southampton proposes vary k (through ω, n or a Doppler shift) while treating the source as a rigid, point-like current.
  - Canós Valero et al. look inside the source.

  None of the three contradicts the others. Section 2 makes this exact.
- Gurvitz et al.'s "formal independency" (SO(3) irreducibility) cannot separate p from T in the field. Both are l = 1 polar vectors of the same parity, and both enter one coefficient a_E1. Irreducibility makes them independent functionals of J, not independent observables: **derived here** (it follows from Section 2.1).

## 2. Can it be settled? Four exact statements

### Takeaway
Yes, in a precise sense:
- **(A)** At one frequency, exterior data place no constraint on p or T separately. For any source and any target pair (p′, T′) there is a source in the same ball with identical exterior fields and identical coupling to every external vacuum field, and with moments (p′, T′). Knowing the source size does not help, and neither does the near field.
- **(B)** Across a band, if the current pattern is rigid, J(r,ω) = f(ω)j(r), with f known, then p, T and every higher Taylor coefficient are determined exactly.
- **(C)** Without rigidity, an O(k²) deformation of the pattern that carries a dipole moment is exactly degenerate with T. So no model-independent dispersion test exists, and a frequency-scaling test checks the rigid-pattern model, not "toroidality".
- **(D)** A probe whose sources overlap the toroid, so that ∇×B − c⁻²∂E/∂t = μ₀J_ext ≠ 0 there, couples to T directly: U = μ₀T·J_ext.

Fernandez-Corbaton et al.'s spectroscopic reductio is not a proof. Under (B) the "doubly infinite" family is just the moment sequence of one fixed function.

Status:
- (A): **mainstream** in substance (Devaney–Wolf, Fernandez-Corbaton et al.); our explicit (p, T)-unconstrained form is **derived here**.
- (B) and (C), with the critique of the reductio: **new to our search**, but elementary.
- (D): **mainstream** (Dubovik's T couples to ∇×B).

### 2.1 Single frequency (A)
Claim. Let J be supported in a closed set K with connected complement. Then:
1. The exterior field at ω, and the coupling ∫J·E dV to every E with (∇×∇× − k²)E = 0 near K, depend only on the transverse part of J̃(K) on the shell |K| = k. Status: **known** (Devaney & Wolf; Fernandez-Corbaton et al. §"Mathematical setting").
2. For any smooth vector field F supported in K, the current J_F = (∇×∇× − k²)F radiates nothing and couples to no external vacuum field. On the shell, J̃_F⊥ = (|K|² − k²)F̃⊥ = 0. By two integrations by parts, ∫J_F·E = ∫F·(∇×∇× − k²)E = 0. Status: **known** construction (Devaney–Wolf non-radiating sources; Savinov 2018 notes the same impossibility).
3. Moments. Write M_ijk = ∫J_i x_j x_k. Integration by parts gives M_ijk[∇×∇×F] = δ_ij G_k + δ_ik G_j − 2δ_jk G_i, where G = ∫F. Hence:
   - ∫J_F = −k² G, so p = −(ik/c)G;
   - T(J_F) = G − (k²/10)∫[(r·F)r − 2r²F].

   For F = ẑφ(r) with φ radial, this gives T = (Φ₀ + k²Φ₂/6)ẑ, where Φ₀ = ∫φ and Φ₂ = ∫r²φ. Two radial profiles fix (Φ₀, Φ₂) independently, so (p, T) can take any value. Status: **derived here**.
4. Corollary. Adding such a J_F to any source leaves all exterior data at ω unchanged and shifts (p, T) by any chosen amount, within any ball, however small. So at one frequency the exterior data constrain only a_E1 (and the other exact multipoles), never p or T. This is stronger than "cannot be separately determined": even p alone is unconstrained. Status: **derived here** (a direct consequence of Devaney–Wolf; not found stated this way).

Numerical check (script §1): bumps (1 − r²/a²)⁴ and (r²/a²)(1 − r²/a²)⁴ at ka = 0.3, 1, 2.5.
- The exact Alaee dipole vanishes to ≤ 1.1×10⁻¹³ of |∫J|.
- ∫J = −k²Φ₀ and T = Φ₀ + k²Φ₂/6 hold to 10⁻¹².
- p + (ik/c)T ≠ 0. Its relative size is exactly k²⟨r²⟩/6, cancelled by the O(k⁴) terms.
- One combination has ∫J = 0 and T = 1 and is exactly non-radiating: a "pure toroid" that is invisible. This is Canós Valero et al.'s "toroidal anapole" idea (T + k²T⁽²⁾ = 0) made exact.
- 3-D quadrature gives zero plane-wave coupling for two transverse polarisations (≤ 2.4×10⁻¹⁶). A uniform field E = ẑ, which is not a vacuum field at k ≠ 0, does couple, with ∫J_z = −k²Φ₀.

### 2.2 Band data with a rigid pattern (B)
Claim. Suppose J(r,ω) = f(ω)j(r) on an interval of ω, with f known and non-zero. Then a_E1(ω) ∝ (i/ω)f(ω)Φ(k), with Φ(k) = ∫j j₀(kr) + (k²/2)∫[3(r·j)r − r²j] j₂(kr)/(kr)². Both j₀(x) and j₂(x)/x² are entire, even functions, so Φ is entire and even in k. Knowing Φ on an interval therefore fixes it everywhere (identity theorem), and with it Φ(0) = ∫j (giving p), ½Φ″(0) = T(j), and every higher coefficient. The same holds for every a_lm and b_lm. If f is unknown, Φ is known only up to an unknown function of k, and nothing about T/p follows. Status: **new to our search** (elementary).

Consequence for Fernandez-Corbaton et al.'s reductio: under rigidity the infinitely many k^(2n) coefficients are moments of one fixed j(r). They are not independent field degrees of freedom, so nothing unphysical follows. The reductio therefore does not prove that spectroscopic separation is impossible. What makes separation possible or impossible is the rigidity hypothesis. Their single-frequency theorem is unaffected. Status: **new to our search**.

Numerical check (script §2): the source is a 24-turn toroidal solenoid (R = 1 m, a = 0.3 m, 1 A) plus a 0.2 m axial wire segment carrying 1 A. A fit of the exact Φ(k) at kR = 0.05, 0.10, 0.15 recovers:
- ∫j = 0.2 A m to 4×10⁻⁸;
- the pattern's T = −3.392429 A m³ as −3.392424.

### 2.3 Deforming patterns: no model-independent dispersion test (C)
Claim. If j(r,ω) = j₀(r) + k²j₂(r) + O(k⁴), then Φ(k) = ∫j₀ + k²[T(j₀) + ∫j₂] + O(k⁴). A pattern change at O(k²) with dipole moment ∫j₂ is therefore indistinguishable, at that order, from a toroidal moment of the same size. The single-frequency family makes this exact at all orders: J_F(ω) = ∇×∇×F − k²F is two fixed patterns, ∇×∇×F and F, with drives g(ω) and −k²g(ω). So any source with two or more independently driven patterns (a multiport antenna, or any resonant scatterer whose internal field changes with ω) has unidentifiable toroidal content, even with full broadband data. Status: **new to our search** (elementary).

Numerical check (script §2): the axial segment alone, with its current rising as I(k) = 1 + k²T_sol/ℓ, has a toroidal moment 5×10⁴ times smaller than the solenoid's. Yet its exact a_E1 matches the toroid-plus-segment source to 2.7×10⁻⁴ of the toroidal term at kR = 0.05, and the mismatch falls as k² relative to that term.

Inference: for a current-fed wire antenna, charge build-up along the wire is O((kL)²), the same order as the toroidal term, and p_z and T_z have the same symmetry. So the "T" extracted from a frequency fit is only as good as the circuit model at O(k²). The same applies to the n⁵ scaling (Savinov 2018b, at fixed ω and varying n) and to the accelerated-source ellipticity (Savinov 2018a, rigid point source): each tests the rigid-source model. Status: **derived here**.

### 2.4 Overlapping probes (D)
Claim. A static closed current in a uniform external current density J₀ẑ has interaction energy ∫J·A_ext = μ₀J₀T_z exactly. The proof: A_ext = −μ₀J₀ρ²ẑ/4, T_z = −¼∫r²J_z for a closed current, and ∫z²J_z = 0 by ∫J·∇(z³) = 0. An electric dipole has no such coupling. So probes that overlap the source (a conductor threading the hole, a Rogowski-type winding, or Canós Valero et al.'s interior field maps) see T. This lies outside the hypotheses of (A). In vacuum ∇×B = c⁻²∂E/∂t, and the coupling falls back to p_eff. Status: **mainstream** (Dubovik & Tugushev: T couples to ∇×B, as quoted from Planes et al. in `toroidal_moments.md` §2). The exactness of the uniform-J result for any closed current is **derived here**.

Numerical check: the 24-turn solenoid gives U/(μ₀J₀) = −3.392362 = T_z, to 10⁻⁹.

### Gaps
- The norm-bounded (stability) version of (A) was not computed: how much a bound on ‖J‖ limits the hidden T when ka ≪ 1. It may be a small extremal problem worth one more step.
- The O(k²) charge dipole of a real toroidal winding was argued, not computed.

## 3. Sharp predictions and the Mie anapole

### Takeaway
- **ω⁶.** For a rigid, divergence-free pattern (p ≡ 0) driven at known current, the radiated power scales as P ∝ ω⁶|I|²: radiation resistance ∝ (kR)⁶, against (kL)² for a Hertzian dipole and (ka)⁴ for a small loop, with the electric-dipole sin²θ pattern. That combination is the fingerprint, valid only under (B). Status: **mainstream** for the formula (`toroidal_radiated_power`); its scope is set by Section 2.
- **Mie zero.** For n = 4 at 550 nm, a₁ vanishes at d₀ = 204.028 nm, with:
  - dd/dn = −55.46 nm per unit index;
  - dd/dλ = 0.3710 (non-dispersive), or 0.541 with silicon dispersion.

  A residual |a₁|² < 10⁻³ (as a fraction of the unitary maximum |a₁| = 1) needs, one parameter at a time:
  - d within −1.020/+1.082 nm (about ±0.5 %);
  - n within −0.0184/+0.0195;
  - λ within −2.90/+2.76 nm.
- **Absorption floor.** Loss imposes min_d |a₁|² = |∂a₁/∂n|²κ² + O(κ³), with |∂a₁/∂n| = 1.6705. For crystalline Si at 550 nm (κ = 0.027968), the floor is 1.906×10⁻³ at d = 199.9 nm. No fabrication can reach 10⁻³ there. Reaching it needs κ < 0.019.
- **Not a true anapole.** At the zero the far field does not vanish: |b₁|² = 0.0762 under plane-wave light and |a₂|² = 0.0018. And by (A), "seeing a true anapole" from outside can only ever mean seeing a₁ ≈ 0. The p_car = −(ik/c)T split is computed from the model's interior field, not measured.

Status:
- the numbers: **derived here**, using our functions;
- the loss-floor identity: **derived here**, probably known in substance;
- tolerances and floor: not found published (query 19).

### Inferences
- Rigid-pattern ω⁶ test: see the Takeaway. In practice the deformation degeneracy (C) applies at O(k²).
- The Mie numbers come from the script's mirror of `mie_a1_zero_diameter`. The mirror equals the Rust function to 10⁻⁹ nm at six indices, with the Rust values pinned. The zero diameter scales as d₀ = x₀(n)λ/π, so the size and wavelength tolerances are tied: δd/d = δλ/λ when n is non-dispersive.
- Loss-floor proof. For real m, a₁ = g/(g + ih) with g and h real, so near a zero a₁ ≈ −i(g_xδx + g_nδn)/h, and every partial derivative has the same phase. With m = n + iκ, a₁ ≈ −i[g_xδx + g_n(δn + iκ)]/h. Hence |a₁|² ≥ (g_n/h)²κ² = |∂a₁/∂n|²κ², with equality at g_xδx + g_nδn = 0. Loss acts as an index error of size κ that no size tuning removes. Script: the floor matches 2.79κ² to 2 % at κ = 10⁻³ and 3×10⁻³.

### Gaps
- Disks (the geometry Miroshnichenko et al. actually used) were not modelled; spheres only.
- The finite numerical aperture of real collection optics was not modelled.

## 4. Supertoroidal pulses: exact energy, momentum, rest frame, and two corrections

### Takeaway
For f = f₀/[(q₁ + iτ)(s + q₂)^α] with the TE fields of Shen et al.:
- **Analyticity.** Re(s + q₂) ≥ q₂ > 0, and the principal power is analytic. This is trivial, and implicit in the Laplace (MPS) representation: **known**.
- **Spectrum.** The 3-D plane-wave spectrum is a closed form (below).
- **Energy.** The physical (real-part) pulse energy is

  U = (π^{3/2}/2) μ₀ f₀² [Γ(α+½)/Γ(α)] (αq₁ + q₂)/(q₁³ q₂^{2α+1}).

  This is finite for every α > 0, so the oft-repeated "α ≥ 1 to ensure finite energy" (Shen et al. 2021; Shen, Papasimakis & Zheludev 2025) is not a necessary condition.
- **Momentum.** cP_z/U = (q₂ − αq₁)/(q₂ + αq₁).
- **Rest frame.** The family is boost-covariant, (q₁, q₂, f₀) → (q₁/λ, λq₂, λ^{α−1}f₀), so the zero-momentum frame is q₂ = αq₁. If Shen et al.'s α = 20 figure uses their usual q₂ = 20q₁, that pulse has zero net momentum.
- **Corrections to Shen et al.:**
  - Their printed H_z (eq. 6, and SI eq. C3) is wrong for α ≠ 1. Their Fig. 6 matches the correct field, so this is a typo, not a wrong figure.
  - The whole-plane polarity of their magnetic "skyrmions" is ±½ (a meron) for α ∉ {1, 2}. Their integer labels are a finite-window reading.
- **On-axis decay.** The on-axis H_z falls as (2ct)^−(α+1).

Status: energy, momentum, rest frame, finiteness for α > 0, the eq. (6) error and the meron result are **new to our search**. Each is proved below and checked to 10⁻¹⁰ or better.

### Inferences (derivations; all **new to our search** unless marked)
1. Spectrum. Write f as a Laplace integral of focus-wave modes, f = (q₁+iτ)⁻¹∫₀^∞ [β^(α−1)e^(−q₂β)/Γ(α)] e^(−βs) dβ. This converges because Re(s + q₂) ≥ q₂, which is the analyticity remark (**known**, implicit in the MPS construction). Then use the Gaussian Hankel transform. With K = |K|, k_z the axial and κ the transverse wavenumber:

   f = (2π)⁻¹∫d³K a(K) e^(i(K·x + cKt)), a(K) = f₀ [(K+k_z)/2]^(α−1) e^(−[q₂(K+k_z) + q₁(K−k_z)]/2) / (2KΓ(α)).

   Every component has one sign of frequency, and the waves travel along −K.
2. Energy. For the magnetic Hertz potential ẑf, |Ê|²ε₀ = |Ĥ|²μ₀ = μ₀K²κ²|ĝ|². By Plancherel, the complex-field energy is U_c = 2πμ₀∫d³K K²κ²|a|². In spherical K-coordinates with v = (1 + cos θ)/2, the K-integral gives Γ(2α+3)/L^(2α+3). The v-integral is a ₂F₁ that Euler's transformation reduces to a polynomial:

   ∫₀¹ v^(2α−1)(1−v)/[q₁ + (q₂−q₁)v]^(2α+3) dv = (αq₁ + q₂)/[2α(α+1)(2α+1) q₁³ q₂^(2α+1)].

   The duplication formula then gives U_c = π^{3/2}μ₀f₀²[Γ(α+½)/Γ(α)](αq₁ + q₂)/(q₁³q₂^(2α+1)). For one sign of frequency the cross terms ε₀E·E + μ₀H·H cancel exactly, so the Re-pulse and the Im-pulse each carry U = U_c/2.
3. Finiteness. Near v = 0 (backward-propagating components) the integrand behaves as v^(2α−1), which is integrable for every α > 0. So "α ≥ 1 for finite energy" is **refuted** as a necessary condition. The scalar ∫|∇f|² would need α > ½. No condition we could construct gives α ≥ 1.
4. Momentum. The same integral with weight −cos θ gives cP_z/U = 2αq₁/(αq₁+q₂) − 1 with the sign of propagation restored, that is cP_z/U = (q₂ − αq₁)/(q₂ + αq₁). At α = 1 this is Lekner's (a − b)/(a + b) with a = q₂ and b = q₁. That is a normalisation-free match to a published result: **known** for α = 1.
5. Boost covariance and rest frame. Under a z-boost, τ = λτ′ and σ = σ′/λ, and D = r² + (q₁/λ + iτ′)(λq₂ − iσ′). So f maps to λ^(α−1) times the same family with (q₁/λ, λq₂). A ẑ-directed Hertz vector is invariant under a z-boost, so the fields follow. The Doppler factor λ² = (1+β)/(1−β) and the momentum formula agree, with the rest frame at q₂/q₁ = α. The rest energy is Mc² = U·2√(αq₁q₂)/(αq₁ + q₂), which is boost-invariant as it should be. For α > q₂/q₁ the net momentum is backward.
6. The H_z typo. From f = A^(α−1)D^(−α), with A = q₁ + iτ and Q = q₂ − iσ, applying Shen et al.'s own operator 4∂_τ∂_σ = ∂²_z − c⁻²∂²_t gives

   H_z = −4αf₀A^(α−1)[αr² − AQ]/D^(α+2).

   Printed eq. 6 minus this equals −4αf₀(α−1)A^(α−2)(q₂ − q₁ − 2iz)/D^(α+1), which is non-zero for α ≠ 1. On the axis at the focus, printed/correct = α − (α−1)q₂/q₁, which is −75 for α = 5 and q₂ = 20q₁. Checks:
   - At α = 5, our field satisfies ∇·H = 0 and Faraday's law to finite-difference accuracy, and eq. 6 fails ∇·H by a factor of 167 at (r, z, ct) = (0.7, 0.4, 0.3)q₁.
   - Counting zeros of Re H_z on the axis for z ∈ [0, 80q₁] at t = 0, our H_z drops from 4 to 2 between α = 5.5 and 5.6. That is the feature Shen et al. report in their Fig. 6. Printed eq. 6 gives 3 and 3.

   So their numerics used the correct field, and eq. 6 and (C3) are misprints.
7. Meron polarity. As r → ∞ in a plane at time t:
   - Re H_r ≈ 2α(α−1)f₀ Im(A^(α−2)) r^(−2α−1);
   - Re H_z ≈ −4α²f₀ Re(A^(α−1)) r^(−2α−2).

   Unless α = 1, or Im(A^(α−2)) = 0 (which holds identically for α = 2 at all planes, and otherwise only on isolated planes), the field turns radial at infinity. Then cos β(∞) = 0 and Shen et al.'s polarity is ±½, not ±1. Script results:
   - α = 20, q₂ = 20q₁: p = +½, −½, +½, −½ at z/q₁ = 14, 10, 6, 3.5. These are the same signs as Shen et al.'s ±1. The radial take-over sets in beyond r ≈ 34–127q₁, where the field is weak (prototype run).
   - α = 5: ±½.
   - α = 1 and α = 2: integers.
8. On-axis decay. At r = 0, H_z = 4αf₀/[A²Q^(α+1)] and E_θ = 0. At fixed τ, |H_z| ∝ |σ|^−(α+1) ≈ (2ct)^−(α+1). Status: **derived here**, elementary.

### Verdict
- **Observability (1–4).** The question is settled, and mostly not by us. Fernandez-Corbaton et al. proved the single-frequency no-go, and it stands. Our additions are clean but elementary:
  - p and T are each unconstrained (not merely "inseparable");
  - with a rigid pattern and known drive, broadband data do separate them exactly;
  - with a deforming pattern nothing does, at the very order where T lives;
  - overlapping or interior probes do.

  Together they reconcile the camps: the Southampton separations work only inside the rigid-source model, and Canós Valero et al.'s interior maps sit outside the theorem. This could be a short comment or pedagogical note (AJP/EJP level). It is not a strong research paper, and Nanz's 2016 book (not opened) may already contain it.
- **Mie (5).** Useful experimental numbers and a neat loss-floor identity, but routine Mie sensitivity analysis. Not a paper. The physics point is that for c-Si at 550 nm a 10⁻³ electric-dipole residual is impossible, whatever the fabrication.
- **Supertoroidal (6–9).** The strongest material: exact, checked to machine precision, and correcting a Nat. Commun. paper on three points (finite-energy range, H_z formula, integer skyrmion labels). It could be a short paper or comment, "Exact energy, momentum and rest frame of supertoroidal light pulses", provided Ziolkowski 1989, Hellwarth & Nouchi 1996 and Lekner's book are checked first.

### Strongest honest claim
For the supertoroidal family f₀/[(q₁ + iτ)(s + q₂)^α], the TE pulse carries energy U = (π^{3/2}/2)μ₀f₀²[Γ(α+½)/Γ(α)](αq₁+q₂)/(q₁³q₂^(2α+1)) and momentum cP_z = U(q₂ − αq₁)/(q₂ + αq₁). It is finite for every α > 0 and at rest when q₂ = αq₁, and the family is boost-covariant. All of this is verified numerically to 10⁻¹². For the toroidal dipole, the honest strongest statement is a reconciliation, not a discovery: T is identifiable exactly when the current pattern is rigid and its drive known, and never otherwise.
