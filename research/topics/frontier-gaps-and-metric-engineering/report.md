# Pulsars exclude Puthoff's theory, not his metric

H. E. Puthoff's "Advanced Space Propulsion Based on Vacuum (Spacetime Metric) Engineering" ([arXiv:1204.2184](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.2184); JBIS 63, 82–89, 2010; DIRD 15) prints no polarizable-vacuum K and no numbers of its own. It "engineers" the metric by assuming g₀₀ > 1 and |g₁₁| < 1 around a craft. Its Table 1 is seven correct identities for static observers, and its "engineered" column is exactly the exterior of a **negative mass**. A standard identity for the lapse forbids g₀₀ > 1 anywhere for ordinary matter and fields. The field route he cites, the Levi-Civita effect, moves K the wrong way by only 1.03×10⁻³⁸ for 1 T over 1 m. A 10⁻¹⁸ clock speed-up 10 m from the centre needs **6.7×10⁹ kg of negative mass** in both GR and PV, and the craft narratives contradict the model and each other. His polarizable-vacuum (PV) *theory*, K = exp(2GM/rc²) with Dicke's scalar dynamics, passes every first post-Newtonian test but is excluded. It radiates **2/3 of GR's binary-pulsar power**, which is 203σ from Hulse–Taylor and about 5000σ from the Double Pulsar ([Ibison 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0302273)). It has only a scalar polarisation, disfavoured by a Bayes factor above 1000. And, by a derivation in these notes, it has no frame dragging. The exponential *metric* is different: read in GR as a horizonless wormhole sourced by a phantom scalar, it is alive. Its shadow is 4.63% larger than Schwarzschild's, which puts it 1.0–1.6σ from the EHT's Sgr A* results. The strong field offers five more tests: a shadow measured to 1.5% (3σ), the strong-lensing offset b̄ = −0.4658 against −0.4002, an ISCO frequency 6.9% lower, a test-field ringdown 7.7% lower, and a 1% neutron-star redshift. The warp-drive and wormhole literature since 1994 agrees on two points: every superluminal shift-only drive violates the null energy condition, and no experiment has measured a metric distortion. White's interferometer, for instance, looked for 10⁻⁷ where GR predicts 3×10⁻⁴⁵. Every Phase 1 and Phase 3 gap in the crates now has a method, a primary source and a test value, and most test values were reproduced. The two large items are a Leaver quasinormal-mode solver, which matches Berti's digits to 10⁻¹⁶, and a TOV Love-number solver. The same checks found eleven defects in the shipped crates and ten errors in earlier research, listed in the defects section below.

All notes are in [notes/](notes/). Every computed number comes from a script beside its note. Seven black-hole scripts had no saved output, so they were re-run for this report on 2026-10-25 under the resource cap in the user's rules, and their outputs are now saved next to them. The other folders' saved outputs were checked against the notes' numbers but not re-run. The "Reproduced" columns below use four words:
- **yes:** a published number was recomputed by a script;
- **identity:** an exact relation was verified;
- **ours:** no published counterpart exists, so the value is a regression value;
- **no:** the published value was not reproduced, and the reason is given.

Status labels are **established**, **speculative**, **fringe** and **excluded**, following the repository's rule to compute both ways and label rather than dismiss.

The radiation test is decisive, and it was published in 2003. It needs no strong-field assumption, because the factor-2/3 deficit sits in a quantity measured to 10⁻³–10⁻⁴. Ibison himself concluded that "PV is incompatible with observation" and suggested that a vacuum anisotropy or the full Yilmaz tensor theory might restore two transverse polarisations. No one has computed the latter. Misner's "Yilmaz cancels Newton" targets Yilmaz's field equations, not Puthoff's action, and that dispute is unresolved in the literature read ([Misner](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9504050); [Alley, Aschan & Yilmaz](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9506082)).

### Five strong-field routes test the geometry, and each says what it needs

The user's instinct that the strong field must offer a test is right. There are five routes, and each states what it needs.

**1. Shadow size.** Static PV's critical impact parameter is 2e GM/c² = 5.436564, against √27 = 5.196152. For Sgr A* that is 55.72 µas against 53.25 µas with the VLTI mass, or 53.44 against 51.08 µas with Keck's. Telling them apart at 3σ needs σ_δ ≈ 0.015 (0.009 for 5σ), with spin marginalised; today σ_δ is about 0.09. Two facts tilt the current data against PV: every δ posterior centres on negative values, and spin can only shrink a Kerr shadow. But whether a *spinning* PV object shrinks too is unknown. Makukov & Mychelkin built an exact rotating Papapetrou metric and left its shadow "for later studies" ([arXiv:1809.05290](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.05290)). That is the single most useful missing computation. Psaltis's truncated-2PN shadow formula gives only +1.85% for PV, so most of PV's +4.63% comes from beyond 2PN, and a post-Newtonian fit cannot stand in for the exact metric ([Psaltis et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01055)).

**2. Photon rings.** ā = 1 in both metrics, so successive subrings shrink by e^π in both, and their ratios cannot tell PV from GR. Only the critical curve (+4.6%) and b̄ can. The shift in b̄ moves the n ≥ 1 subrings' offsets by e^{Δb̄} = 0.936, about 6%. Bozza's observables for Sgr A* are θ∞ = 26.627 µas (GR) against 27.859 µas (PV), s = 33.32 against 32.65 nas, and r = 6.822 mag in both. BHEX's geometric-only n = 1 ring gives the mass-to-distance ratio to about 10%, which is not enough; with source modelling it might be ([BHEX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09498)).

**3. Ringdown.** It needs the perturbation equations of PV dynamics, which have never been computed. The proxy (GR's spin-2 field on the static exponential geometry) rings 7.7% low, which would breach GW250114's bound. But remnants spin, no rotating-PV modes exist, and PV theory radiates a scalar, so the proxy tests a hybrid rather than PV.

**4. ISCO and accretion.** Ω_ISCO is 6.92% lower, so f_GW(ISCO) for 10 M☉ is 409.3 Hz against 439.7 Hz. The efficiency is 5.479% against 5.719%. This is degenerate with spin unless the spin is measured independently. Turimov et al.'s abstract says PV's efficiency is higher; the recomputation says lower, and their paper was not opened.

**5. Neutron stars.** The surface redshift differs by 1% at GM/(c²R) = 0.172, but PV has no published stellar interior, so the mass–radius relation is unconstrained.

Two things are not tests. Echo searches are not, because PV predicts a single barrier with no cavity. The EHT's arguments against horizonless surfaces are not either: PV has a photon sphere and no surface, and infalling matter crosses the throat.

Two published comparisons need correcting:
- **The excluded wormhole is not PV.** The "exponential" Morris–Thorne wormhole that the EHT and Vagnozzi et al. exclude puts e^{−2M/r} in the *areal* radius (r_sh = eM). PV puts it in the *isotropic* radius (r_sh = 2eM), so that exclusion does not apply to PV. EHT Table 4 also lists that wormhole's κ₁ and κ₂ as −1 and −2/3, where the EHT's own convention gives +1 and +2/3: this looks like a sign slip.
- **"5% larger" is a rounding.** The literature's "5% larger" shadow rounds 4.63%, and "over 5%" ([Mychelkin et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15391)) is not supported by 2e/√27.

A laboratory analogue can also verify the mathematics, though not nature. PV's null geodesics are exactly rays in a medium with n = e^{2a/r} ([Boonserm et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03781)). A graded-index waveguide like Sheng et al.'s ([arXiv:1309.7706](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.7706)) could reproduce b_c = 2e·a, b̄ = −0.4658 and the 4x + 4πx² + 48x³ series. It is easier than a Schwarzschild analogue, because n stays finite outside r = 0.

- **White's "Warp Field Mechanics 101" (2011)** proposed a He-Ne interferometer to see a "boost" of 1.0000001 over 1 cm ([NTRS 20110015936](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20110015936)). Computed both ways:
  - *In White's own model*, that boost needs v_s = 0.0299c, and a 0.5 cm Alcubierre bubble at that speed needs −3.4×10²⁰ kg of negative energy.
  - *In standard GR*, a generous capacitor field perturbs the metric by **3×10⁻⁴⁵**, 38 orders below the target.
  - White's rule ⟨v_eff⟩ = γv_i is not a GR result, since Alcubierre's interior moves at v_s whatever the craft's prior speed **[fringe]**.
- **The 2014 Eagleworks poster** reports hardware still being built and no measurement ([NTRS 20140005831](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140005831)).
- **Lee & Cleaver's critique** reaches the right conclusion, but their 1.92×10⁻²³ m Schwarzschild radius is 10²¹–10²³ too large ([arXiv:1407.7772](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.7772)).
- **White's 2013 energy chart cannot be reproduced** ([NTRS 20130011213](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20130011213)). For a 10 m bubble at v = 10, Alcubierre's integral has a floor of 3.7×10²⁸ kg over all wall thicknesses, and the chart's thick end sits 10^8.6 below it.
- **White et al. 2021** in EPJ C ([doi:10.1140/epjc/s10052-021-09484-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjc/s10052-021-09484-z)) compares the *shape* of a Casimir worldline density with an Alcubierre bubble, with no correlation coefficient and no data. Its own transient field reproduces (357 V/m and 0.71 mV at 4 µm). But setting the cavity's 1.7×10⁻⁶ J/m³ equal to a 1 µm bubble's requirement gives a speed of **1.7×10⁻²² m/s**. Because T⁰⁰ ∝ v²f′², a shape match carries no information about speed. Its full text was read only through a fetched summary.

No peer-reviewed laboratory claim of a warp bubble or wormhole was found.

The QNM solver's pitfalls are worth keeping beside the code:
- take Re u₁ < 0 in the tail;
- for m < 0, track the root with Re ω > 0;
- test only n ≤ 3, because overtones n ≥ 5 approach the algebraically special −2i;
- above j = 0.99, N grows as 1/√(1 − a²);
- Leaver's angular continued fraction without inversion picked the wrong ℓ in 36 of 315 cases, which the spectral matrix avoids;
- Leaver and Berti use 2M = 1, while Cook and `qnm` use M = 1.

The Love solver's main pitfalls:
- Read's K_i are in c = 1 form, so p/c² = Kρ^Γ, despite a table caption in dyne/cm²;
- e- and µ-polytropes differ by 5–20% in k₂;
- n < 1 polytropes should be integrated in enthalpy.

## Conclusion

The question "can Puthoff's metric be tested in the strong field?" has a sharper answer than the earlier review gave. Yes, in five ways: shadow size, b̄, ISCO, ringdown and neutron stars. But those reach only the *geometry*. The *theory* that Puthoff wrote down was already falsified by a 2003 weak-field calculation of binary-pulsar radiation, which the DIRD review and the fringe report missed. This changes what the crates should say: `Metric::PolarizableVacuum` is a live, speculative geometry that sits 1–2σ from the EHT, sitting beside a function that shows why PV dynamics is excluded. The most useful missing physics is a rotating-PV shadow. Without it, no spin-marginalised EHT comparison is possible, and the 1.5% precision that would decide the question has no prediction to test. For metric engineering, the paper's lasting content is a theorem it never states. Its engineered column is a negative-mass exterior, so every claimed craft signature needs a violation of the strong energy condition, and the cheapest detector of such a region is a gravimeter rather than a clock: 10⁶ times more sensitive at 10 m.

The defects this time are errors of transcription rather than of algorithm, unlike the unstable forms in the earlier report's D1–D10. They are misprints copied from a source table, an error column read at face value, a constant taken through a secondary paper with its tide correction silently removed, a lens count, and a squared Einstein radius. All of them survived tests that checked the code against the same source it was copied from. Each was found by an independent route: an exact Leaver solver against BCW06, a primary PDS label against a review's table, a lens equation solved numerically against a doc comment. The discipline that follows is to test each transcribed table against a second, independent computation or primary source before shipping it, and to keep a chain of custody (source, tide system, normalisation, sigma level) for every physical constant a test asserts.
