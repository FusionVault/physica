# User needs and tool gaps for satellite-gravimetry data (GRACE, GRACE-FO, GOCE, Swarm, MAGIC/NGGM), as of October 2026

Research date: 2026-10-24. Method: web search and fetch, plus `gh` reads of the gravity-toolkit and SHTOOLS issue trackers and a crates.io registry query. Note: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so journalism/insurance, STAC and "Global Water Monitor" questions are only partly covered (see Gaps). Items dated before 2024 are marked [older].

## 1. Who uses GRACE/GRACE-FO data and what workflows and outputs they need

### Takeaway
The main operational users today are drought monitoring (NASA GRACE-DA indicators feeding the U.S. Drought Monitor), groundwater and climate services (Copernicus C3S, since 2025), and water managers who need basin or aquifer-scale numbers. They want basin time series with stated uncertainty, groundwater split out from total storage, drought percentiles or indices, and lower latency. The main barriers they report are coarse resolution (~300 km), ~40–60 day latency, results that are hard to interpret, and too little trust or transparency.

### Cited Findings
**Operational drought products (US-centric, model-assimilated)**
- NASA Goddard "generate groundwater and soil moisture drought indicators each week" from GRACE-FO TWS assimilated into a land-surface model. Grids are 0.125° for CONUS and 0.25° for global land. Variables are groundwater, root-zone and surface soil-moisture percentiles, with 30/60/90-day forecasts driven by GEOS-5 seasonal forecasts. Downloads are images (PNG/PDF) and "binary data files". The page does not mention GeoTIFF or NetCDF and states no latency. — [NASA/NDMC GRACE-based drought indicators](https://nasagrace.unl.edu/)
- The GES DISC catalogue lists GRACE-DA-DM U.S. V4.0 (7-day, 0.125°, NLDAS-2 forcing, RL06 GRACE/GRACE-FO), superseding V2.0. Percentiles are relative to a 1948–2014 (CONUS) or 1948–2012 (global) period of record. — [Earthdata catalog GRACEDADM_CLSM0125US_7D V4.0](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/ges-disc-gracedadm-clsm0125us-7d-4.0); [data.gov global V3.0 entry](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/groundwater-and-soil-moisture-conditions-from-grace-and-grace-fo-data-assimilation-l4-7-da)
- NASA says drought monitors previously lacked objective information on deep soil moisture and groundwater, which is the gap GRACE filled. — [GRACE-FO news: GRACE-FO Will Help Monitor Droughts](https://gracefo.jpl.nasa.gov/news/129/grace-fo-will-help-monitor-droughts/); [NTRS: GRACE-Assimilated Drought Indicators for the USDM](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180002563) [older, 2018]
- USGS found major differences in 2001–2020 trends between well-based and GRACE-DADM groundwater metrics in some CONUS regions, partly because of resolution. It concludes that GRACE can support, but not replace, well monitoring. — [USGS: Groundwater drought in the United States](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/groundwater-drought-united-states-spatial-and-temporal-variability); [USGS news](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/new-england-water-science-center/news/usgs-investigates-groundwater-drought-metrics-across)

**Drought indices from GRACE alone**
- GRACE-DSI (UC Irvine) is a monthly drought severity index built from GRACE TWS. The original record ran 2002–2014 and was being extended with GRACE-FO. It agreed with PDSI and the USDM over CONUS. — [UCI drought data page](https://www.ess.uci.edu/~velicogna/drought_data.php); [J. Climate abstract](https://scholarworks.umt.edu/ntsg_pubs/384) [older]
- GSTM 2026 (Potsdam, 13–15 Oct 2026): CNES 10-day GRACE/GRACE-FO solutions plus GRACE-DSI capture major droughts and floods and agree with in-situ drought indices (Garnier, GSTM2026-20). Breakpoint (BFAST) analysis dates the onset of the Danube storage drought to about 2015, with groundwater breaking first in 2013 (Boergens, GSTM2026-10). — [GSTM2026 Session B.4 Hydrology](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/GSTM2026/session/59415)

**Groundwater and climate services (Europe)**
- G3P (Global Gravity-based Groundwater Product) subtracts other storage compartments from Copernicus products from GRACE/GRACE-FO TWS. It is a 0.5° NetCDF in mm EWH that excludes Greenland and Antarctica (v1.12 runs to Sept 2023). GFZ says the 2020–2023 prototype moved to operational use in C3S at ECMWF in January 2025. — [GFZ G3P page](https://www.gfz.de/en/section/earth-system-modelling/projects/g3p); [Space4Water G3P](https://space4water.org/project-mission-initiative-community-portal/global-gravity-based-groundwater-product)
- GSTM 2026: a new C3S ECV service, "Terrestrial Water Storage and Groundwater", delivers Climate Data Records for TWSA and groundwater storage change on the Climate Data Store. It targets climate-adaptation and water-management users who "need products with defined uncertainty and provenance". The authors name the near-absence of in-situ reference data at GRACE's native resolution as a key gap (Haas, GSTM2026-97). — [GSTM2026 Session B.6 Towards the Operational Use of Satellite Gravimetry](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/GSTM2026/session/59417)
- The CNES-funded SAGSA project now produces L2 to L4 GRACE/GRACE-FO products (gridded and SH, hydrology/ocean/glaciology) on monthly or twice-yearly updates via the DATATERRA hubs and the CNES GEODES portal (Thoumieux, GSTM2026-70). — [GSTM2026 Session B.6](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/GSTM2026/session/59417)
- The MAGIC Mission Requirements Document names Copernicus EFAS, GloFAS and the European Drought Observatory as candidate operational users. It says TWS was under review to become a GCOS ECV, and that "reliable data delivery, stable data quality and short latency are the main requirements". — [ESA MAGIC MRD, Issue 1.0, 30 Oct 2020, §7.1](https://www.asi.it/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/MAGIC_NGGM_MCDO_MRD_v1_0-signed2.pdf) [older]

**Water managers and practitioners**
- Xu et al. 2023 (J. Hydrology 623:129845, Famiglietti group) surveyed scientists and practitioners. They found a mismatch between what researchers supply and what practitioners want, and recommended better communication, greater accuracy and transparency to build trust, and more solution-oriented research. Only the abstract was accessible (full text blocked with HTTP 403). — [ScienceDirect abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169423007874); [IIASA record](https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/18908)
- The same group's EGU22 abstract says coarse resolution limits use at the scales where water decisions are made, and that the data are hard for policymakers and end-users to interpret. — [EGU22-10542](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU22/EGU22-10542.html) [older]
- A 2025 study says GRACE-FO's coarse resolution limits direct use for local groundwater management, but it still gives useful indicators for deep-aquifer extraction areas. — [MDPI Water 17(24):3572](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/17/24/3572)
- NASA ARSET runs a training course, "Monitoring Groundwater Changes for Water Resources Management", which shows a capacity-building audience of agency staff. — [Earthdata ARSET training](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/learn/trainings/monitoring-groundwater-changes-water-resources-management)
- NASA's applications pages list societal-benefit areas, including drought monitoring and water management. — [GRACE Tellus applications overview](https://grace.jpl.nasa.gov/applications/overview/); [drought monitoring](https://grace.jpl.nasa.gov/applications/drought-monitoring/)

**Research workflows in hydrology at GSTM 2026 (what the science community currently builds)**
- Downscaling: daily 25 km CONUS TWSA, 2002–2024, from a physics-informed deep network (Uz, GSTM2026-78). A random forest gap-fills and downscales to 0.1° with mass-conserving bias correction (Solovey, -34). A U-Net downscales real and simulated NGGM/MAGIC TWSA and groundwater for extreme-event indicators (Goracci, -4). — [GSTM2026 B.4](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/GSTM2026/session/59415)
- Counter-evidence: a 0.5° downscaled product "added little" for estimating specific yield across India (Suryawanshi, -79). — [GSTM2026 B.4](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/GSTM2026/session/59415)
- Separating storage compartments: monthly storage for 14,000 lakes (1992–2026) is computed to remove lake water from GRACE signals (Gaastra, -46). Drainable storage is separated from snow, lake, wetland and reservoir storage across three mascon solutions (Sobouti, -47). Groundwater accounts for about 65% of Colorado River storage losses (Abdelmohsen, -92). — [GSTM2026 B.4](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/GSTM2026/session/59415)
- Gutknecht (GSTM2026-80) compared water-budget components for 30+ European catchments across many products and warns against "cherry-picking" datasets. — [GSTM2026 B.4](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/GSTM2026/session/59415)
- An SLR+GRACE reconstruction back to 1995 is used to benchmark hydrological models (Gałdyn, -63). — [GSTM2026 B.4](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/GSTM2026/session/59415)

**Public and media communication**
- GFZ issued an annual Germany water-storage balance from GRACE-FO for World Water Day (21 Mar 2024): Germany was about 10 Gt below the long-term mean and Europe about 100 Gt below its 2002 average. It used monthly deficit maps, an animated GIF, a "water storage spiral", downloadable captioned figures, and the new portal globalwaterstorage.info for journalists, policymakers and the public. — [GFZ press release](https://www.gfz.de/en/press/news/details/jahresbilanz-2023-des-gesamtwasserspeichers-in-deutschland)
- The GravIS paper (ESSD 2025) explicitly targets scientists without geodetic expertise as well as "students, media, and policymakers". — [Dahle et al. 2025, ESSD 17:611](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/611/2025/essd-17-611-2025.html)

### Inferences
- The highest-value outputs for non-geodesists are basin or region time series with uncertainty, drought percentiles or indices, and groundwater-separated storage. The operational US products are model-assimilated and US-centred. A transparent, observation-only global index (GRACE-DSI or TWS percentiles against a 2002–present climatology) for any region is available only as research code or the CNES 10-day product.
- Many users choose between, or combine, several solutions (CSR/JPL/GSFC mascons, COST-G, GFZ). Mascon solutions differ more from each other than SH solutions do (see Q2), so side-by-side multi-solution comparison with spread is a real need, not a nice-to-have.
- Downscaling is popular in research but the evidence is mixed: the USGS study and the India specific-yield study both found limits. Tools should state the effective resolution honestly rather than sell finer grids.

### Gaps
- No formal, recent (2024–2026) NASA or ESA user survey of GRACE-FO users was found. The Xu et al. 2023 survey's sample size and detailed results were not accessible (403 error).
- Agriculture and insurance: no source was found that confirms GRACE-based index insurance or agricultural risk-transfer products. The one search returned only general satellite index-insurance material ([CGIAR](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/items/46c82a0f-7d41-4d09-8834-0b2b237ef1dd)), and the search budget ran out before a follow-up.
- Cryosphere and ocean user needs (sea-level budget, ocean bottom pressure) are covered only through the MAGIC MRD and the GravIS products, not through user studies.
- A claim that California DWR used GRACE/GRACE-FO data appeared in a search summary, but its source page was not opened, so it is not included as a finding.

## 2. Pain points with existing tools (gravity-toolkit, SHTOOLS, PO.DAAC/GRACE Tellus, mascons, GravIS, ICGEM, Earth Engine)

### Takeaway
The recurring pain points are fragile data access (PO.DAAC cloud migration broke scripted downloads repeatedly from 2021 to 2025), hard installation (Fortran/C toolchains, numpy 2, Windows, Apple silicon), unclear methodology (mascon inputs, low-degree replacement, GIA files), stale or narrow web tools (single-centre portals, outdated mascon versions, Earth Engine lag), no standard error covariance or Level-3 conventions, and an overloaded ICGEM calculator.

**Portals and services**
- ICGEM calculation service, fetched 2026-10-24: the page showed "Due to high load, no new calculations are accepted." It offers Gaussian filtering (half response, half transfer, 6-sigma), a gentle-cut low-pass, the four tide-system options and "Model from Series" (temporal) models. No API is mentioned. — [ICGEM calcgrid](https://icgem.gfz.de/calcgrid)
- GravIS (GFZ/AWI/TUD), described in ESSD Feb 2025. It offers Level-2B SH and Level-3 products: 1° TWS grids with uncertainty, leakage and background atmosphere; 50 km polar-stereographic ice grids; ocean bottom pressure; averages for the 100 largest basins and climate clusters; GeoJSON region geometries; linked map and time-series views, animation, and download of regional averages; and an open Python toolbox for user regions. Its stated limitations: results are reliable only over areas of at least 100,000 km², GIA errors are not included in the uncertainties, leakage corrections are provided but not applied by default, and most processing code is proprietary. It uses GFZ (lower latency) and COST-G chains only. — [Dahle et al. 2025, ESSD](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/611/2025/essd-17-611-2025.html); [GravIS portal](https://gravis.gfz-potsdam.de/)
- A referee on the GravIS preprint asked for CSR and JPL solutions to be added for comparison and for user-drawn polygon regions. This came from a search snippet; the final paper does not mention either feature. — [ESSD preprint essd-2024-347](https://essd.copernicus.org/preprints/essd-2024-347)
- The CCAR Mascon Visualization Tool (CU Boulder) offers click-or-coordinate mascon time series with trend and de-seasoning, using JPL RL06M **v02** and GSFC v02.4 mascons, which are outdated (the current JPL product is RL06.3Mv04). It offers no download and no uncertainty. — [CCAR about page](https://ccar.colorado.edu/grace/about.html)
- The NASA GRACE(-FO) Data Analysis Tool interpolates "all data" to a 1×1° grid "for computational reasons" for quick-look comparisons. — [GRACE Tellus DAT](https://grace.jpl.nasa.gov/data/data-analysis-tool/)
- Google Earth Engine: `NASA/GRACE/MASS_GRIDS_V04/MASCON` (JPL RL06.3Mv04) was listed as covering 2002-03-31 to **2024-09-30** when searched in October 2026, and V03 is deprecated. The non-CRI product does not correct coastal leakage and is "intended for expert use only". — [Earth Engine catalog V04](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/NASA_GRACE_MASS_GRIDS_V04_MASCON?authuser=0); [V03 deprecated](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/NASA_GRACE_MASS_GRIDS_V03_MASCON)
- PO.DAAC's GRACE-FO-OpenToolkit is community code, not official. It computes basin means from any shapefile and exports monthly GeoTIFFs from the mascon NetCDF. — [podaac/GRACE-FO-OpenToolkit](https://github.com/podaac/GRACE-FO-OpenToolkit/blob/main/README.md)

**Methodological pain points from the 2026 GRACE data-assimilation review**
- Springer et al. (HESS 30:985, 19 Feb 2026) found that "calibrated error covariances should be preferred" and that only full covariances capture spatial correlation, which is "particularly strong in the north–south direction". They report "larger differences between different mascon solutions compared to differences between different SH solutions" and that "a standard correction dataset is still missing". They recommend that providers supply "standardized Level-3 products and reliable error estimates" with GRACE-C and NGGM, and they call for community benchmarks and diagnostics similar to ESMValTool. — [Springer et al. 2026, HESS](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/30/985/2026/)
- SAGEA (Liu, Yang & Forootan, Computers & Geosciences 196:105825, Feb 2025) is a Python toolbox that puts post-processing and three error categories in one place: formal full-covariance propagation via Monte Carlo/ITSG SINEX, between-product discrepancy via three-cornered hat and triple collocation, and a third category. It is explicitly aimed at non-expert users. It is part of a wider suite with PyGLDA (assimilation) and SaGEA-Fluid. — [PyPI sagea](https://pypi.org/project/sagea/); [EGU26-17836](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-17836.html)

### Inferences
- Pure-Rust tools without Fortran, C or LAPACK and shipped as prebuilt wheels would remove the largest single class of SHTOOLS and gravity-toolkit complaints: installation, numpy 2, Windows/ARM, thread safety and array-layout copies.
- Users repeatedly need built-in, documented defaults: TN-13/TN-14 low-degree replacement, GIA model files, leakage handling and tide system. The user's gravity-field crate already reads TN-13/TN-14, so documenting those choices in plain language is a cheap win.
- A data-access layer that survives provider moves (PO.DAAC on-prem to cloud, CNES catalogue, ICGEM load-shedding) is a recurring unmet need. Holding the full COST-G archive locally or in an open mirror (the user has all 259 fields) shields users from these outages.
- No mainstream tool combines (a) multiple solutions, (b) user polygons, (c) uncertainty and (d) a current archive. GravIS lacks (a) and (b) in the portal, CCAR lacks (c) and (d), and Earth Engine lags on (d).

### Gaps
- The gravity-toolkit Discussions board, the ITSG/GROOPS issue tracker and the PO.DAAC forum were not read, because of search and tool-call limits.
- No published usability study of GRACE tools was found apart from the SAGEA paper's stated motivation.
- The current ICGEM capacity limits (point counts, maximum degree) are not stated on the page. The "high load" notice was observed only on 2026-10-24.

## 3. Operational and decision uses and what they need (latency, resolution, uncertainty)

### Takeaway
Operational users need, in rough order: continuity, then lower latency (from the current ~40–60 days down to days), then calibrated uncertainty and provenance, then higher resolution. Only weekly model-assimilated US drought indicators and the C3S climate records are operational today. Daily or quicklook gravity remains a demonstration for GRACE-FO; GRACE-C (launch December 2028) plans ~3-day quicklook. MAGIC targets sub-weekly sampling, ~100–260 km resolution and NRT Level-2 products.

### Cited Findings
- Latency: the MAGIC MRD says "The average latency for currently operational GRACE-FO is ~50 days" and that "Near-real time analysis… between few days and weeks is necessary for monitoring and forecasting hydrological extreme events". Its user priorities are: (1) at least 30-year continuous series, (2) higher resolution, (3) daily to weekly sampling, (4) latency reduced "from a few months to a few days and weeks". — [MAGIC MRD §2–3.1, Oct 2020](https://www.asi.it/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/MAGIC_NGGM_MCDO_MRD_v1_0-signed2.pdf) [older]
- MRD-110: the mission "shall be capable to deliver products in near-real time or longer latency periods (a few days)". MRD-170: "shall provide Level-2 near-real time global gravity field products". — [MAGIC MRD §4](https://www.asi.it/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/MAGIC_NGGM_MCDO_MRD_v1_0-signed2.pdf) [older]
- MRD Table 4 hydrology thresholds and targets in EWH, taken from IUGG requirements. Daily/weekly: threshold 600 km @ 3.2 cm, 300 km @ 5.9 cm, 280 km @ 6.0 cm; targets are 10× better. Monthly: 400 km @ 0.5 cm and 260 km @ 4.8 cm. Long-term: 350 km @ 0.1 cm/yr and 150 km @ 5.0 cm/yr. — [MAGIC MRD Table 4](https://www.asi.it/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/MAGIC_NGGM_MCDO_MRD_v1_0-signed2.pdf) [older]
- The MRD's Level-3 section names signal separation as "one of the most important and challenging tasks for the users". — [MAGIC MRD §6.4](https://www.asi.it/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/MAGIC_NGGM_MCDO_MRD_v1_0-signed2.pdf) [older]
- Simulated MAGIC configurations meet the 260 km/monthly hydrology threshold for more than 90% of 405 major basins. — [Pail et al./MAGIC impact, GJI 236:1288 (2024)](https://academic.oup.com/gji/article/236/3/1288/7473715)
- A 2026 HESS study (NGGM/MAGIC for precipitation over Europe) cites targets of sub-weekly sampling, ~100 km effective regional resolution and ~5–10 mm EWH accuracy. This comes from a search summary; the article was not opened. — [HESS 30:4969 (2026)](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/30/4969/2026/)
- Current latency: JPL's data-updates page's latest data entry (7 Nov 2024) announced RL06.3 Level-2/Level-3 fields with improved low-degree terms, and an ACX2 Level-1B product delivered in delayed mode with latency "~40-60 days". There were no 2025–2026 data notices on that page as of 2026-10-24. — [GRACE Tellus data updates](https://grace.jpl.nasa.gov/data/data-updates/)
- A 2026 chapter states that the "typical latency of about 2 months hampers near real-time assessment" of extreme events. — [Springer chapter, GRACE-FO line-of-sight gravity differences (2026)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/1345_2026_414)
- Low-latency research history: daily Kalman-filtered GRACE solutions tracked Ganges–Brahmaputra floods, but at the time they had months of delay [older, 2018] ([HESS 22:2867](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/22/2867/2018/)). EGSIEM aimed at a 5-day latency [older]. A Graz demonstrator produced daily solutions with 1-day latency from quicklook data ([TU Graz](https://graz.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/estimation-of-high-frequency-mass-variations-from-satellite-data-)). A sliding-window NRT approach reached daily degree/order-50 fields with latency down to 1 day ([GJI 2019](https://dx.doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggz084)) [older].
- GravIS future plans include possible near-real-time daily products from preliminary GRACE-FO data for flood and drought assessment. — [Dahle et al. 2025](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/611/2025/essd-17-611-2025.html)
- GSTM 2026: GRACE-C is in assembly, integration and testing, with launch in Dec 2028. Its quicklook latency is about 3 days, which motivates ML screening of solutions (Martinez, GSTM2026-54). ESA-SING provides 12 years of simulated 30-day and 5-day L2 SH and L3 EWH grids for GRACE-C-like, NGGM and MAGIC configurations (Schlaak, P6). GRATTIS launches NET 1 Feb 2027. Sub-monthly TWS from GRACE-C plus NGGM (Ghobadi-Far, -56) and wet-extreme detection (Middendorf, -69) are being assessed. — [GSTM2026 Session A.3](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/GSTM2026/session/59410)
- Continuity risk: sources disagree on GRACE-FO end of life. The CEOS database says September 2026 ([CEOS EO Handbook](https://database.eohandbook.com/database/missionsummary.aspx?missionID=686)); WMO OSCAR (updated 27 Aug 2026) says ≥2029 ([WMO OSCAR](https://space.oscar.wmo.int/satellites/view/grace_c_grace_fo)). DLR says GRACE-C timing aims for overlap with GRACE-FO ([DLR GRACE-C](https://www.dlr.de/en/rb/research-operation/missions/earth-observation-science/future-1/grace-c)). Search results also reported that GRACE-FO moved into a fuel-saving wide-pointing mode that suspended LRI ranging. That page was not opened, so this is unverified ([Wikipedia GRACE and GRACE-FO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRACE_and_GRACE-FO)).
- Uncertainty: C3S users "need products with defined uncertainty and provenance" (GSTM2026-97) ([B.6](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/GSTM2026/session/59417)). Assimilation users prefer calibrated full covariances ([HESS 2026 review](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/30/985/2026/)).
- The review expects that "low-latency periodically updated reanalyses and analyses from assimilation will become more relevant" and notes emerging daily SH and 5-daily mascon products. — [Springer et al. 2026](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/30/985/2026/)

### Inferences
- For an open toolset, the practical latency lever is not new satellite processing but being ready to ingest whatever cadence appears: monthly, 10-day CNES, daily ITSG/Kalman, GRACE-C quicklook, and ESA-SING simulated 5-day fields. Processing new fields automatically within hours of release also helps. Code that assumes "monthly" is a liability.
- The satgrav closed-loop simulator aligns with the active GSTM 2026 thread on future-mission performance (ESA-SING, FEATS-G, the ESA ESM 3.0 6-hourly model). Publishing L2/L3 outputs from it in the same formats as ESA-SING would let hydrologists test downstream tools on MAGIC-like data.
- If GRACE-FO ends before GRACE-C (the CEOS date), users will need gap-bridging products: reconstructions, SLR-based low-degree fields, and Swarm-derived fields. The SLR+GRACE back-to-1995 work at GSTM 2026 shows demand for long, gap-free series.

### Gaps
- No authoritative 2026 latency figure for COST-G combined monthly fields was found. COST-G is known to lag single-centre solutions; GravIS states its GFZ chain has "lower latency" than COST-G.
- GRACE-DA product latency and the exact USDM ingestion workflow were not confirmed: the NASA/UNL page states no latency.
- The "Global Groundwater Monitor" and GRACE-based "flood potential" products named in the brief could not be checked: the search budget ran out.
- Swarm/GOCE user needs (gap-filling, static geoid users) were not researched separately.

## 4. Expected interoperability (NetCDF/CF, Zarr, STAC, COG, xarray, WASM, PyO3) and gaps in the Rust/WASM geoscience ecosystem

### Takeaway
Users expect NetCDF (the format of the JPL mascons, G3P and C3S products), GeoTIFF for GIS users, GeoJSON regions, Python/xarray workflows, and cloud access via earthaccess. Rust now has strong Zarr (zarrs, icechunk), STAC (stac-rs) and new pure-Rust NetCDF/HDF5 readers, which make in-browser NetCDF reading feasible. The Rust ecosystem has essentially no gravity, geoid or spherical-harmonic tooling apart from the user's crates and simple EGM lookup crates.

### Cited Findings
- JPL mascons (RL06.1Mv03 and RL06.3Mv04) are distributed as a single NetCDF file in cm EWH on an equal-area 3° (4,551-mascon) layout. V04 includes uncertainty. — [PO.DAAC DOI 10.5067/TEMSC-3MJ63](https://www.doi.org/10.5067/TEMSC-3MJ63); [Earth Engine V04](https://developers.google.cn/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/NASA_GRACE_MASS_GRIDS_V04_MASCON?hl=en)
- G3P is distributed as NetCDF (0.5°, mm EWH). — [GFZ G3P](https://www.gfz.de/en/section/earth-system-modelling/projects/g3p)
- GravIS publishes region geometries as GeoJSON via GFZ Data Services and offers a Python toolbox for user regions. — [Dahle et al. 2025](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/611/2025/essd-17-611-2025.html)
- PO.DAAC community tooling exports GeoTIFF per month and takes shapefile masks. — [GRACE-FO-OpenToolkit](https://github.com/podaac/GRACE-FO-OpenToolkit/blob/main/README.md)
- Users are moving to `earthaccess` for Earthdata cloud downloads; see gravity-toolkit #161 (2025). — [#161](https://github.com/tsutterley/gravity-toolkit/issues/161)
- Users need `.gfc` (ICGEM) and `shm` (PO.DAAC/ISDC) formats both supported. — [gravity-toolkit #152](https://github.com/tsutterley/gravity-toolkit/issues/152)
- zarrs 0.23.7 is "fully up-to-date and conformant with the Zarr 3.1 specification". zarrs-python provides a Rust-backed codec pipeline for zarr-python, and R bindings (Rzarrs) exist. — [docs.rs zarrs](https://docs.rs/crate/zarrs/latest); [zarrs GitHub org](https://github.com/zarrs); [Pangeo discourse thread](https://discourse.pangeo.io/t/creating-bindings-for-python-julia-r-into-a-rust-zarr-implementation-zarrs/5446)
- A crates.io registry query on 2026-10-24 returned: `icechunk` 2.3.0 (transactional Zarr storage); `netcdf` 0.12.1 (bindings to the C library); pure-Rust `netcdf-reader` 0.9.3 and `hdf5-reader` 0.9.3 (read-only, "no C dependencies", updated 2026-10-21); `netcdf3` 0.6.1 (pure Rust, NetCDF-3); `grib` 0.20.2; `cdshealpix` 0.10.1; `egm2008` 0.1.0 and `egm96` 0.3.2 (geoid lookups); `satkit` 0.24.1 (matched "icgem"). The user's crates showed small download counts: geopotential 0.1.1 (40), gravity-field 0.1.0 (8), geo5d 0.4.0 (47). — [crates.io netcdf-reader](https://crates.io/crates/netcdf-reader); [hdf5-reader](https://crates.io/crates/hdf5-reader); [icechunk](https://crates.io/crates/icechunk); [egm2008](https://crates.io/crates/egm2008); [satkit](https://crates.io/crates/satkit); [geopotential](https://crates.io/crates/geopotential)
- The awesome-georust list (2026) has entries for netCDF and HDF5 bindings, zarrs, GeoTIFF/GDAL, stac-rs and pgstac-rs, maplibre-rs, and PyO3 tools (rusterize, utiles). It has no entries for gravity, geoid, spherical harmonics or GRIB. — [awesome-georust](https://github.com/pka/awesome-georust)
- OxiGeo (0.2.x) is a young, self-described pure-Rust GDAL alternative with GeoTIFF, NetCDF and Zarr drivers and WASM bindings. Esri staff have said there are no plans for Rust geospatial capabilities. A FOSS4G 2025 workshop framed Rust as emerging for scientific computing. — [oxigeo docs](https://docs.rs/crate/oxigeo/0.2.4); [Esri community](https://community.esri.com/t5/arcgis-runtime-sdk-for-net-questions/geospatial-engine-for-spatial-analytics-being/td-p/1044124); [FOSS4G 2025 talk](https://talks.osgeo.org/foss4g-2025/talk/RDZMQP/)
- SHTOOLS #520 and #385 show the cost of the Python-to-Fortran boundary: per-call layout copies and thread-unsafe FFTW planning. — [#520](https://github.com/SHTOOLS/SHTOOLS/issues/520); [#385](https://github.com/SHTOOLS/SHTOOLS/issues/385)

### Inferences
- Python bindings via PyO3/maturin with abi3 wheels are probably the highest-leverage interoperability move. Python/xarray is where users are (gravity-toolkit, pyshtools, SAGEA, PyGLDA, GravIS toolbox), and pure-Rust wheels avoid the Fortran/numpy pain.
- For the web viewer, the pure-Rust `netcdf-reader`/`hdf5-reader` crates plus zarrs/icechunk make a WASM build that reads standard files (or a Zarr store of the 259 fields) in the browser plausible. This is a gap no current portal fills, since GravIS, CCAR and DAT are server-rendered.
- Outputs should be CF-compliant NetCDF (lat/lon, time with month bounds, units "m" or "mm" EWH, an uncertainty variable via `ancillary_variables`), with GeoTIFF/COG per month for GIS users and GeoJSON basin time-series CSV/JSON for non-coders.
- STAC: stac-rs exists, but no evidence was found that GRACE users ask for STAC, so it is low priority.

### Gaps
- Search budget ran out before checking (a) whether any GRACE Level-3 product is published as Zarr/kerchunk or via STAC (PO.DAAC cloud, Earthdata), (b) the maturity and WASM-compatibility of `netcdf-reader`/`hdf5-reader`, and (c) Pangeo user demand for Zarr GRACE stores.
- What `satkit`'s gravity model support covers (degree, time-variable terms) was not checked; it only matched the crates.io keyword "icgem".

## 5. Visualisations that users and educators value

### Takeaway
The proven pattern is a linked map and basin time-series dashboard with animation, uncertainty and downloadable regional series, as in GravIS. Media and the public respond to annual national balances, "water storage spirals", deficit animations and captioned downloadable figures. The gaps are multi-solution comparison, user-defined regions, drought-category or percentile maps outside the US, uncertainty bands that non-experts can read, and an up-to-date archive (CCAR and Earth Engine lag).

### Cited Findings
- GravIS portal features: zoomable maps; time-series plots; animation of gridded data; variable and region drop-downs with descriptions; linked map-region and time-series clicks; download of regional averages; GFZ and COST-G shown by default. — [Dahle et al. 2025, ESSD](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/611/2025/essd-17-611-2025.html)
- GFZ's public communication used monthly deficit maps, an animated GIF, deficits since 2018 for Germany and Central Europe, a long-term series from 2002, an animated "water storage spiral" for Europe, Greenland/Antarctica ice maps, and globalwaterstorage.info with fact sheets and dossiers for journalists and policymakers. — [GFZ press release, 21 Mar 2024](https://www.gfz.de/en/press/news/details/jahresbilanz-2023-des-gesamtwasserspeichers-in-deutschland)
- The NASA/UNL drought-indicator viewer shows weekly percentile maps by continent with 30/60/90-day forecasts, as PNG/PDF downloads. — [nasagrace.unl.edu](https://nasagrace.unl.edu/)
- The CCAR mascon tool offers click-to-time-series with a time-window slider, trend overlay and de-seasoning (annual and semi-annual removal), but on outdated mascon versions. — [CCAR](https://ccar.colorado.edu/grace/about.html)
- The NASA DAT gives quick-look comparisons on a 1° grid. — [GRACE Tellus DAT](https://grace.jpl.nasa.gov/data/data-analysis-tool/)
- A GravIS referee asked for comparison with CSR/JPL and for user-drawn polygons. — [essd-2024-347](https://essd.copernicus.org/preprints/essd-2024-347)
- GSTM 2026 C3S abstract: the service provides "visualisations … for decision makers" alongside the CDS data. — [B.6](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/GSTM2026/session/59417)
- Practitioners find GRACE results hard to interpret and call for better communication and transparency. — [EGU22-10542](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU22/EGU22-10542.html); [Xu et al. 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169423007874)

### Inferences

**Overall prioritized list (synthesis across all five questions; ranking is the researcher's judgement from the evidence above)**

1. **Region and basin time series with uncertainty for any polygon (GeoJSON/shapefile upload or drawn in the browser), across several solutions, showing the spread between them.** Who needs it: water managers, C3S-style climate users, researchers avoiding "cherry-picking", journalists. Why: GravIS lacks CSR/JPL and drawn polygons in the portal; CCAR is outdated; Earth Engine lags; mascon solutions differ significantly from each other (HESS 2026). The user's crates already have synthesis, filtering and COST-G data; the missing parts are polygon averaging with leakage/scaling options and covariance-propagated or month-to-month-noise uncertainty.
2. **Standard, documented Level-3 outputs:** CF NetCDF plus GeoTIFF/COG plus CSV/JSON series, with uncertainty variables, provenance (solution, filter, GIA model, TN-13/14 choices) and version change logs. Who: assimilation modellers, C3S/Copernicus, GIS users. Why: HESS 2026 calls for "standardized Level-3 products and reliable error estimates"; C3S users need "defined uncertainty and provenance".
3. **Python bindings (PyO3, prebuilt wheels with no Fortran or C).** Who: the whole Python GRACE community. Why: install, numpy 2, Windows/ARM and thread-safety failures dominate the SHTOOLS and gravity-toolkit trackers.
4. **Drought and extremes layer that works globally:** GRACE-DSI, percentiles against a 2002–present climatology, drought categories, onset and breakpoint detection, and de-seasoned anomalies. Who: drought monitors outside the US, NGOs, media. Why: GRACE-DA is model-based and US-focused; GRACE-DSI is monthly research data; the CNES 10-day DSI is new (GSTM 2026).
5. **Cadence-agnostic ingestion and low-latency readiness:** monthly, 10-day, daily and GRACE-C quicklook (~3 days), plus simulated NGGM/MAGIC L2/L3 (ESA-SING format). Process new fields automatically on release. Who: flood and drought early-warning (EFAS, GloFAS, EDO), MAGIC preparatory science. Why: MAGIC MRD latency targets; GRACE-C quicklook plans.
6. **Robust data access and an open mirror of the archive** (all 259 COST-G fields, with ICGEM/ISDC/PO.DAAC readers tolerant of endpoint changes). Why: repeated PO.DAAC migration breakage and ICGEM "high load" refusals.
7. **Browser (WASM) computation:** user-chosen filter radius and destriping, recomputed client-side from SH coefficients, with NetCDF/Zarr read in the browser. Who: educators, students, analysts without Python. Why: current portals offer fixed, server-side products only. Pure-Rust NetCDF/HDF5 readers and zarrs make this feasible.
8. **Groundwater separation workflow** (TWS minus soil moisture, snow, surface water, lakes and reservoirs from GLDAS/ERA5-Land/lake datasets), with stated uncertainty. Who: groundwater managers. Why: this is the top practitioner interest (Xu 2023, G3P, many GSTM 2026 abstracts). It needs ancillary data, so it is more work.
9. **Media and education visuals:** anomaly animations, a "water storage spiral" or stripes, annual national or basin balances in Gt with everyday comparisons (Lake Constance ≈ 48 Gt), and captioned figure export. Who: journalists, educators, outreach teams. Why: GFZ's World Water Day material is the model to follow.
10. **Expert or differentiating features:** SH Hessians and gradients (SHTOOLS #356 unanswered since 2022), thread-safe high-degree synthesis, gradiometry error propagation, and a closed-loop simulator for quantum or multi-pair concepts. Who: geodesists and mission designers. Why: few alternatives exist and the community is small.
11. **Downscaling (optional, with caveats).** Demand is high in research, but the evidence of value for decisions is mixed (USGS; India specific-yield study). If offered, state the effective resolution next to the grid spacing.

### Gaps
- No user study of GRACE visualisations specifically (for example, which uncertainty displays non-experts understand) was found.
- Journalism uses beyond GFZ (NASA Earth Observatory, newspaper groundwater investigations) and educator tools (Science On a Sphere, classroom materials) could not be searched after the budget ran out.
- The Global Water Monitor annual report, a likely media-facing GRACE-based product, could not be verified.
